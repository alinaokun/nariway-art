Stephen L. Ferszt and Aime E. Salazar (Olshan Frome Wolosky LLP tax attorneys), "Federal Estate and Income Tax Issues Affecting Artwork Located In the United States at Death" (Trusts & Estates / WealthManagement.com, published Aug 21, 2026). A practitioner-facing walkthrough of the tax mechanics triggered the moment a US-situated collection changes hands at death: the collectibles capital-gains rate, the step-up in basis at death, and a genuinely specific mechanism not otherwise on file here — a nonresident, non-US person's US estate-tax exemption on situs property is only $60,000 (vs. the domestic exemption, see [[claims-register]] C23), but artwork on loan to a museum or in transit to or from an exhibition at the time of death is *not* treated as US-situs property and so falls outside that exposure. A concrete, dated legal nugget directly relevant to any foreign collector's museum-loan strategy as an estate-tax lever, and useful ballast alongside Finkelstein's case study (see [[What to Do When Collections Outlive Their Owners - Finkelstein]]) for anyone advising a family through exactly this moment.
