# Keeping Control of Your Collection: Consider a Private Museum (Erskine, Forbes)

*By **Matthew F. Erskine, JD** (trusts-and-estates attorney; see [[Matthew F. Erskine]]). Forbes, Aug 4, 2026. Saved to the Research Library 2026-08-14 at Alina's request. On thesis: how a collector keeps control while bringing a collection into public life.*

## The idea worth carrying into Nariway's research
The article's core structure is the **Private Operating Foundation (POF)**, a vehicle Nariway had not been tracking and now should.

- A **private foundation** is a 501(c)(3) usually funded by one family and often just **grantmaking** (it gives money away).
- A **Private Operating Foundation** instead **actively runs a charitable program itself**, for a collection, that means **operating the museum** (exhibiting, conserving, educating), not writing checks.
- Because it *operates*, a POF gets **tax treatment closer to a public charity** (more favorable donor deductions, no annual 5% payout requirement) **while the founding family can retain substantial control** of the board and program. That combination, control plus public benefit plus better tax treatment, is exactly the "keep control and still go public" tension collectors feel.
