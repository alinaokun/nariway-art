# Learning — professional development (guide)

A work-function (no persona) for a founder who intends to be the leading authority in this field, not merely an expert. It curates what is worth reading and following to stay at the front of everything relevant to Nariway, tracks what has been read, and surfaces what is new and genuinely worth the time. Records are the notes in this folder. Open `reading-list.base` for the sortable, filterable view.

## The standard
The bar is authority, not coverage. The goal is the canonical and the current, the things a genuine expert in how private collections enter public life would have read, followed, and formed a view on. Depth over popularity. A short list mastered beats a long list skimmed.

## How each item is tracked
`kind` (book, report, publication, newsletter, resource, thinker) · `topic` · `priority` (essential, important, worthwhile) · `status` (to-read, reading, read, following) · `link`. Mark status as you go, so the base shows what is next and what is done.

## How it grows
The definitive list is being built by research now, across books, reports, publications, thinkers, and centers. Beyond that, [[nariway-signals]] surfaces new reports, major essays, and notable new writing as they appear, and the strongest land here. New releases in the field, a new Deloitte or Art Basel report, an important book, a scholar's new work, are added and flagged.

## Scope
Collector-founded museums and private foundations, the future and legacy of private collections, art and estate and succession planning, cultural philanthropy and museum gifting, museum governance and long-term sustainability, deaccessioning, art-secured finance, and the economics of the art market as it bears on collections.

## Substack and newsletters
Whom to read, engage, and follow on Substack and adjacent platforms is kept in [[substack-register]], maintained by the Substack function.

## People to follow directly
Clare McAndrew (the field's principal market economist, author of the Art Basel and UBS reports). Olav Velthuis (art-price sociology). Martin Gammon (deaccessioning). Andrew McClellan (the museum-idea historian). James Cuno (the encyclopedic-museum argument, former Getty head, worth reading with his critics). Gail Dexter Lord and Barry Lord (museum planning). Georgina Adam (the market's money flows). Rachel Pownall (art finance, Maastricht).

## Institutions and communities
Center for Art Law. TIAMSA, the International Art Market Studies Association, the scholarly peer community worth entering. American Alliance of Museums and the Association of Art Museum Directors, whose published guidelines are primary sources on gifts and deaccessioning. University of Leicester School of Museum Studies. Sotheby's Institute of Art and Christie's Education. Deloitte Art and Finance.

## Start here (the first several)
1. The Private Collector's Museum, Georgina Walker, the subject book-length.
2. Art Basel and UBS, the annual Market Report and the Survey of Global Collecting, the data every authority knows cold.
3. Larry's List Private Art Museum Report, the only dedicated data set on private museums.
4. Art Held Hostage, John Anderson, the Barnes, the defining case of donor intent against survival.
5. Deaccessioning and Its Discontents, Martin Gammon.
6. The Deloitte and ArtTactic Art and Finance Report.
7. Subscribe now, The Art Newspaper, the Center for Art Law newsletter, and Katya Kazakina's column at Artnet.

## Verify before citing publicly
This starter list was assembled from model knowledge, not confirmed against a library catalog. The reports, the major publications, and the well-known books and authors are reliable, but a handful of exact titles, subtitles, and dates are flagged in their notes with a `verify` field. Confirm those on WorldCat or the publisher before you cite them in writing or from a stage. Being the authority means the citations are right, so this list becomes trustworthy only after that pass, which the Learning function will do as items move from to-read to read.
