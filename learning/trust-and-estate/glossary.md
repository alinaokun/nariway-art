# Trust & estate glossary

*Terms kept precise, so Alina uses them the way an attorney does. Plain definitions, grows as notes are written. Not legal advice; confirm any term's operative meaning in the relevant jurisdiction.*

- **Personal representative (PR)** — the umbrella term for the person appointed to administer an estate. An **executor** is named in a will; an **administrator** is appointed when there is no will or no named executor able to serve.
- **Probate** — the court-supervised process of proving a will and administering an estate. Handled in **Surrogate's Court** in New York and New Jersey.
- **Letters testamentary / letters of administration** — the court document that gives the PR authority to act on the estate's behalf.
- **Marshaling assets** — gathering, taking control of, and safeguarding the decedent's property, including art, as a first step of administration.
- **Fiduciary** — a person who must act for another's benefit, held to duties of loyalty, care, impartiality, and prudence. A PR and a trustee are fiduciaries.
- **Tangible personal property** — physical, movable property (art, furniture, jewelry) as distinct from real estate or financial assets. How it passes is often governed by specific wording in the will.
- **Specific bequest** — a gift of a particular identified item (a named painting) to a named beneficiary. It generally cannot be sold out from under that beneficiary.
- **Ademption** — when a specifically bequeathed item is no longer in the estate at death, so the gift fails.
- **Residuary estate** — what remains after specific gifts, debts, and expenses; it passes to the residuary beneficiaries.
- **Self-dealing** — a fiduciary transacting with the estate for personal benefit (for example buying an estate artwork). Generally prohibited without consent or court approval.
- **Prudent investor / prudent administrator rule** — the standard requiring a fiduciary to manage assets with care, skill, and caution, judged by the process, not hindsight.
- **Fair market value (FMV)** — the price a willing buyer and willing seller would agree on, neither compelled, both informed. The tax standard for valuing estate art.
- **Qualified appraisal / qualified appraiser** — an appraisal and appraiser meeting specific IRS requirements, needed to substantiate art values for tax purposes.
- **Blockage discount** — a valuation reduction argued when selling a large quantity of one artist's work at once would depress the price.
- **Cy pres** — a court doctrine that reshapes a charitable gift's terms when the original purpose becomes impossible or impractical (relevant to donor-restricted collections).
