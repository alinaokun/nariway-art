# Market learning — the companion evidence engine

Nariway already has a **collection-evidence engine** (what happened in real collection transitions — see [[estate-transition-synthesis]]). This is the small companion: a **market-evidence engine** — how attorneys, fiduciaries, collectors and families actually experience the problem, and what they would pay someone to do about it.

Its single job is to test the one hypothesis desk research cannot: **H7B (willingness to pay)**, which [[research-program]] marks *Untested — only provable through market contact*. Every substantive market conversation should make Nariway smarter about H7B and leave a trace here.

**This is not another CRM or dashboard.** It is a capture habit plus one weekly read. Keep it lightweight; the point is to make the market work more effective, not to build another system to maintain. Movement and people live in [[market-entry-sept-2026]]; rooms in [[events-radar]]; the fuller roster in [[warm-network]].

## Part A — Market Evidence Register
**You never fill out fields.** After a conversation, give Claude informal notes — a few sentences is enough (*"Spoke with Jane Smith, estate attorney. Biggest issue she sees is families not knowing what they own. She brings in an appraiser first. Thought an independent coordinator could help on larger estates but wasn't sure who'd pay. Offered to introduce me to a fiduciary."*). Claude extracts the structured entry and flags anything uncertain. You have the conversation; Claude turns it into learning. **Unknown is a fine value.**

Three disciplines Claude applies:
- **Separate Observed from Inferred.** Observed = what they actually said/did. Inferred = the read. A read never hardens into a fact.
- **Do not infer warmth or intent.** A polite "interesting" is not a buying signal; being researched, connected, or invited is not a relationship (same standard as [[market-entry-sept-2026]]).
- **Grade the evidence level**, because these are very different signals and early founders wrongly bank the weak ones as validation:
  1. "Interesting idea" (weakest — basically noise)
  2. "I see this problem"
  3. "I'd refer someone for this"
  4. "I have a matter like this now"
  5. "I'd pay for this"
  6. "Here's what I'd pay for" (strongest — real H7B evidence)

**Entry template (Claude fills this from your notes — for reference, not homework):**
```
### YYYY-MM-DD — [Name], [role] ([firm])
- Where/how: (event, intro, call, DM)
- Trigger situation discussed (observed):
- Problem described (observed):
- Current workaround / who owns it today (observed):
- Where coordination broke down (observed):
- Language they actually used (quote if possible):
- Reaction to Nariway's role (observed):
- Potential scope they'd expect (observed vs inferred):
- Who would hire / pay (observed vs inferred):
- Objections or concerns (observed):
- Any budget / willingness signal (observed — rare; do not invent):
- My inference (kept separate):
- Next step:
```

## Part B — Weekly Operator Brief (Friday)
Replaces the old standalone weekly scorecard. Each Friday, ask Claude to *"run the operator brief"* — **on-demand, not automated**, for the next four weeks (through early October 2026). Claude reads the [[market-entry-sept-2026]] movement log + working list and this register and produces the one page. You keep logging; Claude assembles.

**Evaluate after four runs.** Keep/automate the brief only if it passes one test: *did it cause me to do something the next week I otherwise might not have done?* — surfaced a forgotten follow-up, told me which relationship is moving, caught a recurring market need, or stopped me wasting time. If it mostly restates what I already know, change it or drop it. Automation follows proven usefulness, never precedes it.

**The one page:**
1. **What moved outward** this week.
2. **Strongest conversations / who moved closer** (relationship labels, honestly applied).
3. **Follow-ups due** (where momentum is at risk).
4. **Introductions** created or requested.
5. **Real collection situations surfaced** — the leading indicator.
6. **What the market taught us** — new evidence toward H7B (pointer to the register entries).
7. **What changed in our assumptions** — where the week revised a prior belief.
8. **What appears to be wasting my time** — name it plainly.
9. **The three highest-value external actions for next week.**

**Discipline:** the brief makes Alina *act*; it does not generate documents to read. **Do not recommend building new products, agents, reports, or infrastructure unless market evidence in the register shows a clear, repeated need.** When the two engines (collection evidence + market evidence) start pointing at the same repeatable, paid-for job, that is the signal — not before.
