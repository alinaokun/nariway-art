## Call questions (research call, aimed at the H7A test)
Method per [[problem-discovery]]: researcher not seller, "tell me about the last one," follow the chronology, hunt disconfirmation. Do NOT pitch; watch for the pull signal ("I have a family you should talk to").

**Open (framing).** "I research what happens when serious collectors reach the point of deciding the long-term future of a collection, before they have settled on donating, selling, keeping it in the family, or something else. Over thirty years you have been in the middle of that, and I would love to understand how it actually unfolds."

**The last one (chronology).**
1. Tell me about the last collector or family you worked with who had to decide what would ultimately happen to a collection. Walk me through it from the start, what triggered it, who they called first, who ended up involved.

**The H7A core (who owns the decision).**
2. In that situation, who was actually responsible for the decision itself, not the appraisal or the trust document, but the choice of what the collection should become? You, the family, the attorney, or did it fall between people?
3. When a family is weighing keep versus divide versus donate versus sell, before any of it is settled, who holds that whole picture? Is that a role you play, or something that happens informally?
4. Where does it break down? Which part of that decision tends to have no clear owner?
5. Was there a moment when an independent person whose only job was the decision itself would have helped, or is that already covered by you or the family office?

**The boundary / incumbent test.**
6. How would you describe the boundary of your role? What do you own end to end, and where do you hand off to the attorney, the CPA, or the museum?
7. When you coordinate the attorney, CPA, appraiser and museum on a succession, who is quarterbacking that, and how does it usually get set up?

**The naming / field questions.**
8. When you think about a collection over its entire life, is there anyone whose actual job is to think about the collection as a whole, or is it assembled case by case each time?
9. What would you call this problem, the thing families reach out to you about when they "don't know the right questions to ask"?

**Demand-side (for the collector, if they join).**
10. What did your collector most struggle to figure out about their collection's future, and what did they wish someone had helped them with earlier?
11. What did they get wrong, or nearly get wrong, that better guidance would have prevented?

**Close / network.**
12. Who sees a completely different part of this than you do, that I should also understand?

**Listen hardest for:** whether she treats the whole-collection decision as a role someone owns or as something improvised case by case (the H7A answer), and any friction she names that no one clearly owns.
