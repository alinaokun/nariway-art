## Who / why
**David Odo** — Director, **Honolulu Museum of Art (HoMA)**, appointed 2026, arriving as HoMA prepares to mark its centennial in 2027. A specialist in nineteenth-century photography of Japan, he joins from the Georgia Museum of Art at the University of Georgia, where he was director and chief curator and grew annual attendance to a record 61,000 while raising overall revenue 141 percent; earlier roles include the Harvard Art Museums, the Yale University Art Gallery, the Rijksmuseum, the Smithsonian Institution and Harvard's Peabody Museum. The appointment is a homecoming — Odo was born and raised in Hawai'i, the son of the late Franklin Odo (the University of Hawaii's first permanent ethnic-studies director), and visited HoMA as a child, drawn especially to its Buddhist sculpture collection. HoMA itself began life as the Honolulu Academy of Arts, founded in 1922 on Anna Rice Cooke's personal art collection and her own Beretania Street mansion — by her death in 1934 she had given the museum over four thousand works — making it a textbook example of a private collection becoming a public institution. **Public-source lead — no contact yet.**
