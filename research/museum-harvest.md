# Museum harvest — a museum visit into database signal, not noise

The standard procedure when Alina visits a museum. **Trigger:** Alina says *"run the harvest for [museum]."* Goal: find genuine collection-level transitions worth the [[candidate-universe|Collections database]], not a directory of every donor on a wall label. Alina notices and judges in the room; the scan happens afterward, by Claude.

## Method
Scan the museum's OWN official pages first (collection, collection-area, exhibition, and press-release pages), preferred over secondary press, for named private collections, collectors, estates, foundations, major gifts, bequests, and long-term loans. Sort every name into one of five buckets:
1. **Major collection gift or bequest** — a named private collection given or bequeathed to the museum.
2. **Private collector lending works** — lent one or a few works (often to an exhibition); not a transition.
3. **Artist estate or foundation** — distributing an artist's works (a separate transition type).
4. **Historic donor** — one or a few gifts; usually not a collection-level story.
5. **Acquisition-fund or sponsorship name** — not a collection.

## Do not auto-add anything to Collections
Produce a **shortlist** of only the cases that appear to have a substantive collection-level transition story. For each, show:
- collector or collection name
- museum
- type of relationship (which bucket)
- what appears to have happened to the collection
- whether enough public information exists for a meaningful [[case-template|Collections]] entry
- whether it is already in the Nariway corpus
- **recommended action: research / watch / skip**

The test is the same one that keeps the database clean (see [[candidate-universe]]): is there a *collection-level story* (a real collection, and something that happened to it), or just a name on a wall? If not, do not force an entry. Anything that clears the bar still passes the standard research and QA-verification gate before it publishes.

*(Buckets 2 and 4 are often worth logging as CRM prospects even when they are not Collections cases — a lender or donor can still be a collector worth knowing.)*
