## Sourcing rule (standing)
**Every retrieved item carries a clickable source link** — a markdown `[title](url)`. Research agents are instructed to always return real URLs; registers store them (a `source:` property + a `[Source]` link in each note, or a linked cell in a table). No bare source *names* — Alina should never have to Google to reach the original.

## Cadence & escalation
- **Silent collection** — nothing reaches you because it was found.
- **Weekly digest ("Nariway Signals")** — ~5–10 genuinely relevant items across the four beats, each with one line on why it matters and where it was filed. *Radar is continuous; thinking is weekly.*
- **Mid-week escalation only for time-sensitive items** — a conference/submission deadline closing, a prospect making a major public legacy announcement, a highly relevant opening you'd need to book travel for.

## Sources & format (elevated 2026-08-19, Alina: the brief had gone too short and thin)
Signals is a **daily morning READ**, not a narrow new-cases radar. Target **8 to 12 genuinely interesting items** every morning, structured editorially: a one-line orientation, a LEAD item with real framing, then grouped sections ("What's happening", "Worth reading", "To see", "For the record"), each a bolded headline + why-it-matters + a clickable link. Idea-teaser subject line. No emojis, no "nothing new" padding, no audit of sources checked.
- **"Worth reading" is now a required, first-class section** (it had been missing). Every run searches the on-thesis writers in [[substack-register]] (Read + Engage tiers) plus Katya Kazakina, Georgina Adam, Josh Baer, Marion Maneker, and the Center for Art Law for their **latest posts**, and surfaces 2 to 4 genuinely worth-reading pieces with links. These people publish constantly, so this section should rarely be empty.
- **LinkedIn / public voices** get their own sweep: recent public posts/essays by prominent collectors, advisors, family-office/estate professionals, and museum/foundation leaders (many named in [[warm-network]], partners, prospects). Login-gated feeds may not fully load, so surface what search makes visible; deeper LinkedIn-public work stays with the **network-research** routine. Named accounts worth a standing watch get added to [[substack-register]] as Alina identifies them.
- **Why it had shrunk:** the "new institution announced today" beat is naturally thin once seeding ended, and removing the old audit section (2026-08-17) left a gap. The fix is to broaden into the field's discourse (what is being *published and discussed*), not just hard institutional news, and to search harder (WebFetch is blocked, so lean on WebSearch by name).

## How it runs
- **First cycle: now (manual).** It seeds all four registers AND defines the filter — what counts as *signal* vs. noise — before any automation. (This is the cycle currently running.)
- **Then weekly.** Once the filter proves out, this can be a **scheduled task** that scans, deposits, and drafts the digest automatically — or a ritual Toi runs each check-in. *Founder's call after seeing the first digest* (see the open question on HOME).

**Cases (+3 → filed to [[candidate-universe]])**

- **Ty Warner v. Santa Barbara Museum of Natural History.** In an Aug 10 Ninth Circuit brief, Warner argues the museum stripped his name from the Sea Center in 2014 over his tax conviction, then in May 2025 solicited $100,000 a year for life plus a **$50M planned estate gift** — offering donor recognition in return. His lawyers: at the right price, the reputational objection could be ignored. The 2003 agreement had promised naming for "no less than 25 years." This is the donor-intent case in its modern form, and it is live.
