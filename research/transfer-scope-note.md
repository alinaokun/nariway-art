# Nariway Scope Note — The "$992 Billion" Figure (v0.1, 2026-08)

*Output of the landscape sprint's Transfer pass. Purpose: stress-test the headline before Nariway ever repeats it. Feeds the Transfer inquiry area in [research-program.md](research-program.md). DOCUMENTED = in a primary source; INFERRED = flagged reasoning.*

## The number, cracked open
**DOCUMENTED — Deloitte Private & ArtTactic *Art & Finance Report 2025*.** $992B = **5% × $19.84T**, where $19.84T is the share of a projected $31T decade-long wealth transfer attributable to the **$30M+ (UHNW) cohort** (Altrata *Family Wealth Transfer 2024*). So:
- Scope is **"art AND collectibles"** (adds watches, jewellery, cars, wine), **global**, **~10 years forward**, ~$100B/yr.
- The whole headline hinges on **one unsourced lever: "5% of UHNW wealth is art/collectibles."** At 3% → ~$595B; at 8% → ~$1.59T.
- **No fine-art-only breakdown exists.** It is a bundle.
- **"Change hands" = ownership transfer (mostly inheritance), NOT $992B of art coming to market.** Global art market sales are only ~$59.6B/yr in total, of which auction is ~$24.8B (see [[claims-register]] C6).

## Stock figure (confirmed)
UHNWI art-and-collectibles wealth: **$2.174T (2022) → $2.564T (2024) → projected ~$3.473T by 2030** (2025 report); the 2023 report's $2.174T→$2.861T-by-2026 vintage also checks out. It's a model (population × wealth × art-share), not an audit.

## The outcome distribution — the uncomfortable part
**DOCUMENTED — Art Basel & UBS *Survey of Global Collecting* (Arts Economics):**
- 2024: **91% had inherited/gifted works; 72% KEPT at least some.** Sellers sold for practical reasons — **55% storage, 47% estate tax** — not taste.
- 2025: **80% plan to pass to children/spouse; sell intentions eased to ~25%.** ~90% of Gen Z heirs kept inherited works.
- **Reconciling the donation numbers (important — don't repeat my earlier error):** Deloitte's "6% donate to public museums" is a *near-term, single-primary-intent* reading; Art Basel/UBS asked differently and found **~49% have made plans to donate works to museums** and **~70% hope to donate some works eventually.** Not contradictory — donation intentions are *widespread but soft, long-horizon, and partial* (some works, not the whole collection). **Do NOT cite "6%" as proof museum donation is rare.**
- **Outcomes are NOT exclusive — model as a portfolio.** A collection typically branches: retain *and* donate some *and* sell some *and* lend *and* perhaps institutionalize a core, all at once. What is genuinely rare is **founding a new institution.**
- **Keep two findings separate — do not fuse them.** The survey establishes **(a) retention is common** and, separately, **(b) planning/preparedness gaps are widespread.** It does *not* establish that the *same* retained collections are the unmanaged ones. "Retained **and** unmanaged" is a **hypothesis to investigate**, not a finding. The only fully defensible narrow claim is: *new public institutions are a small tail of outcomes.*

## The pain that IS documented (supports H7)
- **Estate planning is now the #1 family-office priority: 67%** (up from 41% in 2023). Wealth managers putting art in estate plans: **81% in 2025** (doubled since 2017).
- **Preparedness gap:** 61% of collectors had **not** discussed the collection with heirs; <40% of heirs knew an inheritance was coming; only 14% (of those without a wealth manager) had a formal, communicated plan.
- Collectors ask for **valuation, succession, protection — not investment** (only 7% of family offices focus on art *as investment*).

## Headline Nariway can defend
> "Deloitte estimates ~$992B of art **and collectibles** could **change ownership** (largely by inheritance) globally over ~a decade — ~$100B/yr — by assuming **5% of the $19.8T that $30M+ individuals are projected to transfer** is art. It is a model, not a measured flow, and does **not** mean $992B of art hitting the market; most inherited art is retained, not sold."

**Do NOT say:** "$992B of art will be sold," "$992B of fine art," any regional split, or that succession problems scale cleanly by value band — none are supported.

## Value-band mapping — INFERRED (Nariway's construct, not sourced)
No source segments the succession problem by $2M/$10M/$50M/$100M. What's documented: the $30M+ cohort holds 64% of transfer value (top-heavy), and the preparedness failure is broad (across the whole base, not by band). Treat any band framing as professional judgment, labelled as such.
