# Nariway Institutional Case Study Protocol — v0.1

*One methodology within the [Nariway Research Program](research-program.md) — it governs how a **single** collection/institution case is studied. Every Artobiography case study is produced through this protocol, and each one simultaneously serves three readers: the public (a story), the founder (learning), and the future advisory practice (pattern + network). Its **primary** job is the founder's learning.*

---

## Design principles (these govern everything below)

1. **Learning at scale, not publishing at scale.** The asset is pattern recognition, not volume. Depth over throughput. Never optimize for more articles or more fields.
2. **The founder stays inside the loop.** Agents remove mechanical friction; they do not read, synthesize, interview, or write *for* her. Agents produce **research packets, never finished articles.**
3. **The unit of analysis is the collection and its *pathway*** — not the museum. The museum (or foundation, or gift, or auction) is one *state* in a sequence, not the subject.
4. **"Unknown" is a finding.** Every quantitative claim carries a source and a confidence flag. Gaps are recorded, not hidden.
5. **The architecture is emergent.** We do not design an agent org chart. After ~3 cases, the repeated tasks in §4 become the first few agents.

---

## 1. The unit of analysis: the collection and its branching pathway

Each case tracks one collection from private ownership toward (or away from) public life. Critically, the model is **branching, not linear**: at a transition, a collection rarely picks one exclusive outcome — it usually splits into a **portfolio of simultaneous dispositions**, each with its own form and its own outcome over time.

`Private collection` → `Transition event(s)` → **portfolio of dispositions** → `institutional form(s)` → `outcome(s) over time`

Each **disposition** is one of: retain (family) · sell (auction / private) · donate to existing museum (intact / partial-scattered) · long-term / permanent loan · found a new institution (museum / foundation / house museum / art park / exhibition space) · university partnership · traveling program · intentional dispersal across multiple institutions · hybrid.

Example — one 500-work collection: 200 retained by children · 100 sold · 75 donated across three museums · 100 into a family foundation · 25 in a public exhibition space. **Store the branches and their proportions, not a single "chosen form."**

A branch is *complete* when its outcome is observable; living-founder branches have open outcome cells — note them. **The advisory insight hiding in this model:** the client's real question may be less "what should I do with my collection?" than **"what should happen to each part, and how do the decisions fit together?"**

## 2. The core question every case must earn its keep by illuminating

Not "tell the story of X." The master question and the full hypothesis set (H1–H7B, incl. the split H7A/H7B) live in the [Research Program](research-program.md); each case is built to move one of *those* from opinion toward evidence. This protocol contributes evidence up the funnel — especially to the **later-funnel durability refinement** of H3/H6, once a case has actually reached institutional form:

- Does a founding endowment above some multiple of annual operating expense predict surviving the founder?
- Does governance design (independent board vs. family control) predict post-founder survival better than collection value?
- Below a certain value/coherence, does a *partnership* (university, existing museum) outlast a *standalone* museum?
- Do tightly focused collections institutionalize more durably than broad ones?
- Does community fit / local audience matter more to survival than art-historical significance?

But a case is *not* limited to museums that survived — it is chosen because a Research Program inquiry area (Transfer / Models / People / Potential) says it matters. A failed plan, an Onera-like foundation, or a scattered collection is as valuable a case as a durable museum.

## 3. The variable set (lean v0.1 — deliberately small)

Grouped in blocks. Keep it to what you would actually *reason* with. Resist expansion.

- **A · Identity & pathway spine** — collection name; collector(s); current form; pathway sequence (dated); current status.
- **B · The collector** — who, wealth source, why they collected, stated legacy intent.
- **C · The collection** — focus/coherence; approx. size; approx. value (where known); concentration; whether ownership was transferred. **Cultural opportunity (scored separately from viability):** what can the public see here that they can't easily see elsewhere — a region, an overlooked artist group, a category, a historical story, a community, a medium, a tradition, a point of view? *The strongest reason to exist is not the same as the biggest budget.*
- **D · The transition** — trigger; decision date; alternatives considered; who advised; why this form.
- **E · Institutional design** — legal structure; opening date; building (new / adapted / existing); size; admission model; staffing at open.
- **F · Money** — founding endowment (where known); endowment-to-opex ratio; founder's share of operating budget; earned vs. contributed revenue; capital source.
- **G · Governance & succession** — initial board; family vs. independent control; what changed at/after founder transition. **Founder constraints & retained control:** restrictions the founder imposed on *display, sale, lending, acquisition, deaccessioning, governance, location, family control* — *and how each changed over time.* The organizing question: **what did the founder have to give up for the collection to survive?** Legacy and control may conflict — the institutions that last may be those whose founders built in enough flexibility for future stewards. (See H8; this field may prove as predictive as endowment size.)
- **H · Outcome & durability** — survived founder? current status; distress signals; deaccessioning events; closure/merger; recorded cause.
- **I · Advisors & specialists used** — museum planner, curator, nonprofit attorney, architect, appraiser, registrar, conservator, etc. *(This block doubles as the network map.)*
- **J · Evidence & confidence** — source per claim; confidence level; open questions; what only an interview can resolve.
- **K · Friction** — for each transition, *what was hard, and why?* (funding gap · family disagreement · museum rejection · zoning · community opposition · tax problem · governance conflict · collection incoherence · storage · conservation · staffing · founder control · poor attendance · succession failure · advisor fragmentation · construction overrun · legal challenge · institution unwilling to accept gift). **Friction is where the business comes from** — the eventual service will emerge from what founders repeatedly *couldn't get done*, not from what institutions *are*.
- **L · Decision owner & coordinator** — *who actually sat beside the collector when the fundamental decision was made?* (collector alone · spouse · children · estate attorney · art advisor · museum director · family office · architect · philanthropic advisor · foundation executive · consultant · auction house · **no identifiable person**) — and *separately*, who coordinated the project afterward. Different roles. Across ~30 cases this is the empirical test of **H7A** — far stronger than inferring the gap from competitors' websites.

## 4. Division of labor (the AI principle, operationalized)

| Agent labor (removes friction) | Founder judgment (never delegated) |
|---|---|
| Locate & pull primary docs (see §5) | Read the packet and the primary sources herself |
| Extract structured data from Form 990/990-PF into the schema | Decide what the case *means* — which hypotheses it moves |
| Build the dated timeline; assemble the advisor list | Conduct interviews (relationships, not extraction) |
| Cross-check claims across sources; flag contradictions | Write the Artobiography piece |
| Assemble a **research packet**: sourced facts, gaps, open questions | Update the running Hypotheses & Archetypes doc (§8) |
| Maintain database, citations, follow-up queue; transcribe interviews | Choose the next case to test the weakest hypothesis |

**Hard rule:** an agent's output is a *research packet*, never a publishable article.

## 5. Source map (priority order)

1. **IRS Form 990 / 990-PF** (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, GuideStar) — the financial spine; for foundations the single most valuable source.
2. **State incorporation / nonprofit filings** — legal structure, founding intent.
3. **Founding-era press & obituaries** — timeline, founder intent, decision moment.
4. **Oral histories & interviews** — Smithsonian Archives of American Art oral histories are excellent; founder/director interviews.
5. **Museum's own history pages & annual reports** — *claims, not facts; flag accordingly.*
6. **Larry's List & academic private-museum studies** — comparative baseline.
7. **Museum-consultant project write-ups** (Lord, AEA, Schwartzman&, etc.) — who did the work.
8. **Books / monographs** on major cases.

Evidence hierarchy: 990s and legal filings are hard evidence; press and self-description are claims. Tag each accordingly in block J.

## 6. Interview guide skeleton (what only a conversation reveals)

Aim: the *undocumented* — the decision moment, the alternatives rejected, the regrets, the money reality behind the press, the post-founder governance friction. Also: **every interview is a future referral node** — treat it as a relationship.

1. When did you first realize the collection couldn't just stay private?
2. What options did you seriously consider — and reject? Why?
3. Who did you turn to for help, and what was missing?
4. What did it actually cost to open — and to keep open?
5. What do you wish one single person had handled for you?
6. What changed after the founder stepped back / died?
7. What would you do differently?
8. What does "legacy" actually mean to you now that it exists?

---

## 7. Editorial bar — a piece ships only if…

- It works as a **human story** for a reader who will never build a museum.
- It carries a **real question or tension** — the institution treated as an experiment with a result, not a monument to be admired.
- It teaches something **non-obvious about the transition.**
- Underneath the narrative sits a **complete case record** (the database row + packet), whether or not all of it is published.

*(Prose voice, length, and form are governed separately by the Nariway/Artobiography editorial voice — not by this document. This protocol governs research, not writing.)*

---

## 8. Learning capture (the flywheel's memory)

After each case, update a living **`hypotheses-and-archetypes.md`**:
- Current candidate **pathways** (are archetypes emerging?).
- Each **durability hypothesis** with its supporting and contradicting cases, and a confidence level.
- The **next case**, chosen deliberately to test the weakest-supported hypothesis.

This is what turns three case studies into pattern recognition, and eventually into advisory IP: *"We've studied N collector-founded institutions. Here are the recurring pathways, what each requires, and which reliably survive the founder."*

---

## 9. How the agent architecture emerges (deferred, on purpose)

We do **not** draw an org chart now. Run this protocol by hand-with-Claude on ~3 cases. The repeated §4 tasks will name the first agents themselves — likely a *990/financial extractor*, a *source-packet builder*, a *timeline & advisor mapper*, and a *database steward*. Smallest useful architecture, discovered from real work.
