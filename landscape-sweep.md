## A. Taxonomy of pathways / models

Most real cases are hybrids; the value is in naming pure types so mixtures become legible.

1. **Standalone collector museum** — Southern Nevada Art Museum (Las Vegas, 2026); Magazzino Italian Art (Cold Spring NY, 2017).
2. **Private operating foundation** (legal/tax backbone under many below) — Barnes Foundation (1922); Hall Art Foundation (2007).
3. **Small exhibition foundation** — Onera Foundation (New Canaan); Julia Stoschek Foundation (time-based media).
4. **House museum** — Peter Marino Art Foundation (Southampton, converted landmark library); Isabella Stewart Gardner (precedent).
5. **Art park / open-air campus** — Chinati Foundation (Marfa, Judd, 1986); Longleaf Art Park (Walton County FL, Berkowitz).
6. **University / institution partnership** — Anderson Collection at Stanford (2014); Ogden Museum of Southern Art / Univ. of New Orleans (2003).
7. **Permanent / long-term loan** — Doris & Donald Fisher Collection → SFMOMA (100-year loan, 720+ works).
8. **Seasonal / appointment-to-public venue** — Hall Art Foundation (VT, May–Nov weekends); Peter Marino (summer season).
9. **Gift to existing museum — intact vs. scattered** — Pizzuti → Columbus Museum of Art (intact, named branch, 2019); Souls Grown Deep (deliberately distributed across 12+ museums).
10. **Traveling private collection** (no fixed home) — Kinsey African American Art & History Collection (39 venues since 2006).
11. **Abandoned / changed museum plan** — Berkowitz (Miami museum cancelled → Longleaf Art Park); Fisher (Presidio museum blocked → SFMOMA loan); Barnes (relocated to Philadelphia against founder's indenture by court order).

Scale context: Larry's List counts **446** privately founded contemporary art museums worldwide; 82% founded since 2000; Germany (60), US (59), South Korea (50) lead. The Art Newspaper: many close within a generation — **succession is the field's structural weak point.**
