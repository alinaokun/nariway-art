# The Wolfsonian–FIU

One-line: Mitchell "Micky" Wolfson Jr. spent decades assembling a 200,000-object design-and-propaganda collection meant to show how objects persuade, then in 1997 gave the whole collection plus three Miami Beach buildings to the State of Florida — the single largest gift to the state at the time — making it a Florida International University museum while he was still living and remains an active donor.

**Why in the sample:** a university-partnership pathway with an unusually large, thematically tight collection (design as persuasion, 1850–1950) gifted whole to a *public* university rather than a private one, and a living founder who transferred control decades before any succession question arose — a clean early test of H6/H8's "founder gives up control voluntarily, while living" mechanism, distinct from [[anderson-collection-stanford]] and [[hedreen-seattle-university]]'s deceased/deferred-gift versions of the same pathway.

## Narrative (sourced)

Mitchell "Micky" Wolfson Jr., heir to a Florida broadcasting and theater fortune, spent decades collecting objects — furniture, propaganda posters, industrial design, decorative arts — from 1850 to 1950, drawn to what they revealed about how design and mass communication persuade [secondary: [Miami-Dade Cultural Affairs](https://www.miamidadearts.org/mitchell-%E2%80%9Cmicky%E2%80%9D-wolfson-jr)]. He opened a private research library in a 1927 Miami Beach building in 1986, enlarged it into a full 56,000 sq ft museum by 1992, and opened The Wolfsonian to the public in 1995 [secondary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfsonian%E2%80%93FIU)]. In 1997, while still very much alive, Wolfson donated the entire collection and three historic buildings to the State of Florida, making the museum a unit of Florida International University — at the time, reportedly the single largest gift to the state — and the collection has since grown further, including an additional ~25,000-object gift from Wolfson himself [secondary: [FIU News, 2019](https://news.fiu.edu/2019/the-wolfsonian-fiu-celebrates-micky-wolfsons-legacy-with-one-of-a-kind-collection)]. This makes Wolfsonian–FIU an unusually clean test of the university-partnership pathway's founder-control mechanism: unlike [[anderson-collection-stanford]] (gift completed near the founders' deaths) or [[hedreen-seattle-university]] (a promised gift explicitly deferred until a building is finished), Wolfson handed control to a public institution decades before any succession pressure existed, and has remained an active, living donor to the collection ever since. Because FIU is a public university, the museum has no standalone EIN or Form 990 of its own — a structural rather than a merely-unpulled disclosure gap, distinct from every private-foundation case in this dataset — so its true operating economics sit inside FIU's own budget and were not separable this run.
