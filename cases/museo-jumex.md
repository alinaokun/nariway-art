## Narrative
Museo Jumex sharpens the sample's international coverage in a specific way: it is a **standalone, purpose-built museum funded entirely by one family's industrial wealth**, with the founder still alive and still personally presiding, and with essentially no public financial disclosure of any kind located this run — a genuine test case for how thin the evidence base can get outside jurisdictions with a 990-equivalent regime. The 2001–2013 arc (an appointment-access gallery inside the family's own juice factory in Ecatepec, then a Chipperfield-designed public museum in Polanco) mirrors a pattern this sample has seen from private display maturing into public institution, but compressed into a single family's control throughout, with no independent board surfaced at any stage. Because López remains both founder and president, this case currently offers no data on the question the report's central governance claim turns on (does the institution survive a founder-sole structure past the founder's death?) — it is coded `not-yet-testable`, correctly excluded from Pattern 14's governance table, and flagged here as a case to re-check in future years as López ages, alongside [[audain]], [[thoma]], and the sample's other living-founder-sole cases.
