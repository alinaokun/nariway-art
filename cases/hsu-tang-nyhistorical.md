## Narrative
Agnes Hsu-Tang, an archaeologist and art historian who was elected chair of New York Historical's board of trustees in October 2021 and began serving in that role in February 2022, and her husband Oscar Tang, a longtime philanthropist, announced in February 2026 a promised gift of 150 works by more than 100 Indigenous American artists to the institution she chairs [secondary: The Art Newspaper, "New York Historical receives gift of 150 works by Indigenous artists"]. The gift spans from early-20th-century potter Nampeyo of Hano (Tewa) through contemporary painter and sculptor Jeffrey Gibson (Choctaw/Cherokee), and was timed deliberately to the 250th anniversary of the founding of the United States [secondary: Native News Online]. The museum showcased the collection in the exhibition *House Made of Dawn: Art by Native Americans 1880 to Now, Selections from the Hsu-Tang Collection*, running April 22 through August 16, 2026, describing it as the largest gift of Native art to a New York art institution since the establishment of the Museum of the American Indian [secondary: New York Historical press release]. Coverage credits New York Historical's chief executive, Louise Mirrer, with coordinating the gift.

For this evidence base, the case is valuable less for its own institutional durability (too new to assess, and absorbed into a 220-year-old institution's own governance rather than existing as a separate entity) than as a clean data point for H2's insider-exception reading: this is a sitting board chair's gift to her own institution, the same configuration [[heckler-american-folk-art-museum]] and [[caplan-baltimore-museum-of-art]] already established, now an eighth confirming instance. Coded this run specifically to sit alongside a closer look at the two candidate *non-insider* H2 cases ([[kirk-edward-long-famsf]] and Artur Walther's gift to the Met) — see [[what-we-now-believe]] for this run's finding that both of those "arm's-length" candidates carry their own, less formal, institutional ties once checked more closely, sharpening rather than resolving the insider-exception question.
