## Narrative

Andreas Bechtler, a Swiss-born Charlotte resident (b. 1943), inherited a portion of the modern-art collection assembled by his parents Hans and Bessie Bechtler and augmented it with his own acquisitions, producing a collection of more than 1,400 works by major figures of 20th-century modernism [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechtler_Museum_of_Modern_Art]. In 2006 he decided to commit the entire collection to the City of Charlotte and to build a museum to house it [source: https://www.wfae.org/local-news/2009-12-29/the-swiss-family-behind-the-bechtler-museum-of-modern-art]. The Bechtler Museum of Modern Art — the product of a public-private partnership among Wells Fargo, the City of Charlotte, and Mecklenburg County — opened January 2, 2010 in a purpose-built Mario Botta building in the Levine Center for the Arts [source: https://www.bechtler.org/aboutus]. The operating nonprofit, the Andreas H Bechtler Arts Foundation, reported FY2024 (year ending June 30, 2024) net assets of $34.3M against $4.3M of expenses, a net-assets-to-opex ratio near 8x — but this is an especially soft upper bound, because the foundation likely carries the art collection on its balance sheet, so the figure is not a proxy for spendable endowment [source: https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/562225722]. Operations run close to breakeven (FY2024 revenue $4.24M vs expenses $4.29M) [source: https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/562225722]. Because Andreas Bechtler is living, the pivotal survive-the-founder test is not yet applicable; the case is coded stable/moderate, and the interesting structural question — whether the collection is owned by the foundation and loaned, or transferred to the City — remains only partially sourced and is central to the H8 constraint reading.
