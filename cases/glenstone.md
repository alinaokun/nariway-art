## Narrative
Mitchell P. Rales, co-founder of the industrial conglomerate Danaher, and his wife Emily Wei Rales, a former art dealer, built Glenstone on their Potomac, Maryland estate as a private operating foundation devoted to post-WWII and contemporary art shown in dialogue with landscape and architecture. It opened in 2006 with a Charles Gwathmey gallery and was transformed by the 2018 "Pavilions," a 204,000-square-foot Thomas Phifer building (reported at ~$219M) set in roughly 230–300 acres of managed landscape with major outdoor sculpture. The collection — about 1,300 works acquired as museum-quality single examples — is presented free, with visitation deliberately capped near 400 people a day (about 180,000 a year) to preserve a contemplative experience. The institution's defining feature is its capitalization: in 2021 Mitchell Rales gave roughly $1.9 billion to the foundation (disclosed in 2023), and its FY2024 Form 990-PF reports ~$2.7 billion in net assets against ~$65 million in expenses, a coverage ratio (~41.7x, upper bound) far beyond any other case in the sample. Governance is entirely family-controlled: both founders sit as uncompensated officers and Emily Wei Rales directs the program, so every durability question turns on succession, which is `not-yet-testable` while both are living and active. There is no documented funding, zoning, or governance friction — the risk profile here is founder-dependency, not money. Glenstone is the sample's clearest example of the "capital solves durability, but not succession" pattern: it is as well-endowed as some university museums, yet its long-term constraints remain deliberately `open`.
