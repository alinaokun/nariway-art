# Gochman Family Collection

One-line: Philanthropist Becky Gochman and former Manhattan gallerist Zach Feuer founded the Gochman Family Collection in 2021 to build one of the country's foremost holdings of contemporary Native and Indigenous art; in 2026, five years in, they hired a former Whitney Museum curator as director and are opening a dedicated 10,000+ sq ft public exhibition space in Katonah, NY.

**Why in the sample:** a living-founder, very-early-stage `found-standalone-museum` case — the collection moved from private residences-as-showrooms plus a no-fee institutional lending program straight to a purpose-directed public gallery within five years of founding, materially faster than this sample's typical multi-decade collector-to-museum arc. Also a clean H5 case: contemporary Native/Indigenous art is a field major encyclopedic museums have historically underrepresented, and the collection is explicitly built around that gap. A useful contrast to [[barjeel-art-foundation]] and [[thoma]] as another loans-plus-emerging-space model rather than an immediate purpose-built museum.

## Narrative
Becky Gochman, a philanthropist, and Zach Feuer, a former Manhattan gallerist, founded the Gochman Family Collection in 2021 "to expand and deepen the global understanding of contemporary Native art" and to support artists whose practices engage colonial history and identity. In five years the collection grew to more than 750 works, shown first in two residences-turned-showrooms (Manhattan and Palm Beach) and circulated to major institutions through a no-fee lending program — the collection's initial public-life model was access-by-loan, not a building. In 2026 the founders hired Laura Phipps, previously an associate curator at the Whitney Museum of American Art, as the collection's first director, timed to the opening of a dedicated 10,000+ sq ft, two-site public exhibition space in Katonah, NY (Westchester County), opening in stages beginning with a 5,000 sq ft main gallery near the Metro-North station. The space will include room for readings, performances, and artist studio space. This compresses the typical collector-to-institution timeline this sample usually sees over decades into five years, and moves the case from a pure `traveling-program` model (its original form) to a branched `found-standalone-museum` + `traveling-program` hybrid. A small private foundation (Gochman Family Foundation, EIN 86-2177481, tax-exempt since October 2021) exists but reports minimal revenue ($51K FY2024) against $2.1M total assets and $282K in grants given — a scale that suggests it functions as a grantmaking vehicle adjacent to the collection rather than the collection's own holding entity; the collection's works themselves do not appear to sit on this foundation's balance sheet, the same disclosure gap this sample has already flagged for [[kinsey]] and [[thoma]].
