One-line: German collector Julia Stoschek is closing her ten-year-old Berlin exhibition venue at the end of October 2026 — retaining her original 2007 Düsseldorf space and shifting the foundation's emphasis toward international touring presentations of her 900+-work time-based-media collection, a "strategic realignment" the foundation frames as proactive, not funding distress.

## Why in the sample

Every prior `found-foundation`/building-contraction case in this dataset (CAM Raleigh, Deutsche Bank/PalaisPopulaire, Rubin Museum) explicitly cites budget pressure, attendance decline, or real-estate cost as the trigger. Julia Stoschek Foundation is the first case coded here where a living founder, still actively collecting and by every account still well-resourced, voluntarily gives up a major public venue at the peak of its own reported visitorship (450,000 visitors over ten years) to redeploy toward a touring/international model — testing whether `primary_friction`'s vocabulary (funding-gap, poor-attendance, etc.) has room for a genuinely elective contraction, the same schema gap [[rauschenberg-foundation-hq]] first flagged. It is also the sample's clearest single-medium tight-thesis European private foundation (time-based/media art only), a useful H4 comparator to [[sammlung-boros]] (contemporary art generally) and [[fondation-beyeler]] (broad modern collection).

## Narrative (sourced)

Julia Stoschek opened her first private exhibition space for her time-based-media art collection in Düsseldorf in 2007, then a larger second venue in 2016 inside a former Czech Cultural Center in Berlin's Mitte district — a 3,000-square-meter space that hosted roughly 22 exhibitions and drew about 450,000 visitors over its decade of operation [source: ARTnews, 2026; Ocula, 2026; confidence: secondary]. In 2026 the foundation announced the Berlin venue will close at the end of October, describing the move as a "strategic realignment" toward international projects rather than a response to financial distress; the Düsseldorf venue, open since 2007, is explicitly retained [source: ArtReview, 2026; Artforum, 2026; confidence: secondary]. Stoschek has already been testing the touring model directly — a 2026 Los Angeles exhibition, "What a Wonderful World: An Audiovisual Poem," curated by Udo Kittelmann, ran six weeks and drew more than 30,000 visitors [source: ARTnews; confidence: secondary].

The collection itself — over 900 works by 300+ artists, spanning video, film, multimedia installation, net/computer-based art, and performance from the 1960s to the present — is not reported as being sold, dispersed, or placed elsewhere; only the Berlin *building* is closing. This makes the case a clean test of whether "outcome" should be coded at the level of the collection (which is not distressed, arguably strengthening its international profile) or the specific venue (which is closing), a distinction [[case-template]]'s `outcome_category` vocabulary does not currently separate.
