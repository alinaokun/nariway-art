# Pintô Art Museum

One-line: Dr. Joven Cuanang, a Filipino neurologist, began patronizing a community of young contemporary artists (the Salingpusa collective) near Antipolo in the late 1980s and opened his own collection to the public in 2010 as the Pintô Art Museum, a six-building complex on his own land outside Manila.

**Why in the sample:** the Philippines' first case in this dataset, a genuine Southeast Asian geographic gap distinct from every prior Southeast Asian case ([[uob-art-collection]]/Singapore corporate, [[museum-macan]]/Indonesia, [[khao-yai-art-forest]]/Thailand); and a founder-occupation this sample has not coded before — a physician-patron rather than a business, finance, or industrial figure, funding the museum from medical-practice income rather than a large corporate or family fortune. A clean H5 case: the founder's explicit thesis was giving unrepresented young Filipino artists exposure they could not get through mainstream commercial galleries at the time.

## Narrative
Dr. Joven Cuanang, a Filipino neurologist, began collecting and personally patronizing a group of young artists, the Salingpusa collective, in the late 1980s, in the optimistic cultural moment following the 1986 People Power Revolution, when numerous grassroots artistic initiatives sprang up outside Manila. An ecology foundation established in Antipolo to protect local waterfalls became an informal gathering point for the artists' weekend painting sessions, and Cuanang's patronage grew alongside it. In 2010 he opened his personal collection to the public as the Pintô Art Museum, built across six buildings (designed by Salingpusa member and architect Tony Leaño in an organic, eclectic style, deliberately without air conditioning, relying on open-air ventilation) on roughly 12,000 square meters of his own land in the Grand Heights subdivision of Antipolo, Rizal. The museum's name, "pintô," means "door" in Filipino, reflecting Cuanang's stated framing of the museum as a convergence of his two vocations, art and medicine, and both practices' capacity to heal. No independent board, foundation entity, or financial disclosure vehicle was located this run — the museum appears to operate as a founder-owned, founder-sole enterprise, a governance shape distinct from nearly every other `found-standalone-museum` case in this dataset, most of which carry at least a nominal foundation wrapper even under otherwise sole founder control.
