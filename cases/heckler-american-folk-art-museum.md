## Narrative
Audrey B. Heckler began collecting self-taught and Art Brut art in 1993, after spotting a poster for the Outsider Art Fair — an entry point into the field she would go on to become one of its most significant private collectors in, assembling more than 700 works at the collection's height by figures including James Castle, Aloïse Corbaz, William Edmondson, Madge Gill, Morris Hirshfield, Martín Ramírez, Adolf Wölfli, Thornton Dial, and Sister Gertrude Morgan [secondary: [Artnet News](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/audrey-b-heckler-estate-american-folk-art-museum-2615369)]. She joined the American Folk Art Museum's board as a trustee in 2003 and served for more than two decades.

In September 2020 Heckler promised nearly all of her holdings, roughly 500 works, to AFAM [secondary: The Art Newspaper]. She died in 2024; the museum subsequently renamed one of its flagship galleries and its Visionary Award in her honor. In 2025 her estate transferred a further group of "dozens of masterpieces," bringing the cumulative total delivered to the museum to roughly 150 works to date, with the balance of the 2020 promise apparently still to come [secondary: Artnet News, American Folk Art Museum]. A dedicated exhibition of the new gifts is planned for spring 2026.

**Relevance to the evidence base.** Heckler is close to the cleanest possible confirming case for the 2026-08-21 H2 finding that museums readily, enthusiastically accept an intact collection specifically from donors who are already structurally embedded, here, a 20-plus-year sitting trustee giving to her own institution, with a named gallery and a renamed award as the relationship's visible sweeteners.
