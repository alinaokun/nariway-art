## Narrative
Florence and Herbert Irving were major, long-tenured Met patrons well before their headline gift: Florence was elected a trustee in 1990 (trustee emerita from 1996), the museum opened two Asian Wing galleries in the Irvings' names in 1994 and 1997, and the couple endowed a curatorship in Asian art in 2011 [secondary: Met press room]. In 2015, the centennial year of the Met's Department of Asian Art, they gave **1,275 objects** of Chinese art, jades, Buddhist sculpture, porcelain, and scholar's-studio material, into the museum's permanent collection [secondary: [Met press room, "Met Museum Receives $80 Million Gift from the Irving Family"](https://www.metmuseum.org/press/news/2017/irving-gift)]. Unlike this dataset's other insider-trustee gifts (Heckler, Caplan, Hsu-Tang), the notable feature here is not the museum's willingness to accept the gift, that door was never in question given decades of prior trusteeship and naming, but a specific term built into the gift itself: the Irvings agreed that "some items could be sold to raise funds for future acquisitions," according to a Met spokesperson [secondary: [Artsy](https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-metropolitan-museum-will-sell-300-works-chinese-art-sothebys)].

The Met has exercised that authorization repeatedly, not as a one-time event. A 2019 trade-press piece tracked cumulative sales from the gift already reaching $8.27 million and rising, under the pointed headline "The Metropolitan Museum's Disposable Irving Gift of Chinese Art" [secondary: [CultureGrrl](https://www.artsjournal.com/culturegrrl/2019/09/8-27-million-counting-metropolitan-museums-disposable-irving-gift-of-chinese-art.html)]. The largest single tranche to date is a 2026 Asia Week partnership with Sotheby's, offering more than 300 works, including a dedicated single-owner sale of 120-plus Imperial Qianlong-period jades, brushpots, paintings, and scholar's objects [secondary: [The Value](https://en.thevalue.com/articles/the-met-collection-the-irvings-sothebys); [Artnet News](https://news.artnet.com/market/met-auction-chinese-art-irvings-1630885)]. Every proceeds dollar is directed to an Irving-named acquisitions fund inside the Department of Asian Art, not to general operations, consistent with AAMD's standing rule that deaccession proceeds may fund only future acquisitions or the direct care of the remaining collection (see [[market-intelligence]] §4.5).

**Relevance to the evidence base.** This case supplies a new H8 data point from the opposite direction of this dataset's usual founder-constraint cases. Barnes locked his indenture to prevent any change; the O'Keeffe/Fisk gift explicitly forbade sale, later overridden only by *cy près* once the recipient's own solvency made compliance impracticable (§3, §4.4). The Irvings did the reverse: they designed flexibility *into* the gift terms from the outset, explicitly anticipating and authorizing future resale, handing the ongoing disposition decision to the museum's own leadership rather than trying to freeze it. It sits closest in this dataset to the Rachofsky "whatever remains" pledge (loosest constraint chosen deliberately) but goes one step further: Rachofsky's flexibility concerns his *own* future collecting choices, while the Irvings' concerns the *museum's* future choices about a gift already completed. Whether a donor-authorized resale-for-acquisitions clause becomes a repeatable model, or stays a single, unusually well-telegraphed case, is not yet answerable from one instance.
