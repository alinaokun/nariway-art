## Narrative

The Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art Sri Lanka closes a real, previously uncoded geography gap for this dataset (its first Sri Lankan case) with a governance shape distinct from almost every other standalone-museum entry: there is no individual collector-founder at all. In 2016, a Founding Committee — spanning local artists, collectors, and art historians alongside international figures including Suhanya Raffel (executive director of M+, Hong Kong) and museum consultant Michael Snelling, chaired by businessman Ajit Gunewardene — began planning the country's first museum dedicated to modern and contemporary Sri Lankan art. A dedicated Museum Committee took over execution in 2019, and the museum opened to the public on December 12, 2019, temporarily housed on the seventeenth floor of the Colombo Innovation Tower while the search for a permanent, purpose-built home continues. The John Keells Foundation — the philanthropic arm of Sri Lankan conglomerate John Keells Holdings — provided primary sponsorship, matching the founding board's own contributions to fund a reported 20 million LKR (~$118,000) operating budget through the museum's first several months.

This is a genuinely different shape from this dataset's usual "wealthy individual builds a museum around a personal collection" pattern (McNay, Broad, Glenstone): MMCA Sri Lanka was designed from the outset as a national institution built by committee, closer in spirit to a mission-founded museum like [[mint-museum]] than to any founder-driven case, but with a distinctive corporate-foundation funding backbone (John Keells) rather than accumulated small donor bequests. It is also, at seven years old and still operating out of temporary commercial space, a live test of H5 (filling a genuine representational gap — Sri Lanka had no dedicated modern/contemporary art museum before this one) whose institutional durability has not yet been stress-tested by any funding or governance crisis; its `outcome_category: emerging` and `durability_signal: moderate` reflect a real, functioning institution still mid-course toward a permanent home rather than a settled outcome either way.
