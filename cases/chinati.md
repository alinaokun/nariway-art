# The Chinati Foundation

One-line: Donald Judd converted a decommissioned Army fort in far-West Texas into a foundation for the *permanent* installation of large-scale work (his own 100 mill-aluminum boxes + 15 concrete works, plus Flavin, Chamberlain and others); founded 1986 during his life, it has run 32 years past his 1994 death on ~340 acres.

## Narrative (sourced)

Donald Judd left the New York gallery system in the 1970s convinced that work should be installed permanently in a fixed relationship to its space, and he found his instrument in Marfa: with early backing from the Dia Art Foundation he began renovating the buildings of the decommissioned Fort D.A. Russell around 1978–79 [secondary: [chinati.org](https://chinati.org/about/mission-history/)]. He formally established The Chinati Foundation in 1986 to hold and display large-scale work — his own *100 untitled works in mill aluminum* and *15 untitled works in concrete*, Dan Flavin's fluorescent installation across six former barracks, and John Chamberlain's sculptures downtown — as permanent, unmovable ensembles rather than a rotating collection [secondary]. That permanence doctrine is the institution's entire product, which places it at the far distinctiveness end of H4. Judd was the sole decision-owner: he acquired the land, sited every work, and set the mission before his death on 12 February 1994 [secondary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinati_Foundation)]. The foundation has operated for 32 years since, through director successions, in substantially its founded form — a clear `survived_founder = yes`. Financially it is small but sturdy: FY2024 net assets of $25.29M against $3.71M of expenses give a net-assets-to-opex cushion of roughly 6.8x, an **upper bound** because those net assets include the fort's land and buildings and the true endowment (Schedule D) has not been pulled [primary, [990 via ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/742340423)]. The single most important caveat is that Chinati's numbers are institution-scale small precisely because its value sits in irreplaceable installed works that never appear at full worth on the balance sheet.
