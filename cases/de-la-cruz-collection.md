## Narrative
Rosa and Carlos de la Cruz began collecting contemporary art from their Miami home in the late 1980s, opening it to visitors by appointment, and later founded and chaired a nonprofit kunsthalle, The Moore Space (2001–2007), before building a dedicated 30,000-square-foot private museum in the Design District, which opened in 2009 and remained free to the public for fifteen years. Rosa de la Cruz died on a Sunday in February 2024 — the museum's usual closed day — and, in Carlos's own account, he simply never reopened it: he had not planned to sell the collection and had not expected her death.

What followed is among the fastest, cleanest closures in the sample: no board dispute, no funding crisis, no multi-year litigation — a single surviving spouse deciding, in the weeks after his wife's death, to close permanently and consign the collection to Christie's. The first sale alone, in May 2024, realized $34.4 million across 26 lots, on a collection Christie's had obtained over 200 works from and that had been informally estimated pre-sale at roughly $30 million total — the opening sale already exceeding the full pre-sale estimate, a gap this case does not resolve. The former building itself was sold separately, to the Institute of Contemporary Art Miami for $25 million eight months later, doubling that institution's footprint. The sale was reported as controversial within Miami's art world, evidently on grounds of what the city was losing rather than any dispute over who had the right to decide — Carlos, as surviving spouse and co-owner, was never contested as the decision-maker.

For the report, De la Cruz is a valuable contrast to Fisher Landau along exactly the founder-status axis: both museums closed and their collections eventually reached the auction block, but Fisher Landau's founder closed her own museum six years before dying, with the auction following a further six years later through an estate process; De la Cruz's founder died first, without warning, and the surviving spouse closed and sold within the same year — no locked succession plan, no attempted continuation, no institutional intermediary at all. It also sharpens the `decision_owner` vocabulary in a small but real way: **spouse** is an existing coded value, but every other spouse-decision case in the sample so far (Weisman) involves a *institutionalized* continuation (a foundation, a board); De la Cruz shows the same value covering an entirely different outcome — immediate, discretionary liquidation.
