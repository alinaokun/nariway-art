## Narrative
Museum Berggruen gives this sample its first case of a collection passing not into an independent nonprofit or foundation but into direct ownership by a national government — a structurally distinct pathway from every prior German case coded here. [[sammlung-boros]] found a "foundation" that was legally a GmbH; [[fondation-beyeler]] and [[muzej-lah]] both sit inside Switzerland's foundation-supervisory regime. Museum Berggruen sits inside neither: it is owned by the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz, a joint federal-and-state public foundation that also governs the rest of the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, financed on the same appropriations basis as any other branch of a national museum system. There is, in the ordinary sense, no separate "Museum Berggruen" balance sheet to pull at all — the sample's data-availability finding here is neither "a filing not yet obtained" (the US pattern) nor "no disclosure regime exists" (the [[yuz-museum]]/[[khao-yai-art-forest]] pattern), but a third, distinct shape: a real, well-documented public-budget system that simply does not disaggregate one collection's costs from the whole museum network's.

The case's most genuinely novel finding for this sample is the *durability of family involvement after full state ownership*. Berggruen's 2000 sale was explicitly, and by his own account, priced well below market value specifically so the collection would stay in Germany in a dedicated building — a founder-constraint exercised once, seven years before his death, through the terms of a single transaction rather than through any retained governance right. And yet the family did not simply walk away once the state took ownership: his son Nicolas Berggruen holds an ongoing board seat at the museum decades later, and in 2021 the family committed to donate €1 million a year starting in 2025, a real, recurring financial contribution layered on top of full public funding, now three generations deep (Heinz → sons Nicolas and Olivier). This is a different shape of founder-legacy persistence than anything else in the sample: not a family retaining control (contrast Barnes, or the Bass family at [[sid-richardson]]), and not a family stepping away entirely once an institution is independently governed (contrast MoMA's absorbed-founders model), but a family voluntarily remaining a *funder* of an institution it no longer owns or controls, years after the transfer.

The sale-price contradiction across sources ($120M / 200M DM / 253M DM / 400M DM / €126M / €129M) is worth naming precisely rather than picking a figure: it likely reflects genuinely different quantities being reported (the federal government's specific contribution vs. the combined federal-plus-Berlin-state total vs. a larger "originally discussed" sum), not simple reporting error, but no single source located this run disambiguates which figure answers which question. This is exactly the kind of contested-number case the case-template's binding rule exists for: better to carry all the candidate figures forward, flagged, than to assert false precision.
