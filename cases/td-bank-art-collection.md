# TD Art Collection (TD Bank Group)

One-line: A Canadian bank has built and continuously grown a 6,000-plus-work corporate art collection since 1963 with no individual founder, now used deliberately to foreground historically underrepresented Canadian artists — a second founder-less corporate case (after [[wesfarmers-art-collection]]) testing whether H6's governance-durability framing needs a founder at all.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample:** a second instance of Batch 48's Wesfarmers question — a large corporate collection with no individual decision owner, credited founder, or personal collecting sensibility at any point — but in a different sector (retail banking, not industrials) and jurisdiction (Canada), with a genuinely distinct institutional-design choice: TD built a dedicated, free, standalone public gallery for one specific sub-collection (Indigenous/Inuit art) from as early as 1986, decades before most of this dataset's other bank-collection cases (UBS, Deutsche Bank, HSBC) opened comparable public-facing spaces.

## Narrative (sourced)

The Toronto-Dominion Bank (formed by a 1955 merger, now TD Bank Group) began a general corporate art collection in 1963 on the stated premise that art functions as "a catalyst for dialogue" for colleagues, clients, and customers [secondary: TD Stories]. Two years later, in 1965, the bank began a specifically focused sub-collection of Inuit art from the Canadian Arctic, later formalized as a dedicated body of work assembled to mark Canada's 1967 centennial [secondary: TD Stories, iaccca.com]. In 1986 TD opened the TD Gallery of Inuit Art (renamed the TD Gallery of Indigenous Art) in Toronto — a free, standalone, rotating public exhibition space drawn from the wider corporate collection, a materially earlier public-gallery commitment than most other bank-collection cases in this dataset. The collection has since grown to more than 6,000 works spanning painting, sculpture, drawing, and photography, displayed mainly non-publicly across more than 1,000 TD branches, offices, and conference facilities in Canada and the United States [secondary: ARTnews, TD Stories]. Current program materials describe an explicit, stated curatorial mandate to "amplify diverse voices" and bring artists "often not being reflected in many museums and corporate collections" — including women, Black, Indigenous, Pan-Asian, Latinx, and LGBTQ2+ artists, as well as seniors and artists with disabilities — into the collection, a deliberate broadening of the original 1960s Inuit-art-specific focus [secondary: TD Stories]. No individual founder, controlling executive, or single decision-owner is named in any source located this run; acquisitions are credited to an in-house "TD Art team" operating within ordinary corporate structure, the same `no-identifiable-person` decision-owner shape as [[wesfarmers-art-collection]]. No sale, deaccessioning event, or funding crisis was located for the collection across its 63-year history, spanning multiple recessions and Canadian banking-sector consolidations.
