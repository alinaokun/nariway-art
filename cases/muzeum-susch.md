# Muzeum Susch (formerly Art Stations Foundation, Poznań)

One-line: A living Polish collector closed her twelve-year-old Polish museum in 2016 after a planned domestic relocation collapsed, then rebuilt the entire institution from scratch in a different country — a genuinely new pattern in this dataset: whole-institution cross-border migration, not closure, dispersal, or a static single-site museum.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample:** closes a second Eastern European instance (after [[kunsthalle-praha]]) while adding a genuinely distinct pathway shape this dataset had only seen once before, in miniature, at the Salsali Private Museum (Dubai to Berlin, noted but not coded — see `candidate-universe.md`): a founder relocating an entire institution, collection and all, across a national border rather than closing it outright or dispersing it. Also a second data point (after Susch's own family-board structure) on `governance_control_at_founding` inside a single-founder institution actually being family-plural from the outset, not sole.

## Narrative (sourced)

Grażyna Kulczyk, a Polish entrepreneur and one of the country's wealthiest self-made businesswomen, converted a former brewery complex in Poznań into the Art Stations exhibition space in 2004, running a substantial public program there for twelve years, including exhibitions of Dorota Nieznalska, Jan Berdyszak, Roman Opałka, and a Tadeusz Kantor/Piotr Uklański dialogue [secondary: Artsy, DESA Unicum, e-flux]. The Poznań exhibition program concluded in spring 2016 with a final show, after an intended relocation of the institution within Poland — reported as a Warsaw site — did not materialize [secondary: e-flux, Artforum]. Rather than dispersing the collection or discontinuing the institution, Kulczyk began construction the same year on an entirely new site: a former 12th-century monastery and 19th-century brewery in Susch, a small village in the Swiss Alps, under a newly registered Swiss foundation, Art Stations Foundation CH (registered 2017, seated in St. Moritz/Baar) [secondary: transartists.org, northdata.com]. Muzeum Susch opened to the public on January 2, 2019, with an inaugural exhibition, "A Woman Looking at Men Looking at Women," continuing and sharpening the collection's stated focus on conceptual art and overlooked, largely women, artists [secondary: Artforum, Sotheby's]. The Swiss foundation's governance is not sole-founder: public registry records list Kulczyk's son, Sebastian T. Kulczyk, as board chair, with Kulczyk herself and at least one other named individual as board members — a family-plural structure from the outset of the Swiss entity, distinct from a single controlling founder with no other named governance participant.

## Gaps / contradictions

- The exact mechanism of the 2016 Poznań closure (a "planned relocation fell through") is reported consistently across secondary sources but without a named specific reason for the Warsaw plan's failure (funding, zoning, or a change of mind) — coded `primary_friction` as a best-fit guess between funding-gap and zoning, flagged as uncertain.
- No financial figures of any kind were locatable for either the Polish or Swiss entity; Switzerland's foundation-disclosure regime is a structural, not tooling, gap for this dataset's schema, joining Georgia, Slovenia, and other non-US jurisdictions already flagged this way.
- Whether the Swiss foundation's family-board structure (son as chair) reflects a deliberate succession-planning choice or simply local governance/residency requirements for a Swiss Stiftung was not resolved this run.
