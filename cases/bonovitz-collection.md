## Narrative
Jill and Sheldon Bonovitz began collecting art together in the late 1960s and, over roughly five decades, assembled one of the country's most significant collections of work by self-taught 20th-century American artists, figures like James Castle, Bill Traylor, and Martín Ramírez, who worked outside formal training, often in isolated circumstances and with unconventional materials (reclaimed wood, sheet metal, house paint, stove soot). In 2011 the couple formalized a promised gift of more than 200 works by 27 artists to the Philadelphia Museum of Art, where the collection was first shown in the 2013 exhibition "Great and Mighty Things" and again in 2023's "Of God and Country." Sheldon Bonovitz, chairman emeritus of the law firm Duane Morris, has served on the boards of trustees of both the Philadelphia Museum of Art and the Barnes Foundation, placing this case squarely inside the H2 "insider exception" pattern this evidence base's disconfirmation hunts have now confirmed nine times: museums readily accept intact collections most often from donors who are already structurally embedded in the institution, not from arm's-length collectors. Unlike most of this sample's donate-to-existing-museum cases, there is no dedicated foundation, trust, or filing vehicle standing between the collectors and the museum; the gift moved directly from a private couple to an existing institution with no intermediate financial structure to trace.
