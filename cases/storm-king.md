# Storm King Art Center

One-line: Star Expansion Company owners Ralph E. Ogden and his son-in-law H. Peter Stern founded Storm King in 1960; a planned Hudson River School museum pivoted to monumental modern sculpture (the 1967 purchase of 13 David Smith works was the hinge), and it now sits on ~500 acres, thriving 66 years on across two family successions.

**Why in the sample:** the durability benchmark for the *found-art-park* pathway — a founder-family institution that survived both founders and repeatedly professionalized. Tests H7A (a clear multi-generation decision-owner spine) and H4 (landscape-scale placement as the distinctive product).

## Narrative (sourced)

Ralph E. Ogden and his son-in-law H. Peter Stern, co-owners of the Mountainville-based Star Expansion Company, founded Storm King in 1960 and originally envisioned a museum of Hudson River School painting housed in a 1935 Normandy-style residence [secondary: [stormking.org](https://stormking.org/about/history/)]. Within a year the founders had committed instead to modern sculpture, and the 1967 purchase of thirteen works from the estate of David Smith (1906–1965) established the model that made Storm King famous — large sculpture sited across an open Hudson Valley landscape rather than in galleries [secondary: [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/feature/storm-king-art-center-most-famous-works-1234576216/)]. That landscape-scale placement is the distinctive product (H4), and the founders protected it structurally by acquiring roughly 2,100 additional acres of Schunnemunk Mountain to guard the viewshed [secondary]. The decision-owner spine is unusually clean for H7A: Stern ran the center after Ogden's death in 1974, stepped down as president in 2008 in favor of his son John P. Stern, and died in 2016 — three generations of continuous family stewardship [secondary: [Hudson Valley One](https://hudsonvalleyone.com/2016/05/12/art-gone-wild-john-stern-reimagines-the-500-acre-sculpture-park-created-by-his-family/)]. Financially it is the strongest of the four parks coded here: FY2024 net assets of $83.97M against $9.33M of expenses give a net-assets-to-opex cushion near 9.0x [primary, [990 via ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/141457573)]. As always that ratio is an **upper bound** — the ~500 acres and buildings sit inside net assets and the true endowment corpus (Schedule D) has not been pulled.
