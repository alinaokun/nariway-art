# Hall Art Foundation

One-line: Andrew and Christine Hall founded a nonprofit in 2007 to show their postwar/contemporary collection on a converted Vermont dairy farm, then in 2010 added a second, larger site in Germany — a nearly thousand-year-old castle that had been Georg Baselitz's home and studio until 2006 — making it this dataset's clearest dual-country, single-collection foundation.

**Why in the sample:** a living-founder foundation split across two countries and two very different building types (a New England farmstead vs. a German castle-monastery), adding international and building-typology variety, and a useful comparator to [[sammlung-boros]] and [[feuerle-collection]]'s German gGmbH structure for testing whether that governance shape recurs across unrelated collectors.

## Narrative (sourced)

Andrew Hall, a commodities trader, and his wife Christine assembled a large postwar and contemporary collection and in 2007 founded the Hall Art Foundation to show it publicly, choosing a former dairy farm in Reading, Vermont — a cluster of a converted 19th-century stone farmhouse and three barns, expanded with a new reception center in 2017 [secondary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_Art_Foundation); [Hall Art Foundation](https://hallartfoundation.org/)]. The Foundation's second and larger site came from an unplanned opportunity: a studio visit to painter Georg Baselitz, whose home since 1976 had been Schloss Derneburg, a fortified castle near Hannover with a nearly thousand-year history as a monastery and aristocratic residence; when Baselitz sold the property in 2006, the Halls acquired and renovated it, opening it as the Kunstmuseum Schloss Derneburg museum site in 2010, operated through a dedicated German nonprofit entity, Schloss Derneburg Museum gGmbH [secondary: [independent-collectors.com](https://independent-collectors.com/cities/reading/hall-art-foundation); [BMW Art Guide interview](https://bmw-art-guide.com/cities/derneburg/interview-with-christine-and-andrew-hall)]. Both sites are free and open only by appointment, seasonally, with guided tours — a lighter-touch access model than a conventional year-round museum. The German site's gGmbH structure is the same legal form this dataset has already flagged for [[sammlung-boros]] as carrying no independently locatable governing board — worth tracking as a second data point (not yet a confirmed pattern with only two cases) on whether Germany's appointment-only private-museum scene systematically favors this particular founder-controlled corporate form over a traditional foundation-with-board structure.
