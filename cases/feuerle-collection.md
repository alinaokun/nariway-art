## Narrative
The Feuerle Collection earns its place in the sample less for its individual facts, which are thin (no financials, no board, no object count, all genuinely unlocatable rather than merely unpulled), than for what it confirms about a pattern this dataset first noticed in [[sammlung-boros]]: a European private collector opens an appointment-only museum inside a converted historic building, describes the entity in press materials as a "foundation" or "non-profit organization," and yet no independent governing board, foundation charter, or public filing is locatable through ordinary search. With two cases now sharing this exact shape, it is worth treating as a genuine sub-pattern rather than a one-off gap: **appointment-only private European museums may use "foundation" language as a curatorial/prestige framing rather than as a description of an actual independent-board legal structure**, the opposite direction from [[museo-poldi-pezzoli]] or [[fondation-beyeler]], where the foundation label corresponds to a real, documented governance instrument. The distinguishing test, not yet run systematically, would be pulling the actual Handelsregister/Stiftungsregister entry for each entity, which WebFetch's continued block prevents this run.

Substantively, Feuerle differs from Boros in a way worth keeping distinct: Boros holds a single-period contemporary collection in a bunker used largely as-found; Feuerle deliberately juxtaposes deep antiquities (Han-to-Qing dynasty Chinese furniture, 7th-13th century Khmer sculpture) against contemporary work inside a bunker extensively re-engineered by John Pawson (including a geothermal system drawing on the site's groundwater lake) — a coherence strategy built on **contrast across eras**, not a single thesis. This gives the sample a second `coherent-multi` bunker-museum case rather than a duplicate of Boros's `tight-single-thesis` contemporary focus.
