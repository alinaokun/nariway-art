# Barjeel Art Foundation

One-line: Sultan Sooud Al Qassemi's Barjeel Art Foundation (Sharjah, UAE, est. 2010) has grown a personal collection of Arab modern/contemporary art to 1,800+ works and built public reach almost entirely through loans (130+ institutions globally) rather than a permanent building, and as of 2026 the founder is publicly soliciting UAE venue partners because the collection has outgrown its current display capacity — a living-founder test of the "public life without a building" pathway at real scale, and the sample's first Gulf/Emirati case.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why coded this run:** closes two real gaps at once — no prior case in the dataset is founded by a collector based in the Gulf, and no prior case tests the Thoma/Petrucci-style "loans-only, no dedicated public building" pathway at this scale (1,800 works, 130+ borrowing institutions) with a still-living, still-actively-collecting founder. Also a distinct thesis from the sample's existing regional-canon cases (Souls Grown Deep, Cisneros): Barjeel's stated mission is building and internationalizing the canon of modern Arab art specifically, a field major Western museums have historically underrepresented — a clean instance of H5 (filling a representational gap).

## Narrative (sourced)

Sultan Sooud Al Qassemi, an Emirati writer and commentator, established the Barjeel Art Foundation in Sharjah in 2010 specifically to manage, preserve, and exhibit his personal collection of modern and contemporary Arab art, with an explicit mission of contributing to the intellectual development of the Gulf region's art scene [secondary: universes.art, Wikipedia]. The collection has grown to more than 1,800 works, drawn from Arab artists worldwide and their diaspora, spanning painting, sculpture, and installation [secondary: universes.art]. Unlike most standalone-museum cases in this dataset, Barjeel has never built a dedicated permanent public venue; its public reach instead comes almost entirely through in-house exhibitions at rented or partnered space, an active publications program, and — most distinctively — an unusually large volume of international loans, having lent works to more than 130 institutions globally, including Tate Liverpool, Tate St Ives, MoMA PS1, the Art Institute of Chicago, and the Mori Art Museum [secondary: universes.art]. This places Barjeel structurally closer to the sample's loans-only cases ([[thoma]], [[petrucci-family-foundation]], [[broad]]) than to its standalone-museum cases, but with a materially different founding thesis: where Thoma and Petrucci frame loans-only as a deliberate model chosen from strength, Al Qassemi's own 2026 public appeal — soliciting UAE cultural institutions and public buildings willing to display at least 400 of the collection's 1,800 works on a "medium to long-term" (three-plus-year) basis — reads as a founder actively seeking more permanent display capacity than the foundation currently has, a real and current friction point rather than a settled strategic choice [secondary: The Art Newspaper, 2026-05-14 coverage cited via search]. No board, executive director, or succession structure beyond the founder himself was located in sources checked this run; as with several of the dataset's other living-founder, founder-sole cases (Bechtler, Rennie), the honest read is `governance_control_at_founding: founder-sole` and `survived_founder: not-yet-testable`, with the collection's long-term durability an open question once Al Qassemi is no longer the one making acquisition and lending decisions.
