# Artists' Legacy Foundation

One-line: painter Squeak Carnwath and sculptor Viola Frey — both living at the time — co-founded a peer-support grant-making foundation in 2000 with Carnwath's husband Gary Knecht; after Frey's 2004 death it also became the steward of her own artwork and archive, but its primary activity from the start has been funding *other* artists' work, not preserving its founders' own collections — a foundation model built around artistic community rather than a single legacy.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why coded now:** the second of the three artist-foundation leads named in [[candidate-universe]]'s "Artist-endowed foundation candidates" list still open after the prior two runs coded Nancy Graves, Resnick/Passlof, and Frankenthaler. It is a deliberately different shape from all four: co-founded by artists who were *both still living*, one of whom (Carnwath) remains alive and active in the foundation today, and its mission is peer philanthropy first, single-artist-legacy stewardship second (Frey's own estate joined the foundation's work four years after founding, not at its origin).

## Narrative (sourced)

Squeak Carnwath, a painter who has lived and worked in Oakland since 1970 and taught at UC Berkeley (now Professor Emerita), studied under sculptor Viola Frey at the California College of Arts and Crafts [secondary: [Wikipedia, Squeak Carnwath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squeak_Carnwath)]. In 2000 Carnwath, Frey, and Carnwath's husband Gary Knecht incorporated the Artists' Legacy Foundation together — notably, with both artist co-founders still alive and working, a structurally different starting point from every other artist-foundation case in this dataset, each of which was created by or for a single artist's own posthumous estate [secondary: [artistslegacyfoundation.org/about-us](https://artistslegacyfoundation.org/about-us/)]. The founders' stated purpose was for painters and sculptors "to come together to support and encourage fellow artists," not primarily to preserve either founder's own legacy.

Viola Frey died in 2004. Rather than spin off a separate Frey-specific vehicle, the existing foundation absorbed her artwork, archives, and reference materials and designated her its first "Legacy Artist" — a defined category the foundation's own materials describe as protecting the legacies of deceased artists connected to it [secondary: violafrey.org; Cause IQ profile]. Cynthia de Bos, the foundation's director of collections and archives, manages the Viola Frey Archives and fields research requests, functioning as a conventional single-artist-estate operation nested inside the larger, ongoing granting foundation [secondary: search synthesis, violafrey.org/contact]. In 2007 the foundation launched its signature program, the Artist Award — an anonymously nominated, unrestricted $25,000 grant to a painter or sculptor, juried each year by a rotating panel — and has distributed more than $450,000 through it since [secondary: artistslegacyfoundation.org].

Carnwath remains the foundation's living president as of 2026, giving this case a governance shape this dataset has not otherwise coded cleanly: a `founder-sole`-adjacent structure where the controlling founder is neither deceased (removing the succession-vacuum question entirely, for now) nor a will-appointed board acting on a dead founder's behalf, but an actively engaged living artist continuing to run the institution she co-founded a quarter-century ago. Financially the foundation is thinly margined on an annual basis (2024 revenue of $136K against expenses of $312K) but sits on a comparatively large asset base (~$20M), a profile consistent with a foundation drawing down investment gains to fund its award program rather than one under any near-term financial strain.
