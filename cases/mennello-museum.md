# Mennello Museum of American Art

One-line: Marilyn and Michael Mennello donated 35 paintings by self-taught Florida folk artist Earl Cunningham to the City of Orlando in 1998, which opened a dedicated museum in a historic house to hold them, a municipal-ownership pathway distinct from every other founder-museum case in this sample, and one that has now outlived both founders (Marilyn d. 2006, Michael d. 2020) by two decades and six years respectively.

**Why in the sample:** the sample's first case where the receiving institution from day one is a city government rather than an independent nonprofit board, a family trust, or the founders themselves, `governance_control_at_founding: parent-institution` here means a municipality, not a university (the pattern this value has coded so far, e.g. [[anderson-collection-stanford]], [[ogden]]). Also a clean, low-drama survived-founder case at modest scale, useful as a counterweight to the sample's marquee institutions.

## Narrative
Marilyn Logsdon Mennello and Michael A. Mennello collected the work of self-taught Florida folk painter Earl Cunningham (1893–1977) and, working with Orlando Mayor Glenda Hood, donated 35 of his paintings to the City of Orlando in 1998. The city opened a dedicated museum to hold the gift inside the Phillips House, a historic residence built for Howard Phillips, and named it for the donors. In 2018 Michael Mennello (Marilyn had died in 2006) gave a further 14 paintings and 5 sculptures, appraised at $8.75 million, and established the Michael A. and the Honorable Marilyn Logsdon Mennello Foundation. Michael Mennello died of COVID-19-related complications in December 2020. The museum has continued operating under City of Orlando ownership through both founders' deaths, supported day-to-day by the nonprofit Friends of the Mennello Museum, whose most recent available Form 990 (FY2024) reports $306,000 in revenue against $355,000 in expenses on $2.08 million in total assets, figures for the support organization specifically, not a full museum operating budget, which the city does not appear to disclose separately from its general arts and culture spending. The case is useful chiefly for its governance shape: unlike this sample's university-partnership cases, the "parent institution" here from day one is a municipal government, giving the report a third distinct flavor of the "founder hands off control before death" pattern (family foundation, university, and now city).
