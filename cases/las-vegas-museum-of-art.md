One-line: Elaine Wynn — Wynn Resorts co-founder, billionaire philanthropist, and a longtime LACMA trustee — spent roughly two decades pursuing a standalone art museum for Las Vegas, watched an earlier institutional vehicle for that goal (a Reno-based nonprofit branch effort) fold into the existing Nevada Museum of Art rather than open in Las Vegas, then in 2024 founded a fresh, independent nonprofit (LVMA) with a 50-year LACMA collection-access partnership, died in April 2025 before any venue opened, and the project — now delayed to a fall 2029 full opening, with a smaller "Grand Central Galleries" prelude space open in 2026 — is proceeding under the board and partnership she built.

## Narrative (sourced)

The current Las Vegas Museum of Art is not Las Vegas's first attempt at a dedicated art museum. In 2019, a Reno-based effort — the Nevada Museum of Art — hired a curator (Heather Harmon) specifically to lead a proposed Las Vegas branch, reported at the time with a $250 million price tag and 125,000–150,000 sq ft. That branch effort traces back further still: an earlier nonprofit, the Art Museum at Symphony Park, had been pursuing a Las Vegas museum independently for years before formally merging its board into the Nevada Museum of Art on May 24, 2018 — folding what had been a standalone Las Vegas ambition into an existing Reno institution rather than producing a new Las Vegas venue [source: knpr.org, 2018-06; reviewjournal.com archival coverage; confidence: secondary]. No Las Vegas venue resulted from that 2018 merger; a Nevada Museum of Art spokesperson later confirmed the *current* LVMA project has **no connection** to that earlier, absorbed effort [source: search-synthesized from designdash.com; confidence: secondary]. Coded here as `abandoned-plan` (the Symphony Park effort's Las Vegas-specific ambition) folded via `merger-into-institution` (the 2018 absorption into Nevada Museum of Art) — a genuine two-step prior pathway distinct from LVMA's own founding.

Elaine Wynn — co-founder of Wynn Resorts, a major art collector, and a LACMA trustee for years before her death — is uniformly credited in press coverage as the person who revived the goal, this time founding an entirely new, independent nonprofit rather than working through the existing Nevada Museum of Art structure. The Art Newspaper's December 2023 report frames the project as backed by "Elaine Wynn and LACMA," with LACMA providing curatorial partnership rather than ownership: a 50-year agreement grants LVMA access to LACMA's roughly 150,000-object collection for its own curation or to receive LACMA-curated traveling shows, while LVMA itself remains legally and financially independent, with its own board, funding, and executive leadership [source: theartnewspaper.com/2023/12/23; designdash.com; confidence: secondary]. Wynn served as LVMA's Founding President of the Board of Trustees. She died in April 2025, at 82 [source: next.io obituary; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_Wynn; confidence: secondary].

The project has continued past her death on a **slipped but unbroken** timeline: the City of Las Vegas approved a 1.5-acre land gift in Symphony Park (2024), and the first concrete public-facing step, "Grand Central Galleries" — a multipurpose media-lab space on the lower level of a Symphony Park parking garage, intended as a communal entry point ahead of the full museum — opened in 2026, while the full museum's opening has moved from an earlier fall 2028 target to **fall 2029** [source: reviewjournal.com/local, neon.reviewjournal.com; confidence: secondary]. No source found this run reports any governance disruption, funding crisis, or leadership vacuum following Wynn's death — the project appears, so far, to be executing the plan she built rather than stalling, though it remains too early (no permanent venue yet open) to call this a completed `survived_founder` test.
