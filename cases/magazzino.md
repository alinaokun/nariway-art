## Narrative
Nancy Olnick and Giorgio Spanu, a New York–Sardinia collecting couple, opened Magazzino in 2017 in the Hudson Valley hamlet of Cold Spring to house one of the largest US concentrations of postwar Italian art, anchored on Arte Povera — Mario and Marisa Merz, Jannis Kounellis, Luciano Fabro, Michelangelo Pistoletto, Alighiero Boetti — the defining tight single thesis that distinguishes this case. The name means "warehouse": the original building is a converted 1964 industrial structure reworked by architect Miguel Quismondo (press variously calls it a former computer factory), to which a purpose-built pavilion, the Robert Olnick Pavilion by Alberto Campo Baeza and Quismondo, was added in 2023. The institution operates as a private foundation (a 990-PF filer, EIN 83-1666946) that is founder-funded and founder-controlled, with an inaugural executive director, Vittorio Calabrese, running day-to-day; its FY2024 filing shows ~$25.4M in net assets against ~$4.8M in expenses, a modest ~5.3x coverage (upper bound) that reflects both its youth and its scale relative to the sample's mega-endowed cases. Because it is only about eight years old and both founders are living, its succession is `not-yet-testable` and its long-term constraints are `open` rather than resolved. The strongest signal here is cultural-opportunity, not viability: a coherent, scholarly, single-movement collection with a research center, the kind of distinctiveness the report weighs separately from durability. The chief unknowns — total works, attendance, any endowment — sit with the foundation directly, and the revenue swings in the 990-PF are donor gifts, not earned income, a common private-foundation reading trap.
