# UOB Art Collection

One-line: United Overseas Bank has built a Southeast Asian art collection since the early 1970s — now variously reported at 2,600 to 8,000+ works — that funds the region's longest-running art prize (UOB Painting of the Year, since 1982) and anchors a dedicated public gallery at National Gallery Singapore, making it the corporate sample's first Southeast Asian case and a rare instance of a bank collection built explicitly to support a regional art ecosystem rather than as office decoration or an investment hedge.

## Narrative (sourced)

United Overseas Bank began collecting art in the early 1970s for two stated reasons: to furnish its then-new Singapore headquarters, and to support and engage with a fledgling local and regional art scene that had little institutional backing at the time [secondary: The Peak Magazine]. The bank formalized that support in 1982 with the UOB Painting of the Year competition, now in its fifth decade and widely described as the longest-running art prize in Singapore and among the most recognized in Southeast Asia, functioning as what one regional outlet called "a direct gateway to the regional art ecosystem" for emerging artists [secondary: Hanoi Grapevine]. The collection itself grew alongside the prize; it is displayed across UOB's head office, branches, and offices in multiple countries, and a portion anchors the UOB Southeast Asia Gallery at National Gallery Singapore, described as housing the world's largest collection of modern Southeast Asian art. UOB is one of the Gallery's Founding Partners and has been a long-standing collaborator since the Gallery's 2015 opening era; the partnership was renewed for a further five years in January 2024, explicitly to fund "important acquisitions" for the shared gallery [secondary: National Gallery Singapore]. Unlike this dataset's other active-steward corporate cases (UBS, JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America), which frame their collections primarily as institutional assets or lending programs, UOB's own public materials frame the collection's founding purpose as regional cultural support first — a closer cousin to a corporate foundation model (Fondation Cartier, Fondazione Prada) operating inside an ordinary commercial bank's structure rather than a separately incorporated entity. Longtime UOB chairman Dr. Wee Cho Yaw (chairman from 1974(preceded family involvement further back), an ARTnews Top 200-listed personal collector of Impressionist and modern art, is documented as a major collecting figure in his own right, though this run's sources do not establish a direct, sourced line between his personal collecting and the corporate program's specific acquisitions or governance — flagged as a gap, not asserted as fact.
