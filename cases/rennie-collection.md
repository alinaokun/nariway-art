## Narrative
Bob Rennie built his collection and its public face — an appointment-only gallery inside his own real-estate company's Chinatown headquarters — entirely outside the museum-or-foundation binary this sample otherwise tracks. There was never a 501(c)(3)-equivalent Canadian charity created for the art itself; the gallery was simply a floor of Rennie's own building, opened to the public by reservation because Rennie chose to, not because any legal structure required it. What makes this case useful for breadth is the *shape* of its ongoing disposition: rather than one clean gift, retention, or sale, Rennie is running two simultaneous, separate philanthropic tracks while still alive — staged donations of individual works to a national public museum (three tranches and counting, 2017–2026) and, unrelatedly, a real-estate and cash gift that seeded an entirely different public institution (the Chinese Canadian Museum) in the same building that once housed his own private gallery. Neither track required an intact-collection acceptance decision from a receiving museum in the way [[what-we-now-believe]]'s H2 discussion describes — the National Gallery of Canada has instead absorbed the Rennie collection through a series of selective, negotiated, self-paced gifts, an approach that sidesteps the all-or-nothing "will they take it intact" question this report's H2 finding is built around. This is a useful counter-shape for the pathway taxonomy: not a founder-museum, not a foundation, not a single bequest, but a living collector personally managing his own multi-track exit from both the art and the real estate that displayed it.
