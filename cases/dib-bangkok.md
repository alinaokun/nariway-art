# Dib Bangkok (Dib International Contemporary Art Museum)

One-line: A Thai collector completed his late father's unfinished museum plan, opening Dib Bangkok in a converted 1980s warehouse in December 2025 to house the elder Osathanugrah's 1,000+-work contemporary collection — a second-generation completion case testing whether a founder's death *before* an institution opens (rather than after) changes the succession dynamics H3/H6 track.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]] — the single machine source the export reads. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample.** A genuinely distinct timing variant on the founder-succession question: every other second-generation case in this dataset ([[ohara-museum]], [[sadberk-hanim-museum]], [[instituto-moreira-salles]]) involves a founder who saw the institution operating before death. Petch Osathanugrah died in 2023 while still only *planning* the museum; his son Purat inherited an unrealized intention, not a running institution, and had to make the founding decision himself rather than merely continue one. Also closes a live Southeast Asia/Thailand geography gap and adds a fast-moving brand-partnership data point (Chanel Culture Fund, within nine months of opening).

## Narrative (sourced)

Petch Osathanugrah — an industrialist, musician, and heir to the Osotspa energy-drink fortune — spent decades assembling a contemporary art collection exceeding 1,000 works, and was named to ARTnews's Top 200 Collectors list five years running from 2018 [secondary: ARTnews]. He had begun planning a private museum to house the collection when he died suddenly of a heart attack in 2023, before the project opened [secondary: ARTnews obituary coverage]. His son, collector Purat "Chang" Osathanugrah, took up the unfinished project as founding chairman, engaging Los Angeles-based WHY Architecture together with local firm Architects 49 to convert a 1980s steel warehouse into an 11-gallery, roughly 7,000 sq m museum with a 1,400 sq m courtyard, sculpture garden, and penthouse event space [secondary: designboom, Dezeen]. Dib Bangkok — styled as the Dib International Contemporary Art Museum — opened to the public December 21, 2025, described in coverage as Thailand's first international contemporary art museum [secondary: Bangkok Post, SCMP]. Admission is ticketed (550 THB for Thai citizens, 700 THB for foreign visitors, reduced student rates), with an outdoor zone free to the public [secondary: GetYourGuide, museum FAQ]. Within nine months of opening, the museum became the Chanel Culture Fund's first Southeast Asian art partner, an annual program commissioning a local artist for display there — a partnership older founder-museums in this dataset took years to secure [secondary: ARTnews].

Dib Bangkok is a useful complication of this dataset's usual "founder-status-at-transition" framing: unlike every prior second-generation case, the founder never saw his own institution open. The son's decision to complete rather than abandon the project — with no located written instruction requiring him to do so — is closer to [[instituto-moreira-salles]]'s and [[ohara-museum]]'s "family steps in without a documented mandate" pattern than to a will-directed case like [[mcnay-art-museum]] or [[rosenwald-collection-nga-loc]]. At nine months old, no durability signal is available yet; the Chanel partnership is an early positive access-and-visibility signal, not a survival test.

- Legal/governance structure (nonprofit foundation vs. private commercial entity) is unconfirmed; ticketed admission at meaningful prices (550-700 THB) is consistent with either a mission-driven nonprofit or a commercially operated private museum, and sourcing located this run does not resolve which.
