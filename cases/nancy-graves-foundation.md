# Nancy Graves Foundation

One-line: sculptor Nancy Graves (1939-1995), best known for her early Bactrian-camel installations and cross-disciplinary practice, endowed a private foundation through her will; it has run continuously since 1996 on a board she herself selected, funding artist grants, exhibition loans, and a catalogue raisonné — no gallery, no public building, a pure grant-making/scholarly-infrastructure legacy vehicle.

## Narrative (sourced)

Nancy Graves built a genuinely cross-disciplinary practice — sculpture, painting, printmaking, film — after an MFA at Yale (1964, alongside Richard Serra, whom she married and later divorced) and a career-launching 1969 solo show at the Whitney built around three life-sized Bactrian camel sculptures, at the time the youngest artist and only the fifth woman to receive one [secondary: [Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/artists/519); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Graves)]. She died of cancer in 1995 at 55, having provided in her will for a foundation to steward her legacy; it became active in 1996 with a board she had herself chosen — a founder-designed, but not founder-sole or family-dominated, governance structure from the outset [secondary: [nancygravesfoundation.org/about](https://www.nancygravesfoundation.org/about)]. Thirty years on, the foundation shows no succession crisis of the Terra/Corcoran type this dataset otherwise tracks, likely because it was never built around a public-facing building or director role that a founder's absence could destabilize — its activity is grants to individual artists, loans of Graves's own work to museum exhibitions, a catalogue raisonné project, and a research/study center for visiting scholars. Financially it is a small, quietly-run private foundation: assets moved only slightly, from $13.46M (2023) to $13.18M (2024) to roughly $12.8M (2025) per aggregator-reported figures, with charitable disbursements (~$650K in 2025) tracking close to the ~5%-of-assets minimum private foundations must distribute annually — a stable-but-not-growing profile, `durability_signal: moderate` rather than `strong`, since three consecutive years of gently declining assets (on WebSearch-only sourcing, not confirmed against the primary 990-PF) is a mild caution the primary filing could resolve either way.
