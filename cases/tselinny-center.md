## Narrative
Tselinny is a genuine schema stress-test on two axes at once. Like Onera, it owns no art collection — it is explicitly, permanently non-collecting by institutional policy, a program-and-residency-driven "center of contemporary culture" rather than a museum. And like MONA, it sits inside its founder's personal corporate structure rather than an independently endowed charitable entity, with no comparable financial disclosure regime to test that structure against from the outside.

What makes Tselinny distinct from both prior cases is a durability risk that is *external* to the institution's own program or finances: founder Kairat Boranbayev was arrested in March 2022 by Kazakhstan's Agency for Financial Monitoring on embezzlement and money-laundering charges tied to a gas-supply deal, sentenced in March 2023, and released on probation after surrendering a substantial portion of assets to the state — part of President Tokayev's broader post-2022-unrest move against Nazarbayev-era business elites (Boranbayev is connected by marriage to the Nazarbayev family). Tselinny's own director, Jamilya Nurkalieva, publicly stated the situation had "been fully resolved" with no ongoing implications for the museum's funding. But because the institution reportedly remains inside Boranbayev's existing corporate structure (Almaly) rather than a separately capitalized or city-transferred entity — a contrast one Kazakhstani cultural-criticism outlet draws explicitly against Almaty Museum of Arts, coded as `succession_locked_before_founder_death: no` for the same reason, only with the future risk already partially realized once — this is a live, sourced illustration of exactly the founder-sole durability risk the report's H8 hypothesis anticipates, not a hypothetical one.

Programmatically, Tselinny is thriving: continuous exhibitions and residencies, a second edition of the Korkut Sonic Arts Triennale running through mid-2026, and an explicit "horizontal institution" curatorial philosophy that refuses both a head curator and a permanent collection. Paired with Almaty Museum of Arts, the sample now has two Kazakhstani cases funded by different oligarch-founders, opened within roughly a year of each other, taking opposite structural bets on the same underlying risk, one moving toward transfer/mixed governance, one reportedly staying inside the founder's private holding company.
