# Fondation Pierre Bergé – Yves Saint Laurent / Jardin Majorelle

One-line: Yves Saint Laurent and his life-and-business partner Pierre Bergé founded a public-interest foundation for their couture legacy six years before Saint Laurent's 2008 death, then, after his death, Bergé deliberately auctioned their entirely separate personal art and decorative-arts collection (733 lots, a record €373.9M) rather than keep, donate, or bequeath it — directing the proceeds toward the Foundation and medical research, and toward Jardin Majorelle, the Marrakech garden-museum the couple had already been publicly stewarding since 1980 — producing a rare, cleanly-documented case where a founding couple pre-built the institutional wrapper for one legacy (the fashion house) while choosing total, planned dispersal for a separate, adjacent one (the personal collection).

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why coded this run:** a genuinely distinct multi-pathway shape the dataset had not coded before — not a single collection facing one disposition choice, but **two separate bodies of material from the same founding couple, deliberately routed to different pathways at the same historical moment** (the couture/design legacy into a purpose-built foundation and two museums; the personal art collection into total, planned auction). It also adds a `decision_owner: spouse` case distinct from every prior instance in the sample: unlike [[weisman]] (a widow institutionalizing continuation) or [[de-la-cruz-collection]] (a spouse choosing immediate discretionary liquidation with no institutional intermediary), Bergé's decision routed the *auction proceeds themselves* directly back into an institution he and Saint Laurent had already built together — neither pure institutionalization nor pure discretionary liquidation, but both at once, split across two different assets. It is also this dataset's first same-sex-partnership succession case, formalized via a French PACS civil union days before Saint Laurent's death.

## Narrative (sourced)

Pierre Bergé met Yves Saint Laurent in 1958; the two became life partners, and from 1961 business partners as well, when they launched the Yves Saint Laurent couture house together [secondary: PinkNews, multiple]. Their romantic relationship ended amicably in 1976, but the business and personal partnership continued for decades; in 1980 the couple bought and began restoring Jardin Majorelle, a historic garden in Marrakech originally designed by French artist Jacques Majorelle, opening it to the public and later adding the Musée Berbère (2011) inside the grounds — a public-access commitment that predates any formal foundation by more than two decades [secondary: multiple]. In December 2002, with Saint Laurent still alive, the two founded the Fondation Pierre Bergé – Yves Saint Laurent, recognized under French law as a public-interest foundation ("reconnue d'utilité publique"), to preserve the couture house's archive and design legacy — a pre-death institutional lock-in six years ahead of Saint Laurent's death, in the same family of cases as [[fondation-beyeler]] (28 years pre-death) and [[chinati]] (a living-founder permanence doctrine), though on a shorter horizon than either. Days before Saint Laurent's death on June 1, 2008, the couple formalized their decades-long relationship through a French PACS (Pacte Civil de Solidarité) civil union [secondary: PinkNews]. The following year, Bergé made a deliberate and separate decision: rather than keep, donate, or bequeath the couple's personal art and decorative-arts collection — assembled over roughly forty years and distinct from the couture archive already held by the Foundation — he auctioned the entire 733-lot collection at Christie's in Paris (staged at the Grand Palais, where more than 30,000 visitors viewed the works during a public preview), achieving a then-record €373.9M for a single-owner sale, spanning Old Masters, Cubist paintings by Picasso, Brancusi sculpture, and Art Deco furniture, plus a Goya given directly to the Louvre [secondary: NPR, museeyslparis.com, Wikipedia]. Proceeds were directed back toward the Fondation Pierre Bergé – Yves Saint Laurent and toward AIDS and medical research, a cause Bergé had long supported publicly [secondary: museeyslparis.com]. Bergé went on to open Musée Yves Saint Laurent Paris in October 2017, weeks after his own death on September 8, and a twin Musée Yves Saint Laurent Marrakech the same year, both under the Foundation's governance [secondary: Wikipedia]. Bergé's own succession was itself resolved before his death: earlier in 2017 he married Madison Cox, a landscape designer and longtime Foundation board member, who became Foundation president on Bergé's death and remains in that role, alongside a named multi-member board including figures such as Paloma Picasso [secondary: Bloomberg LEI filing, museeyslparis.com]. Nine years past Bergé's death and eighteen past Saint Laurent's, the Foundation operates two museums in two countries plus the Marrakech garden complex with no documented crisis, funding gap, or governance dispute — a clean survived-both-founders case.
