## Narrative
The Brauer case is the sample's sharpest current instance of a friction almost every institution-side entry in [[market-intelligence]] #6 describes abstractly: a parent institution deciding that a collection is a liability to be monetized rather than an asset to be carried forever, playing out against the explicit, personal, litigated objection of the person who spent decades building it. It differs from [[cam-raleigh]] in the specific mechanism — CAM's board dissolved its own program from sustained revenue decline; Valparaiso's administration deaccessioned from an *otherwise-solvent* institution's collection to solve a *different* budget line (dormitory renovations), a transfer of value from permanent collection to unrelated capital need that AAMD's own "direct care" deaccessioning rules (see [[market-intelligence]] #6 addendum, the Baltimore Museum of Art parallel) are specifically designed to prohibit for AAMD-accredited museums — Valparaiso, as a university teaching museum, was not bound by AAMD accreditation rules in the same way, which is precisely why the sale could proceed once the lawsuit failed on standing rather than on the merits. The founding director's own 97-year-old objection, litigated and then abandoned to avoid further personal financial risk, is a vivid, current illustration of how little formal power even a collection's own builder holds once legal title sits with a parent institution — directly relevant to [[case-template]]'s H8 constraint-design question, though inverted from most of this sample's cases: here the absence of the *builder's* legal control, not the founder's own constraint choices, is what let the disposition happen.
