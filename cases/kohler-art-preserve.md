## Narrative
The Art Preserve is this sample's first case built around **artist-built environments** rather than discrete artworks — entire houses, yards, and installations created by self-taught American artists (a category the Arts Center's own materials describe as previously lacking any dedicated museum anywhere in the world). It is also a genuine schema stress-test: every other coded case names a private collector as its founder; here, the person who drove the collecting, and who this run codes as the founder for the purpose of `founder_status_at_transition`/`survived_founder`, is a museum's own long-serving director, operating with institutional authority and an existing nonprofit board behind her the entire time, not a private individual disposing of personal property. Ruth DeYoung Kohler II spent 44 years rescuing environments that would otherwise likely have been demolished or scattered after their creators' deaths, then orchestrated a purpose-built museum to house them properly; she died seven months before it opened, and the institution has continued without evident disruption, funded substantially by a related family philanthropic entity (the Kohler Foundation Inc.) rather than the museum's own endowment. This reframes the "survived the founder" question the entire dataset otherwise asks about a founder's own mortality risk to the institution — here, the underlying nonprofit board and a related family foundation's continued funding were the actual durability mechanism, and the founding curator's death tested the *program's* continuity rather than the *institution's* legal existence, since the institution (the Arts Center, founded 1967) predates and will outlast this particular director by design. A candidate addition to the pathway vocabulary alongside the "strategic reallocation" gap already flagged: no current value cleanly names "an institution builds a dedicated new facility to properly house and preserve a category of at-risk cultural material its own staff spent decades personally rescuing."
