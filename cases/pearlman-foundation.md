## Narrative
The Pearlman collection spent 49 years (1976–2025) as a single-host long-term loan at Princeton University Art Museum — the same disposition shape as [[fisher-sfmoma]]'s 100-year Fisher-at-SFMOMA arrangement, but ending in permanent transfer rather than renewal. What makes this case worth adding to the sample is not the ending of the loan itself but the specific mechanism chosen for what comes next: rather than gifting the whole collection to one institution (the Fisher/SFMOMA path), auctioning it (the Ganz, Joe Lewis, or Fisher Landau-estate path), or dispersing it into dozens of unconnected institutions (the Vogel path), the Pearlman Foundation's trustees split 63 works across three major American museums — Brooklyn Museum, MoMA, LACMA — while establishing a standing obligation for the three institutions to lend works to one another going forward. It is neither a clean single-recipient gift nor a fully independent dispersal; it is closer to a permanent, three-way consortium built around a single, tightly coherent collection (the Cézanne core, plus Degas, Manet, Matisse, Modigliani, Renoir, Soutine, Toulouse-Lautrec, Van Gogh).

This sits usefully alongside the sample's other "genuinely novel mechanism" cases: [[daskalopoulos]]'s joint Guggenheim/MCA Chicago ownership (shared *title*, two parties) and [[vogel-collection]]'s fifty-states rule (independent recipients, no ongoing obligation between them). Pearlman is a third point on that spectrum — multiple independent owners, but bound together by a continuing circulation commitment — and is worth tracking as its own named mechanism (a "loan consortium" or "circulating co-stewardship" model) as more dispersal-pathway cases are coded, since the report's existing vocabulary (`donate-existing-museum-scattered`, `intentional-dispersal`) doesn't yet distinguish a one-time scattering from an ongoing, structured network.

The 1955 founding date, 19 years before Henry Pearlman's 1974 death, is also worth flagging against the report's central governance-lock-in finding (Pattern 1): if the Foundation's structure was deliberately established well before his death specifically to hold the art in trust, it would be a fourth pre-death lock-in case alongside Kreeger, Fondation Beyeler, and Storm King's Ogden-era foundation — but this session could not confirm intent (a coincidence-of-timing founding vs. a deliberate succession instrument) without the trust document, so it is flagged as a lead, not coded as a confirmed pattern instance.
