## Narrative
Yayoi Kusama, working from her own Tokyo studio across roughly seven decades of practice, established the Yayoi Kusama Foundation on January 24, 2017, as a general incorporated association, and opened the five-story Yayoi Kusama Museum in Shinjuku that October 1 with an inaugural exhibition of some 600 of her own works [secondary: Wikipedia; Spoon & Tamago]. Unlike most single-artist museums in this dataset, which open only after the artist's death (or, like Chinati, are founded during life but complete their institutional form afterward), Kusama built and opened this museum specifically so she could shape and see it operating during her own lifetime — she remained the museum's living, working center for nine more years, with rotating exhibitions programmed twice yearly [secondary: Yayoi Kusama Museum's own site].

She died on August 14, 2026, at age 97; the Museum, Foundation, her studio, and her company jointly announced the death on August 27, stating only that they would "carry forward Kusama's wishes and, through art, continue to bring hope and strength for the future to people around the world" [secondary: The Art Newspaper]. No successor leader is named, and no policy is stated for the fate of unfinished works, archive access, authentication of existing and future works, or whether her large-scale installations (the "Infinity Mirror Rooms" that anchor the museum's fourth floor) will ever be refabricated or altered after her death [secondary: The Art Newspaper]. This is precisely the succession-vacuum moment H6/H8 tracks, caught at its earliest possible point rather than reconstructed years later — worth a deliberate return visit in a future run once (if) a named successor or governance statement appears, to see whether this resolves toward the pattern of every other confirmed case in the H6/H8 evidence base (an independent-board handoff, per the pattern that has now held across fourteen consecutive tests) or becomes a genuine new disconfirming instance.
