# Mougins Museum of Classical Art (MACAM) → Female Artists of Mougins Museum (FAMM) — a founder liquidating one coherent collection to fund a second, inside the same institution

One-line: British collector Christian Levett opened a private museum in the French village of Mougins in 2011 around a roughly 1,000-piece classical antiquities and arms-and-armour collection (part of over 3,000 purchases across 25 years of collecting); in 2023, after ~250,000 visitors, he closed it as a classical-art museum and consigned the bulk of that collection to Christie's across six sales estimated near £22 million, then reopened the same building in June 2024 as a museum dedicated to women artists.

**Why in the sample:** a genuinely new shape for this dataset's pathway vocabulary — not simply `sell-auction` (the founding collection really was sold, at scale, through a marquee auction house) and not simply `pivoted` (the *institution* did not merely change direction; it deaccessioned its entire founding collection and rebuilt around an unrelated one), but both at once, executed by the same living founder in the same building. This bears directly on H4/H5 (does a maximally coherent, single-thesis collection — classical antiquities and arms/armour, "one of the largest in private hands" — predict institutional permanence?): here it did not, even though the museum itself was popular (a quarter-million visitors) and not obviously financially distressed. It also gives H8 a new texture: Levett, as founder-sole decision-owner, held no constraint on his own founding collection at all, and exercised that total freedom to sell it outright rather than adapt around it — a founder-imposed *absence* of permanence commitment, the mirror image of Judd's [[chinati]] doctrine.

## Narrative
Christian Levett built one of the largest privately held classical antiquities and arms-and-armour collections over roughly 25 years of active collecting (he describes more than 3,000 total purchases across his broader collecting career), opening the Mougins Museum of Classical Art in the small French village of Mougins in June 2011 to display roughly 1,000 of those pieces alongside contemporary art with classical resonances. The museum drew an estimated 250,000 visitors over its 12-year run. In August 2023 Levett closed MACAM as a classical-art museum and consigned the bulk of the collection to Christie's, across six sales spanning New York, London, and online formats, with a pre-sale estimate near £22 million. Rather than close the institution itself, Levett reopened the same building in June 2024 under an entirely new mission: the Female Artists of Mougins Museum (FAMM), reportedly billed as one of the first museums in Europe dedicated specifically to art by women, built around a newly assembled collection. Levett has described the move as "bittersweet" but deliberate — a founder-controlled reallocation of both capital and physical space toward a cause he now prioritizes, not a response to financial distress, community pressure, or any governance conflict located in this run's sources. The case is a clean test of whether collection coherence alone predicts institutional permanence (H4/H5): MACAM's thesis was about as tightly coherent and distinctive as this dataset codes, and it still did not survive, because coherence never bound the *founder's own future choices* the way a locked deed or trust would have. It also complicates a simple reading of `outcome_category`: coded here as `pivoted` because the institution and founder both continue actively operating a public museum in the same space, but a reader focused on the *founding collection specifically* (MACAM's actual antiquities and armour) would code this closer to `closed-dispersed` — a reminder that this dataset's outcome categories track the institution's continuity, not the specific collection's, and the two can diverge sharply within a single founder-controlled case.
