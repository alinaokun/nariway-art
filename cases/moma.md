# The Museum of Modern Art — New York, NY

*Added 2026-08-16 as a coded case. The archetype at the extreme end of the "collectors to public institution" spectrum, and the opposite pole from the [[barnes]]. Quantitative durability fields (true endowment, opex ratio, FTE) are `unknown` pending a 990 pass, flagged for the dataset agent.*

## Coded header
pathway **found-standalone-museum** (three collectors founded a new public institution) · founders **Lillie P. Bliss, Mary Quinn Sullivan, Abby Aldrich Rockefeller** (plus original trustees A. Conger Goodyear, Paul Sachs, Frank Crowninshield, Josephine Boardman Crane) · all **deceased** · **survived_founder YES** (founded 1929, all founders gone by mid-century, thriving ~96 years) · outcome **thriving** · durability **strong** · governance **independent board from founding** (multiple founders + a real trustee board, never founder-sole) · **purpose-built** (53rd St; repeatedly expanded, most recently 2019, Diller Scofidio + Renfro) · coherence **absorbed** (the founding gifts dissolved into a ~200,000-work whole; no founding collection kept intact as a named unit) · decision **collective founders/trustees** · constraints **partial/unusual** (the Bliss bequest, see below).

## Numbers
- Founded **1929**, opened with only a handful of works [MoMA history, primary].
- **1931 Lillie P. Bliss bequest: more than 150 works** (Cézanne, Modigliani, Picasso), the founding collection. Her will conditioned the gift on the Museum reaching **financial stability within three years**; the condition was met and the bequest went on view **1934** [MoMA press / ARTnews, primary-adjacent].
- **The Bliss deaccession clause (the Nariway gold):** her will explicitly **permitted the Museum to SELL her works to build the collection**. Picasso's *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon* was acquired this way. Donor intent deliberately designed for **adaptability**, not frozen preservation.
- Collection today **~200,000 works** [MoMA, secondary/approximate].
- **true_endowment / opex ratio / FTE / attendance: unknown** (need the 990; flag for the dataset agent).

## Why it matters to the report (high thesis value)
1. **The anti-Barnes.** MoMA is the maximal "surrender ownership to a public institution and let the collection dissolve into a larger whole" answer. The [[barnes]] is the maximal "freeze it intact forever by indenture" answer. The two are the endpoints of the [[field-definition|collection-coherence]] spectrum, and MoMA, the flexible one, arguably produced the greater institution.
2. **A strong governance-beats-endowment data point.** Multiple founders and an independent trustee board from day one, never a single-founder control vacuum. The ultimate survivor. Consistent with the report's central finding.
3. **Donor intent designed for adaptability beats donor intent designed for preservation.** The Bliss sell-to-upgrade clause directly supports the first principle *"preserving the collection is not always preserving its value or influence."* This is one of the cleanest illustrations in the dataset.
4. **The three-year financial-stability condition** is an early, deliberate durability-design device worth noting (a founder forcing the institution to prove viability before the gift vested).
