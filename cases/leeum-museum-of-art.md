## Narrative
Samsung's founding family did not simply build a fortune; across three generations — patriarch Lee Byung-chul, his son and successor Lee Kun-hee, and Lee Kun-hee's wife Hong Ra-hee — they built one of Asia's largest private art collections, spanning Korean traditional ceramics and painting through international postwar and contemporary art. In 2004 the family opened Leeum in Seoul, commissioning three separate buildings from three internationally celebrated architects (Jean Nouvel, Rem Koolhaas, Mario Botta) rather than a single unified structure, a design choice that itself signals the museum's dual mission: one wing for Korean tradition, one for global contemporary art, under one institutional roof funded through the family's own Samsung Foundation of Culture (established 1965).

This dataset already holds [[lee-kun-hee-collection]], coded around the historic 2021 gift of 23,181 works from Lee Kun-hee's estate to Korea's national museums, driven by the largest inheritance-tax bill in Korean history and widely credited with reforming Korean tax law to accept art in lieu of cash. What that case could not show on its own is that the donation was never the whole story: Leeum, the family's own standing museum, continued operating throughout and after the donation, holding a separate, undisclosed tranche of the same family's collecting activity. This is exactly the "portfolio of simultaneous dispositions" this dataset's own research program names as the correct frame for how significant collections actually move — retain, donate, and institutionalize all at once, not as a single either/or choice — and Leeum/[[lee-kun-hee-collection]] together are this dataset's cleanest documented instance of that portfolio split from a single family's holdings.

Leeum's own institutional history adds a further, distinct data point to this evidence base's governance cluster. Hong Ra-hee, credited across multiple sources as the museum's chief curatorial and programmatic force, resigned as director in March 2017 after her son, Samsung Electronics Vice Chairman Lee Jae-yong, was indicted (and later convicted and imprisoned) in the Park Geun-hye political bribery scandal — a corporate-governance crisis at the parent conglomerate reaching directly into the museum's leadership, not a funding, succession, or founder-death event of the kind this dataset's H6/H8 cluster usually tracks. The museum's public program visibly contracted through this period; a described "long hiatus" did not end until an October 2021 reopening, explicitly framed in Korean press coverage as a renewed public ambition. Leeum's own survival through this episode — bruised, with a multi-year visible contraction, but never closed or dissolved — sits between this evidence base's cleaner governance-shock survivals (Fredriksen/National Museum of Norway's sustained controversy without closure) and its cleaner governance-shock failures (Terra's board/widow war): a family-linked institution absorbing a serious external reputational and legal shock to its controlling family, without the shock reaching the institution's own legal or financial structure directly.
