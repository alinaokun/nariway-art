## Narrative
UBS is the sample's clearest example of the "active steward" corporate model already named as one shape in [[report-dataset]]'s corporate typology, and it sharpens the typology by supplying the acquirer's-eye view of a merger this sample had previously only seen from the absorbed side. Where [[credit-suisse-ubs-collection]] tracks a distressed parent's collection being folded into something larger, this case is that "something larger": a bank that has run its own collecting program continuously, with no reported crisis, contraction, or disposition event at any point, and enough institutional confidence in the program to open a dedicated free public gallery in its own Manhattan headquarters as recently as 2019 — the opposite trajectory from Deutsche Bank's 2026 retreat from PalaisPopulaire, coded in the same sample.

The collection's own founding story, however, repeats a specific credibility problem this sample has now hit twice. UBS's public marketing traces the collection to 1971 and Donald B. Marron, but Marron's collecting was done as Chairman of PaineWebber, a firm UBS acquired only in 2000 — nearly three decades after the date UBS's own website cites as its founding. This is the same conflation [[credit-suisse-ubs-collection]]'s Gaps section already flagged from the other direction; having it reappear in UBS's *own* marketing materials, not just in secondary press synthesis, upgrades it from "a research gap to resolve" to "a genuine finding about how corporate collections narrate their own history" — a bank's collection history, like a family collection's, accretes through M&A layers (UBS absorbed PaineWebber's collection in 2000, then absorbed Credit Suisse's in 2023), and the institution's own public-facing story compresses that layered history into a single clean founding date that likely isn't accurate. This is directly analogous to the [[report-dataset]] Pattern 8 finding about family collections layering through marriages and generations — corporate collections do the same thing through mergers, and the corporate sub-sample now has two independent cases (this one and Credit Suisse→UBS) showing the same self-narrated-origin problem.
