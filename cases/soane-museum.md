# Sir John Soane's Museum

One-line: Architect Sir John Soane secured a private 1833 Act of Parliament requiring his house and collection be preserved exactly as he left it, free and open forever; nearly 190 years and one statutory-framework replacement later (a 1969 Charities Order), it remains substantially unchanged — the sharpest available disconfirming instance yet of "founder constraints reliably erode," and a second, differently-enforced data point (statute vs. Gardner's forfeiture-to-beneficiary clause) for the "enforceability, not scope, predicts durability" refinement of H8.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]] — the single machine source the export reads. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample — this run's Part D disconfirmation instrument.** [[what-we-now-believe]]'s most recent H8 entries (the thirtieth pass, 2026-09-06, [[isabella-stewart-gardner-museum]]) found a century-long survival of an extremely strict founder constraint enforced by automatic forfeiture to a named beneficiary (Harvard), and explicitly named as its "next test": *"a dedicated search for a second 'forfeiture-to-a-named-beneficiary' constraint design, to see whether Gardner's mechanism is a repeatable pattern or a one-off."* This run searched directly for that second instance and found Soane's Museum — an even older (1837 vs. 1924) and, on one reading, even stricter case (a private Act of Parliament with government trustees and a public Grant-in-Aid, rather than a will's forfeiture clause with a private university as beneficiary). The attempted disconfirmation — that a rich, real-world counter-case to "constraints erode" would eventually surface and *also* show erosion once examined closely, the pattern that broke two of the last five H2 "readily accepted" candidates — failed: no source located this run documents any material loosening of Soane's preservation mandate in nearly two centuries.

## Narrative (sourced)

Sir John Soane, one of the leading English architects of his era (designer of the Bank of England), spent more than three decades continually rebuilding his own townhouse at 12-14 Lincoln's Inn Fields into a combined home, architectural office, and personal museum, filling it with antiquities, architectural fragments, paintings (including Hogarth's *A Rake's Progress* and *An Election* series, plus Turner and Canaletto), and what became one of Britain's most important private collections of architectural drawings — some 30,000 of them, including 57 volumes from Robert and James Adam's own office [secondary: soane.org collections page]. In 1833, four years before his death, Soane took the unusual and deliberate step of securing a **private Act of Parliament** — rather than relying on a will alone — to bind his house and collection: the Act required the property be preserved "as nearly as circumstances will admit" in the exact state Soane left it, and kept open, free, in perpetuity for "amateurs and students" in architecture, painting, and sculpture [primary: UK Parliament historic Hansard record of the 1833 Bill]. This was, in the dataset's own vocabulary, the most extreme available version of `constraints_documented: yes` at the time it was written — arguably stricter even than Barnes's later indenture, since it fixed not just gallery arrangement but the physical fabric of a lived-in house. Soane died in January 1837; a board of trustees the Act itself created took over immediately, and the museum opened to the public that same year. It has now operated under that mandate, or its 1969 statutory successor (The Charities (Sir John Soane's Museum) Order, itself revised several times through 2022 without abandoning the core "preserve unchanged, keep free and open" requirement), for 189 years — through the Blitz (the museum evacuated and protected its collection during WWII, per general institutional history not independently re-verified this run), through the 2012-2015 "Opening up the Soane" restoration project (which this dataset's sourcing frames as *restoring* Soane's own arrangement rather than modernizing past it), and into a 2025-2028 government framework document confirming continued DCMS Grant-in-Aid funding. No source located this run documents Soane's preservation mandate ever being seriously challenged, loosened, or overridden — a genuinely different trajectory from Barnes's own comparably strict indenture, which a Pennsylvania court did eventually override under sustained funding pressure roughly seventy years after Barnes's death. We read the difference as consistent with, and now doubly confirming, the "enforceability, not scope" refinement this dataset's H8 entries have built through Star of Hope (a toothless Attorney General oversight power, unexercised) and Gardner (a self-executing forfeiture clause with a motivated beneficiary): Soane's constraint is enforced by the strongest mechanism yet tested — direct statute, administered by government-recognized trustees, backstopped by ongoing public funding — and it is also the constraint that has held longest and most completely.
