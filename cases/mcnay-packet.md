## A · Identity & pathway spine

- **Collection:** the Marion Koogler McNay collection — French Impressionist/Post-Impressionist/Expressionist + American (esp. watercolors) + New Mexico/Southwest.
- **Collector:** Jessie Marion Koogler McNay (1883–1950); trained watercolorist; oil-inherited fortune.
- **Current form:** McNay Art Museum, San Antonio — 501(c)(3), **EIN 74-1195277**, IRS ruling Oct 1951. [Verified: primary]
- **Pathway (near-linear, single dominant branch):**
  `Private collecting (from ~1913 Armory Show) → in-home art school 1942 (a dry run) → TRANSITION: death 1950 + testamentary bequest (house + ~23 acres + collection + "two-thirds of her wealth" as endowment) → disposition: FOUND A NEW STANDALONE INSTITUTION → founder transition: she died BEFORE the 1954 opening; governance passed to a named self-perpetuating board → OUTCOME: thriving, survived founder 75 years.`
  - *Branch check:* no sale/dispersal/gift-elsewhere branch identified — but whether any works went outside the bequest is **unverified [gap].**
- **Outcome/status:** **Thriving / durable** — net assets ~$100M (FY2025), near debt-free, collection grown to 20,000+ objects. [Verified: primary — 990s]

## B · The collector

- Born Jessie Marion Koogler, Feb 7, 1883, De Graff OH; only child; family to El Dorado KS (1884). Trained watercolorist — *made* art, not only bought it. [Reported: secondary — [TSHA bio](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/mcnay-jessie-marion-koogler); [Kansas Oil Museum bio PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/62505f6e07f85444fa81f2e6/t/626da57f9e62995f935adee2/1651352960252/Jessie+Marion+Koogler+McNay.pdf)]
- **Wealth = oil, inherited (well documented).** Father (Santa Fe RR surgeon) amassed ~3,248 acres of Butler County KS bluestem land by 1908; El Dorado oil came in Sept 1915; by 1935 the Koogler wells had produced 4M+ barrels from 104 wells. Per her father's instruction she **kept personal control of the fortune through all five marriages.** [Reported: secondary — Kansas Oil Museum bio]
- **Five marriages, no children, kept first husband's name:** (1) Don McNay, m.1917, d.1918 Spanish flu — the formative loss; (2) Charles Phillips 1921–25; (3) Dr. Donald Atkinson 1926–36 (mansion built during this marriage); (4) artist Victor Higgins 1937–40; (5) art dealer A.E. Quest 1940–41. [Reported: secondary — TSHA]
- **Why she collected:** converted at the **1913 Armory Show** (Art Institute of Chicago) — "the most thrilling aesthetic experience of her life." Patronized living artists; ran the San Antonio Art Institute in her home from 1942. [Reported: secondary]
- **Stated intent (load-bearing — verify against will):** *"As early as 1942, McNay entertained thoughts of leaving her growing collection… as the beginnings of a museum of modern art."* First director Leeper framed her standard as "the severe standards of quality which governed her taste… enjoying her collection with the same unswerving intelligence with which it was formed." [Reported: secondary; Leeper quote near-primary] · The Wikipedia-attributed "a place of beauty with the comforts and warmth of a home" has **no primary citation [Unclear/contested].**

## C · The collection

- **Focus/coherence:** genuinely coherent modern holding, unusual for its place — Gauguin, van Gogh, Cézanne, Picasso, Matisse, Chagall, Rivera, Rousseau, Soutine, Rouault, Signac, Degas; American watercolors (Cassatt, Homer, Prendergast, O'Keeffe); a distinct Taos/Southwest body (santos, retablos, Pueblo pottery/textiles). First purchase: Rivera's *Delfina Flores*. Dealers Dalzell & Ruth Hatfield shaped the French holdings. [Reported: secondary]
- **Size at death — CONTESTED:** "more than 700 works" (dominant — museum, TSHA bio, Wikipedia) vs. "~200 artworks" as inaugural holdings ([TSHA museum entry](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/marion-koogler-mcnay-art-museum)). Likely: ~200 core paintings within a larger ~700-object gift — **needs the estate inventory.** [Unclear/contested]
- **Cultural opportunity:** repeatedly called **Texas's first museum of modern art** (1954). Before it, there was no regional public venue where Texans could see first-tier European modernism on permanent view. *But the "first in Texas / one of the first in America" superlative is asserted, never argued* — test against Contemporary Arts Museum Houston (1948), Dallas, Fort Worth founding dates. [Reported: secondary; superlative = verify]

## D · The transition (1950 → 1954)

- **Trigger:** her death (pneumonia). Date contested: **April 13** (TSHA, Kansas bio) vs. **April 12, 1950** (museum, Wikipedia). [Unclear/contested]
- **Bequest:** home + ~23-acre estate + collection + **"two-thirds of her wealth" as endowment** — "the largest single gift in the city's history." **Dollar value not found [gap].** [Reported: secondary]
- **The house:** 24-room Spanish Colonial Revival villa ("Sunset Hills"), 6000 N. New Braunfels Ave., by **Atlee B. & Robert M. Ayres.** Completion contested: **1927** (TSHA, Google A&C) vs. **1929** (Kansas bio, museum). Likely design 1927 / finished 1929. She hand-applied stencils, tile, the patio pool. [Reported: secondary]
- **The 1950–54 gap:** four years converting a residence into a museum — per the museum, her will gave "very specific instructions"; the Ayres firm did the work; **~75% of the house was altered** (walls removed for galleries, gallery lighting + fire detection, **six feet of earth excavated for a climate-control basement**), furnishings kept for a "feeling of home." **Opened Nov 4, 1954, inaugural Picasso show.** [Reported: secondary — [Mansion to Museum](https://www.mcnayart.org/blog/mcnay-moments-from-mansion-to-museum-the-metamorphosis-of-mrs-mcnays-home/); [McNay70](https://www.mcnayart.org/mcnay70/)]

## E · Institutional design

- **Legal:** 501(c)(3), NTEE Art Museums; **IRS ruling Oct 1951** (entity recognized ~3 yrs before opening); originally chartered as the **Marion Koogler McNay Art Institute.** [Verified: primary — ProPublica]
- **Buildings:** original Ayres house (see D); **1984** Tobin Library Wing (theatre-arts materials); **2008 Jane & Arthur Stieren Center for Exhibitions**, ~45,000 sq ft — design architect **Jean-Paul Viguier** (Paris) with **Ford, Powell & Carson** (architect of record). 🚩 **NOT Jean Nouvel** — an error in our own tasking; do not propagate. [Verified: primary — Viguier/ArchDaily]
- **Admission:** paid general admission (~$20 adult, secondary) with an extensive sponsored-free program (free Thu eves H-E-B; free 1st Sun; Museums For All; free military/USAA). *Confirm live price before publishing.* [Reported: secondary]
- **Staffing:** founding = director (Leeper) + small staff (count not found); now full departmental structure. **FTE headcount is on 990 Part I line 5 — not yet pulled [gap].**

**Number-hygiene points (the CFO-relevant learning):**
1. **"Total revenue" swings are not donor swings.** The ~$3M/yr gap between named streams and total revenue is realized investment gains + special events. **Contributions** ($4.7–9.4M) is the steady read on donor support; the volatile "total revenue" is investment-driven. Do not read revenue drops as donor collapse.
2. **Earned/gate revenue is tiny (~$0.25–0.70M).** This institution runs on **endowment + contributions**, not admissions.
3. **Near debt-free** (liabilities <1% of assets), so net assets ≈ total assets.
4. **Coverage ratio (derived, upper bound):** net assets ÷ opex ≈ **9.3–9.4×** (FY24/25). ⚠️ Net assets ≠ endowment — they include land, house, buildings (and art may not be capitalized). **True endowment is on Schedule D of the full 990 PDF — not yet line-verified.** Treat 9.3× as an upper bound.
5. **FY2011 anomaly** ($3.2M revenue) — likely a partial-period/reporting quirk; check that year's PDF.

## G · Governance & succession + founder constraints

- **Founding structure:** the will **named 7 trustees as a self-perpetuating board** (fills its own vacancies; no municipal appointment). [Reported: secondary — TSHA]
- **Founder died 1950, before the 1954 opening** → she never governed the operating institution; control passed straight to her named board. Unusual and important.
- **Directors (the succession spine):** John Palmer Leeper 1954–1991 (~36 yrs) → William J. Chiego 1991–2016 (~25 yrs; led the ~$50.8M campaign + 2008 Stieren Center) → Richard Aste 2016–~2022 → **Matthew McLendon**, Director & CEO from Feb 2023 (990 base comp FY25 $302,437). [Verified: primary for current; secondary for history]
- **Governance change:** the closed 7-member board **expanded in 1988** to add up to six elected trustees on four-year terms — a loosening of the original structure. [Reported: secondary]
- **Founder constraints & retained control (as REPORTED — verify against will):** required converting the house per detailed instructions; named the first board; gave detailed endowment-use instructions; retained the domestic "feeling of home" (the house itself as the bound artwork/experience). **Display-arrangement / keep-collection-together / deaccession-limit clauses: NOT FOUND — the central documentary gap.**
- **How constraints changed:** collecting scope broadened far beyond her modern core (now includes medieval/Renaissance, Tobin theatre arts, glass, photography; 20,000+ objects) — implying the original mandate was interpreted well past her focus. *Did this require formal trust modification?* [gap]
- **City vs. trustees:** TSHA says she "gave the city of San Antonio" the estate, yet it has always been a private self-perpetuating trust. **Who holds title? [Unclear/contested].**

## H · Outcome & durability

- **Status:** thriving, near debt-free, net assets >$100M (FY25); collection grown to 20,000–22,000+ objects + Tobin collection + 30,000-vol library. [Verified: primary — 990s; secondary for counts]
- **Attendance CONTESTED:** ~100,000/yr vs ~200,000/yr in secondary sources — **no authoritative figure verified [gap].**
- **Distress/deaccession:** none found — *absence of evidence, not confirmed absence; check AAMD records.* One clear controversy: **2022** — the McNay rescinded a commissioned Sarah Fox puppet-video as off-theme; **five artists withdrew alleging censorship** (Texas Public Radio, Feb 2022).
- **Mechanisms of survival (factual):** endowment + self-perpetuating board established *before* the founder's death; very long director tenures (36, then 25 yrs); a ~$50.8M capital campaign funding the 2008 expansion via donors not debt (consistent with near-zero liabilities).

## L · Decision owner & coordinator (the H7A test)

- **Decision owner = Marion McNay herself, pre-mortem, via her will.** A clean case of the *collector* owning the transition — contemplated from 1942, ran a dry-run art school, and (per the bio) "conceived, planned and perpetuated for any possible contingency, her will covering the tiniest of details… naming the first Board of Trustees." [Reported: secondary — specific]
- **Coordinator = John Leeper + the named trustees + the Ayres firm** stood up the institution across 1954+.
- **→ The H7A implication (see assessment):** here the decision was *not* unowned — the founder owned it thoroughly herself. McNay is the model of the *prepared* founder, not the confused one.
