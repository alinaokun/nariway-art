## Narrative
Alan Medaugh, described in press coverage only as an American businessman, bought his first Hiroshige print on a trip to Japan shortly after marrying, then spent the next fifty years and more than two dozen further trips to Japan refining a single, narrow obsession: the earliest, best-preserved, most vividly colored impressions of Utagawa Hiroshige's woodblock prints, buying scholarly books on the subject as often as he bought prints [secondary: [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/christies-alan-medaugh-hiroshige-collection-auction-1234794665/); [Robb Report](https://robbreport.com/shelter/art-collectibles/private-hiroshige-collection-christies-auction-1238551346/)]. Specialists describe the resulting roughly 700-work holding as ranking among the finest Hiroshige collections ever assembled, distinguished less by breadth than by the exacting quality standard, condition and impression-order, Medaugh applied to every purchase. Christie's began selling the collection in September 2026 across three planned New York auctions; the first, "Hiroshige: Colors of the Four Seasons," on September 15, offered more than 180 prints including a complete run of the late-career series *One Hundred Famous Views of Edo*. No source located this run gives a total pre-sale estimate for all three auctions combined, or explains why Medaugh chose sale over any form of gift, loan, or institutional partnership.

**Relevance to the evidence base.** Medaugh joins [[sandy-lerner-cat-collection]] as a second same-cycle instance of a living, well-resourced collector choosing the plainest possible pathway, a personal auction sale with no foundation, gift, or public installation ever attempted, for a collection that is, by this dataset's own `collection_coherence` measure, about as tightly-focused as anything coded (a single artist, filtered further to a single quality criterion). Read against H4/H5 (distinctiveness predicting public-institution potential), it is a second data point this run for the same finding Lerner supplied: a founder can hold the most distinctive, coherent kind of collection this dataset tracks and still simply sell it, undercutting the assumption that coherence predicts a *pull toward* institutionalization, as distinct from the separate, already-tested question of whether coherence predicts durability *once* public.
