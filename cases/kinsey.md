# Kinsey Collection

One-line: Bernard and Shirley Kinsey's ~700-object private collection of African American art, historical documents and artifacts (1595–present) tours the country as a traveling exhibition — no standalone building — seen by 16M+ people at 40+ venues since 2006, with a small family foundation as its charitable arm and the collection itself privately/commercially owned.

**Why in the sample:** the **no-building, distributed-access** model. Public benefit is achieved by circulation, not by a monument; it isolates the traveling-program pathway and the question of whether a personally-owned collection can institutionalize itself.

## Narrative

Bernard Kinsey (a former Xerox executive) and Shirley Kinsey (a former teacher) began collecting African American art and historical artifacts, ultimately assembling one of the largest private collections of its kind — roughly 700 headline objects ranging from a 1595 baptismal record and a 1773 Phillis Wheatley first edition to paintings and sculpture [source: https://www.gatheringplace.org/kinseycollection | secondary]. Rather than build a museum, they chose circulation: the collection has toured as a traveling exhibition since 2006, appearing at 40+ institutions including the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History and reaching more than 16 million viewers [source: https://news.famu.edu/2025/famu-to-honor-bernard-shirley-kinsey-with-band-hall-naming-oct-16.php | secondary]. The enterprise is deliberately two-part: the collection is owned and toured through the family's KBK Enterprises, while The Bernard and Shirley Kinsey Foundation for Arts and Education (501(c)(3), tax-exempt since 2009) serves as the educational and grantmaking arm [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Kinsey | secondary]. That structure means the public 990 captures only a slice of the whole: the foundation reported just $193,745 revenue, $354,313 expenses and $649,299 net assets in FY2024 — figures that say little about the collection's own value or durability [source: https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/800273378 | primary]. Both founders are living and remain the decision-makers, with son Khalil Kinsey a principal in operations, so the succession model is undocumented [source: https://www.thekinseycollection.com/the-family | secondary]. The model's strength is reach and coherence-as-thesis (a survey of the Black experience, framed as filling the "myth of absence"); its open question is what happens to a personally-owned, tour-dependent collection after the founders [source: https://www.si.edu/exhibitions/kinsey-collection-shared-treasures-bernard-and-shirley-kinsey-where-art-and-history-intersect:event-exhib-905 | secondary].
