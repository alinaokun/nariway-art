## Narrative
Nelly Arrieta de Blaquier was described by The New York Times in 1995 as the owner of Argentina's largest private art collection; she and her husband, Carlos Pedro Blaquier — an industrialist tied to the Ledesma sugar and paper conglomerate — assembled a trove of French Impressionist and Post-Impressionist masterpieces in Buenos Aires over several decades [secondary: [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/sothebys-blaquier-450-million-art-collection-1234794798/)]. Nelly died in 2020; Carlos Pedro died in 2023. Neither founder created a foundation, museum, or any public-facing institutional vehicle for the collection during their lifetimes or at death — the collection simply passed into the family estate.

The eventual sale was delayed by an unrelated legal dispute rather than by any deliberate institutional decision: in 2023, Argentine customs officials fined the family over the export of roughly a dozen paintings — including works by Van Gogh, Monet, Degas, Renoir, Gauguin, and Cézanne — to Luxembourg. That matter was resolved only in July 2026, when authorities waived the proposed penalty, clearing the way for several of those works to be included in Sotheby's November 2026 New York sale [secondary: [Artnet News](https://news.artnet.com/market/blaquier-art-collection-sothebys-2795536)]. The consignment is estimated at roughly $450 million total, led by Van Gogh's *Châtaignier en fleurs roses et blanches* (1890, estimated $150–200M) and a Cézanne from the artist's Harlequin series (estimated to exceed $120M), with works by Degas, Pissarro, and Renoir each projected above $25M [secondary: ARTnews].

**Relevance to the evidence base.** This is a clean, contemporary instance of "sell wins by default, not by design" (see [[what-we-now-believe]]): six years passed between the first founder's death and the sale announcement, and three years between the second founder's death and the announcement, with the intervening period dominated not by any coordinated succession planning but by an unrelated regulatory dispute. No advisor, foundation, or coordinating structure is named anywhere in the sources located this run — the auction house became the default destination once the legal obstacle cleared, exactly the mechanism the belief describes. It is also this sample's first Latin American instance of the deceased-both-founders single-owner auction pathway that [[ganz-collection]] anchors for the US market.
