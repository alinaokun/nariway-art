# Zeitz Museum of Contemporary Art Africa (Zeitz MOCAA)

One-line: German entrepreneur Jochen Zeitz gave his personal collection of contemporary African and diaspora art on long-term loan to a museum built inside a converted 1921 grain silo, funded and owned by Cape Town's V&A Waterfront property company on a 99-year lease; opened 2017, it has weathered a founding-director misconduct scandal (2018), persistent financial-opacity criticism, and its second executive director's death in office (2025), remaining open throughout.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]] — the single machine source the export reads. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample:** the sample's clearest instance of a collector founding a major standalone museum *without* owning the building or bearing full financial responsibility for it — the V&A Waterfront, a for-profit property developer, funded the $38M conversion, retains ownership, and (with Zeitz) has agreed to cover shortfalls for at least a decade. This is a distinct institutional-design shape from every other found-standalone-museum case in this dataset (Crystal Bridges, Glenstone, Chinati), where the founder's own money built and owns the building outright. It also carries real, documented governance friction (a #MeToo-adjacent founding-director scandal, sustained press criticism over financial opacity) that the institution has survived without collapsing — a live test of H6/H8's governance-durability claim under real strain, not just at founding.

## Narrative (sourced)

Jochen Zeitz, who became the youngest CEO of a public German company at 30 when he took over Puma, began collecting contemporary art from Africa and its diaspora in 2002, eventually amassing what is widely described as one of the world's leading collections in the category [secondary: Larry's List]. In 2013 he partnered with Cape Town's V&A Waterfront — the property company that redeveloped the city's harbor district — to convert a disused 1921 grain-silo complex, once the tallest building in Sub-Saharan Africa, into a museum designed by Heatherwick Studio; the V&A Waterfront funded the $38M conversion and retained ownership of the building, granting the museum a 99-year renewable lease, while Zeitz gave his personal collection to the new institution on long-term loan as its founding core [secondary: The Art Newspaper]. Zeitz MOCAA opened September 22, 2017, immediately the largest museum dedicated to contemporary African art anywhere. Governance friction surfaced quickly: founding executive director and chief curator Mark Coetzee resigned in 2018 at a trustees' meeting Zeitz flew in specifically to attend, after museum curators themselves gathered evidence of alleged racial slurs and sexual misconduct against staff [secondary: News24]. The same period produced sustained press criticism — from the Mail & Guardian and others — that the museum was declining to disclose financial information South African nonprofit law otherwise expects of it, a criticism this run could not independently resolve one way or the other for the current (2025-2026) period. The institution has continued operating without interruption through this friction and through a second major leadership transition: executive director and chief curator Koyo Kouoh, who had led the museum since 2019 and had just been named curator of the 2026 Venice Art Biennale, died in May 2025; the museum named Elvira Dyangani Ose — previously director of Barcelona's MACBA — as her successor, with Dyangani Ose taking up the role October 2026, an orderly, publicly announced succession rather than a governance vacuum.
