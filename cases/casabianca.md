## Narrative
Casabianca is the sample's cleanest instance of a pathway the taxonomy has not coded before: a private art collection made publicly accessible through a for-profit hospitality business, with no foundation, no nonprofit registration, and no separation at all between the "museum" function and the family's existing commercial operation. Where every other found-* case in the sample (McNay, Menil, Phillips, even MONA in its own way) creates *some* institutional form distinct from the founder's personal or business assets, Casabianca creates none: the villa is part of the same corporate structure as the De Santis family's hotels, ticketed at €15 like any paying attraction, and explicitly marketed as "a house, not a museum."

This matters for the report in two ways. First, it is a genuinely new answer to the "what becomes of it" question that costs the founder almost nothing structurally to set up, no 501(c)(3)-equivalent filing, no board, no governance apparatus, just opening a few rooms to ticket-buyers, which makes it a strong test case for whether "public access without institution-building" is a viable, low-friction pathway other collector families might copy, precisely the kind of overlooked model [[research-program]]'s Models desk exists to catch. Second, it makes the collection's long-term future entirely contingent on the hotel business's own commercial fortunes and the family's continued interest, with none of the legal permanence (however imperfect) that even a founder-sole foundation like Chinati or Grounds For Sculpture provides. If the hotel group is ever sold, restructured, or the family's priorities shift, nothing in the visible record obligates the collection to remain public, or even together. It is the sample's most extreme version of the "no constraints documented" state (`constraints_documented: open`), because there is no instrument at all to constrain, and no institution to survive its founders.
