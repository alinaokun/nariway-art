## Narrative
Muzej Lah is the sample's clearest test yet of whether the "new-region private museum" pattern the candidate universe has been tracking informally (Almaty, Khao Yai, AMOCA in Cardiff) generalizes outside the Global South and Central Asia into a wealthy-but-culturally-under-institutionalized corner of Europe. Slovenia has no equivalent to Switzerland's or Austria's dense private-museum infrastructure, and the Lahs' choice to commission a major international architect (David Chipperfield, fresh off internationally recognized museum work) for a purpose-built home for a genuinely serious, three-decade collection reads as a deliberate act of regional cultural infrastructure-building, the same public-benefit logic Nariway's own north star names, applied to a place, Lake Bled, better known globally as a tourist postcard than a cultural destination.

The case also adds a structural wrinkle worth tracking as more small European cases are coded: **the legal vehicle is Swiss (Lucerne), not Slovenian**, even though the museum itself, the collection's subject matter (a meaningful share of named artists are Slovenian, notably Zoran Mušič), and the founders' own business base (glassware manufacturing) are all Slovenian. This is the second time this sample has seen a European collection route its formal governance through Switzerland rather than the country the collection and museum actually live in (the first being [[fondation-beyeler]], though Beyeler's museum is itself in Switzerland, unlike Lah's). Whether this is a tax/regulatory-efficiency choice, an estate-planning choice, or simply reflects where Igor Lah's broader business empire (the CEEREF real estate fund, listed in Vienna) is already domiciled is unconfirmed, but it is now a real pattern worth watching: a European collection's *legal home* and its *physical/cultural home* can be two different countries, a distinction the current schema's single `geography` field cannot represent without a note like this one.
