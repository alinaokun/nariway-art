# Charles Dee Mitchell Collection → Dallas Museum of Art

One-line: A Dallas art critic and former Half Price Books executive who spent decades collecting photography and works on paper signed a bequest in 2021 committing his 540-work collection and his own home to the Dallas Museum of Art; both arrived in 2026, exactly as written, with no dispute, no litigation, and the house's sale proceeds funding a namesake endowment — a clean, complete instance of the plan-ahead bequest pathway.

## Narrative (sourced)

Charles Dee Mitchell built his collecting capacity an unusual way: he worked at Half Price Books, the Dallas-based used-book retailer, rising into corporate ranks where stock options he held grew substantially over time, funding an early retirement in 2006 that he devoted to reading, arts patronage, writing, and curation [secondary: Glasstire]. As a critic and curator active in the Dallas art scene, he assembled a personal collection with a distinctive focus on photography — conflict photography in particular — alongside drawings, prints, sculpture, metalwork, ceramics, and other works on paper, by artists including Earlie Hudnall Jr., Dayanita Singh, Chris Marker, Patti Smith, Malick Sidibé, Barbara Rossi, Cecilia Vicuña, Karl Wirsum, Alex Katz, and Gladys Nilsson [secondary: Glasstire, KERA News]. In 2021, four years before his death, he signed a bequest committing the collection to the Dallas Museum of Art; he had already given 50 works by Texas artists to the museum during his lifetime, ahead of the eventual full bequest [secondary: Glasstire]. Mitchell died in November 2025 at age 74 [secondary: KERA News]. In 2026 the bequest was fulfilled as written: 540 works entered the DMA's collection, and Mitchell's own distinctive East Dallas home — itself part of the gift — was sold by the museum, with proceeds directed to a Mitchell-named endowment supporting the museum's works-on-paper collection [secondary: Glasstire]. No dispute, litigation, or contested figure appears anywhere in the located coverage — a rarity in this dataset's bequest cases, most of which surface at least one point of friction (a contested will, a museum's initial reluctance, a family disagreement).
