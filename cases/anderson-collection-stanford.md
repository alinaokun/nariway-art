## Narrative
Anderson gives the sample its second university-partnership case, and it is a genuinely different shape from the first. [[ogden]] is a living founder's regional-canon collection folded into an existing university museum system; Anderson is two now-deceased founders' national-scope postwar collection given its own free-standing, purpose-built building, physically and administratively distinct from Stanford's existing Cantor Arts Center next door. In practice it behaves less like "a gift absorbed into a university" and more like a standalone museum that happens to sit on a university's land and payroll — the university-partnership pathway turns out to hide at least two structurally different sub-models the schema currently treats as one.

The case's most live finding is procedural rather than financial: founding director Jason Linetzky, who had managed the Anderson family's private collection for over a decade *before* the 2011 gift and led the public museum for the entirety of its 12-year life, stepped down in July 2026, with an interim director in place while Stanford runs an external search. This is the first leadership change in the institution's history, arriving right as this case is being coded, and it is a clean instance of a pattern this sample has coded before only in hindsight (Weisman, Pearlman): the founder-connected leadership era ending and a formal institutional process taking over, tested here in real time rather than reconstructed after the fact. Whether the museum's identity (which the founding director himself embodied, having worked directly with the family) persists through an arm's-length Stanford hire is exactly the kind of forward-looking data point [[what-we-now-believe]]'s H6/H8 governance thread is built to track, and it is genuinely open as of this coding.

Financially, Anderson deepens a structural-gap pattern this sample already carries for university-embedded cases: because the collection has no separate legal identity from Stanford itself, no 990-style disclosure applies at the collection level, and the building-cost figure itself is contested ($30.5M reported at the 2011 architect selection vs. $36M reported at the 2014 completion) with no source isolating what portion, if any, the Anderson family paid personally versus Stanford's broader capital-campaign pool. This is a different flavor of "unknown" than [[khao-yai-art-forest]]'s (a jurisdiction's disclosure regime the entity may never have opted into) — here a real, well-resourced institution (Stanford) simply does not disaggregate one museum's finances from the university's consolidated books, a structural opacity by institutional design rather than by absent regulation.
