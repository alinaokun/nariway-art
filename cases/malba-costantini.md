# MALBA (Museo de Arte Latinoamericano de Buenos Aires)

One-line: Eduardo Costantini founded MALBA in 2001 with 200+ donated works of his own; in December 2025, at 78, he personally bought Zurich's 1,233-work Daros Latinamerica Collection and folded it into the museum, roughly doubling its holdings to ~3,000 works ahead of MALBA's 2026 25th anniversary — a living founder actively consolidating a *second* major private collection into his own institution, a pathway variant this dataset had not yet coded.

**Why in the sample:** the sample's first case of a founder-museum's living founder acquiring an *entirely separate* privately assembled collection (Daros Latinamerica, built by the Swiss Daros Foundation/Ruser family) and merging it into his own standalone institution — a genuinely distinct mechanism from the more common "collector donates to an existing museum" pathway this dataset otherwise tracks. Also the sample's clearest non-U.S., non-European Latin American case with a still-living, still-acquiring founder, testing H4/H5 (a museum built for a specific regional/representational gap — Latin American art underrepresented in global institutions — actively growing its distinctiveness rather than just holding steady).

## Narrative
Eduardo Costantini, an Argentine real-estate developer, opened MALBA in September 2001 with more than 200 works donated from his own collection, built specifically around Latin American modern and contemporary art — a category, sources note, that global institutions had historically treated as an adjunct to European or American art history rather than a subject in its own right. The museum has operated continuously for nearly 25 years and, unlike most cases in this sample where the founder's active role ends at founding or at death, Costantini has remained the collection's driving force throughout.

In December 2025, at age 78 and ahead of MALBA's 25th anniversary (September 2026), Costantini personally acquired the **Daros Latinamerica Collection** — 1,233 works by 117 artists, assembled in Zurich by the Swiss Daros Foundation and spanning painting, sculpture, photography, video and large-scale installation from the 1950s through the 2010s — and folded it into MALBA, roughly doubling the museum's total holdings to approximately 3,000 works. This is a distinct mechanism from the sample's more common "collector donates to an existing museum" pathway (e.g. [[fisher-sfmoma]], [[cisneros]]): here, a *living founder of his own standalone museum* is the buyer, using personal capital to acquire a wholly separate, previously assembled European private collection and consolidate it under his own institution's roof, rather than a museum board or curatorial department driving the acquisition. It is functionally a founder-driven merger-of-collections event, executed as a personal purchase-then-gift rather than an institutional negotiation — a variant this dataset's controlled vocabulary does not yet have a dedicated pathway value for, flagged here alongside the Lehman and Michener schema-coverage gaps.

For the report, MALBA is most useful paired with the sample's other Latin American cases as an argument that H4/H5 (distinctiveness and representational gap over raw market value) can *compound* rather than plateau: a museum founded specifically to fill a gap large institutions left open has, a quarter-century later, used its own credibility and the founder's continued wealth to absorb another collection built for the same underserved category abroad, rather than the more common trajectory of a founder-museum's growth flattening after the founding gift. Because Costantini is living and no post-founder governance plan is documented in sources located this run, `survived_founder` is `not-yet-testable` and `constraints_documented` is `open`.
