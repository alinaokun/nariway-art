## Narrative
Museo Poldi Pezzoli is the sample's deepest available test of survival over a genuinely long horizon. It opened in 1881, two years after its founder's death, per a will that both bequeathed the collection and building and, in the same instrument, established the governing rules under which it would operate, an 18-years-before-death lock-in even earlier than [[kreeger-museum]]'s architectural pre-commitment and earlier than [[fondation-beyeler]]'s 28-year Stiftung lock-in, though structurally different: Poldi Pezzoli's founding document handed governance to a standing public-institutional framework from day one, rather than to an independent board the founder himself assembled.

Its one major disruption in 145 years was external, not governance- or funding-driven: British bombing destroyed the roof and much of the interior on August 15, 1943 (the collection itself had been evacuated beforehand), and the museum closed for roughly eight years while the Italian state funded reconstruction, reopening December 3, 1951. That the state itself funded the rebuild, rather than the institution facing a Corcoran-style endowment-erosion dissolution, reflects Poldi Pezzoli's hybrid public/private governance form: a purely family-controlled foundation might not have had a comparable state backstop available.

The case also sharpens H8 (founder constraints and durability) in a specific way the sample hadn't shown before. Poldi Pezzoli's will did impose a binding constraint, his display system had to be maintained "in accordance with the rules of the Brera Gallery." Unlike Barnes's much broader indenture (governance, location, and display all locked by one founder, ultimately requiring a court's cy pres override to survive), this constraint is narrow, display method only, and coexists with 145 years of thriving, unbroken operation. Read together, the two cases suggest H8's real predictive variable may not be "did the founder impose any constraint" but "how much of governance itself the constraint locked" — Barnes locked governance and location; Poldi Pezzoli's will locked only a display convention and handed everything else to a standing public framework immediately. Worth testing further as more historical European house-museum cases are added.

Set beside [[corcoran-gallery]] (dissolved 2014 after 126 years, on an eroded endowment) as the sample's other century-plus case, Poldi Pezzoli is the positive pole: comparable age, comparable house-museum form, and a state-and-hybrid-board governance structure that has so far avoided the endowment-erosion clock entirely, though its own financial durability, no endowment figure or FTE count was locatable, remains genuinely unmeasured by this report's standard tools, an Italian instance of the data-availability finding (Pattern 4) rather than a contradiction of it.
