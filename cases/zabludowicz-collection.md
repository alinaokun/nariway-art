# Zabludowicz Collection

One-line: Finnish-British collectors Anita and Poju Zabludowicz opened a free public exhibition space for their 8,000+-work contemporary art collection inside a converted London chapel in 2007, then closed it after 16 years (December 2023) while the underlying charity remained in healthy financial standing, stating a deliberate pivot toward expanded loans and digital access rather than any funding crisis.

**Why in the sample:** extends this dataset's "building vs. collection" comparison set ([[rubin-museum]], [[cam-raleigh]], [[rauschenberg-foundation-hq]], [[deutsche-bank-collection]]) into the private-foundation population for the first time — every prior case in that set closed or divested a building under some form of financial or operational pressure. Zabludowicz's own 2024 accounts show a real annual surplus (£211,430 retained) in the same year it announced closure, testing whether "a well-funded institution abandoning its building" always signals distress, or can be a genuinely voluntary strategic choice (see Part D disconfirmation note in [[what-we-now-believe]]).

## Narrative
Anita and Poju Zabludowicz began collecting contemporary art in 1994. In 2007 they converted a disused 1870s Methodist chapel in Chalk Farm, north London, into a free public exhibition space for the collection, operated under a registered UK charity, Zabludowicz Art Projects (charity no. 1120067). Over 16 years the space built a reputation for championing emerging and mid-career artists, particularly large-scale installation and video work, and grew the collection to more than 8,000 pieces. In November 2023 the foundation announced it would permanently close the London space that December, framing the decision as a deliberate strategic pivot: expanding "international reach" through increased free-of-charge loans to institutions and a stronger digital presence, rather than maintaining one fixed building. The foundation's most recent available accounts (FY ending 29 December 2024) show total income of £690,136 against expenditure of £478,705, retaining £211,430 that year — a financially healthy position in the same period as the closure, not a picture of the funding exhaustion that has driven comparable building closures elsewhere in this dataset (Rubin Museum, CAM Raleigh). Press coverage at the time also noted that the Zabludowiczs' ties to Israel (Poju Zabludowicz co-founded the pro-Israel lobby group BICOM) had drawn a 2021 boycott/disaffiliation campaign from a group of artists; a foundation spokesperson explicitly denied any connection between the closure and that controversy, stating it was solely about expanding access. This dataset treats that denial as the founders' own stated position, not a confirmed absence of any other factor — the underlying causal question is not resolved by sources located this run.
