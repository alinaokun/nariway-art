## Narrative
Ernst and Hildy Beyeler spent roughly half a century as Basel gallery owners before converting their private collection of classical modernism into a foundation in 1982 — a full 15 years before the Renzo Piano-designed museum building even opened in 1997, and 28 years before Ernst's 2010 death. That sequencing matters: the governance instrument (the Beyeler-Stiftung) predates the institution's physical existence, let alone the founders' deaths, which is the earliest and most durable pre-death lock-in the sample has found so far — earlier than Kreeger's architectural pre-commitment (22 years) and Sid Richardson's foundation charter (12 years).

Ernst Beyeler had no children, so unlike the sample's other thriving deceased-founder survivors (the de Menils, the Phillipses, Electra Havemeyer Webb), there was no family succession available or attempted — the museum instead passed directly to an independent Board of Trustees chaired by industrialist Hansjörg Wyss, whose own foundation now co-funds operations (~SFr1.5M/yr). Sixteen years on, the museum is not merely surviving but actively growing: it welcomed its nine-millionth visitor in 2024, staged a major Matisse retrospective the same year, and is in the middle of a significant physical expansion — a Peter Zumthor-designed addition and a newly public park opening in phases from autumn 2026 through January 2027. For a report built on the wealth-transfer thesis, Beyeler is useful precisely because it shows the *non-family* version of durable succession: a childless founder couple, a foundation locked in decades in advance, and an independent board willing to keep investing rather than merely maintain.
