## Narrative
Benini Sculpture Ranch is a small case, but it fills a specific, real gap in the sample: every art-park case coded so far (Chinati, Pyramid Hill, and Longleaf as a pivot-into-a-park) is a survival or succession story. None shows what it looks like when the *original site itself* doesn't continue — and Benini does, cleanly, without any of the crisis machinery (death, litigation, funding collapse) that drives the sample's other discontinuities. Italian-born artist Benini and his wife Lorraine purchased a 140-acre property near Johnson City, Texas — notably including land with a former Lyndon B. Johnson family-ranch connection — in 1999, and ran it for fifteen years as an open-air sculpture ranch drawing a reported 2,000–3,000 visitors a month. In 2014 they relocated their base of operations to a new, smaller, purpose-built 35-acre site in nearby Marble Falls, opening MuseoBenini in 2015: a 6,500-square-foot gallery and studio complex the foundation describes as one of fewer than 300 single-artist museums in the world.

No source found this session explains why the original ranch ended — whether it was a lease or land-tenure issue tied to the LBJ-family property, the cost and scale of maintaining 140 open acres versus a compact purpose-built gallery, or simply a choice by two aging founders to consolidate. What is documented is that the founders themselves drove the change, remained active afterward, and — as recently as June 2025 — formally incorporated the "Benini Foundation" as a 501(c)(3) public charity, with Benini himself now serving as the foundation's honorary board chair and the museum hosting free public Saturday hours as of fall 2025.

For the report, Benini is most useful as a **founder-controlled pivot while living**, a category the sample already has one example of (Fisher Landau's 2017 Center closure, six years before her 2023 death) but where Benini differs sharply: Fisher Landau's closure led to an eventual estate-driven dispersal with the founder no longer steering it; Benini's founders relocated, rebuilt, and are still actively running a (now-formalized) successor institution. It is also, at n=1, far too thin to generalize from, and its `survived_founder` field cannot yet be tested — both founders are alive. Its main value to the report is as a living-founder case still early enough in its institutional life that succession is entirely open (`constraints_documented: open`), worth revisiting as the founders age, exactly the forward-looking framing the report already applies to Glenstone, Crystal Bridges, and the rest of the living-founder cohort.
