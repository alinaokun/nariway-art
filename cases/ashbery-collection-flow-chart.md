# John Ashbery Collection & Archive (Flow Chart Foundation / Morgan / Beinecke / Harvard)

One-line: poet John Ashbery and his husband David Kermani founded a joint nonprofit, the Flow Chart Foundation, in 1998, nineteen years before Ashbery's 2017 death; after his death, Kermani split the poet's papers, reading library, and personal art collection, mostly gifts from artist friends rather than market purchases, across Harvard, the Morgan Library, and Yale's Beinecke Library, while the Foundation keeps stewarding the couple's Hudson house and its documented legacy.

**Why in the sample:** the sample's clearest test yet of the `spouse`-as-decision-owner value and of H1's actor thesis (a surviving spouse, not a child or advisor, makes the first real disposition decisions), and a genuine **pre-death governance structure predating the disposition by nineteen years** — the same shape as [[pearlman-foundation]] and [[wolf-kahn-foundation]], both also founded roughly two decades before their founders' deaths, now a third independent instance of the pattern. It is also a `hybrid` pathway in the fullest sense this dataset has coded: one collection, deliberately split by *type of material* (papers to a university archive, art on paper to a museum, objects/art to a research library, house/legacy to a dedicated foundation) rather than kept together or sold as one lot.

## Narrative (sourced)

John Ashbery (1927-2017), the American poet, worked as an art critic for three decades and formed close friendships with numerous visual artists over his lifetime, many of whom gave him drawings and works on paper directly [secondary: [The Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/ashbery-collection)]. In 1978 he and his husband, David Kermani, whom he met in 1970, bought a Victorian house on West Court Street in Hudson, NY, which Ashbery filled over a 25-year restoration with paintings, statuary, and memorabilia reflecting his own aesthetic sensibility [secondary: [Flow Chart Foundation, "Created Spaces"](https://www.flowchartfoundation.org/created-spaces)]. In 1998, nineteen years before his death, Ashbery, Kermani, and (per one source) fellow poets Dara Wier and James Tate established the Flow Chart Foundation, named for Ashbery's 1991 book-length poem, as a nonprofit supporting poetry and the interrelationship of the arts [secondary: search-synthesized, single-source on the co-founder detail, flagged for confirmation].

Ashbery died in 2017. Kermani, as his husband and literary executor, distributed the estate's holdings across several institutions rather than keeping them together or selling them: Harvard University acquired Ashbery's papers and reading library in a deal announced January 2019 [secondary: [Harvard Library](https://library.harvard.edu/about/news/2019-01-23/john-ashbery-papers-and-reading-library-acquired-harvard-university)]; the same year, roughly 25 works on paper by Ashbery's artist friends (James Bishop, Joe Brainard, Jane Freilicher, Jean Hélion, Fairfield Porter, Larry Rivers, and others, plus two 19th-century Constantin Guys sheets and Ashbery's own earliest surviving collage) went to the Morgan Library & Museum [secondary: The Morgan]; and a broader "John Ashbery Collection of Artwork and Objects" opened for research at Yale's Beinecke Rare Book & Manuscript Library [secondary: [Beinecke](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/article/open-research-john-ashbery-collection-artwork-and-objects)]. The Hudson house itself was not sold or converted into a physical house museum; instead the Flow Chart Foundation built "Created Spaces," a virtual-reality documentation project reconstructing each room with archival photographs, biographical notes, and audio of Ashbery and Kermani discussing the space [secondary: Flow Chart Foundation]. In 2026 the Morgan mounted "Friends Who Came to See Me: Drawings from John Ashbery's Collection" (May 1-October 25, 2026), the first dedicated public exhibition drawn from the 2019 gift [secondary: The Morgan].

No source located this run indicates Kermani held a Morgan Library trusteeship or prior formal relationship with the Morgan before the 2019 gift, though the collection's placement across three elite Northeastern institutions (Harvard, Yale, the Morgan) suggests Kermani, as a career librarian himself (an MLS from Columbia, author of the first Ashbery bibliography), had strong professional-network access to exactly this tier of recipient institution, a different kind of "insider" advantage than the trustee/board-chair ties this dataset's H2 disconfirmation searches have found elsewhere [secondary, inferential — not confirmed as a decisive factor].
