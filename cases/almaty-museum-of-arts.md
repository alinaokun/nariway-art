## Narrative
ALMA is the sample's first Central Asian case and, alongside Muzej Lah (Slovenia) and the Casabianca villa (Italy) coded this and the prior run, extends the sample's "new-region private museum, living founder" cluster into a fourth geography. What distinguishes it within that cluster is not the art (a now-familiar living-collector, purpose-built pattern already coded for Muzej Lah and Almaty's Southeast Asian and Latin American analogues in the broader candidate universe) but the **succession design stated publicly at opening**: Smagulov's own materials describe an intended transition from founder-sole control toward a mixed governance model that explicitly includes state participation, framed in the museum's own language as turning the institution into "a collective public asset rather than a private endeavour."

This is a genuinely distinct answer to H8 (founder-constraint design predicting durability) from anything else in the sample. Every other found-standalone-museum case's governance trajectory has to be *inferred* from what actually happened, years or decades later (Fondation Beyeler's move to an independent board years before Beyeler's death; Weisman's foundation eventually broadening past founder-sole control). ALMA instead **announces the intended trajectory at the moment of founding**, before any of it has happened — a stated intent, not yet a locked instrument, coded here as `open` rather than `yes`, per the template's rule that stated-but-unexecuted plans are not the same as binding constraints. Whether Kazakhstan's state-participation model actually delivers durability the way an independent Western board does is untestable at this stage and worth returning to in a future run once the museum has operated long enough to test it.

The endowment-fund detail is the case's most citable financial fact and its biggest gap simultaneously: the museum states one exists and frames it as central to public-asset status, but no figure, legal structure, or governance detail for it was located this run, and Kazakhstan's complete absence of a US-style public disclosure regime for nonprofits means this may remain permanently `unknown` rather than merely `unpulled`, the same structural condition already coded for the sample's Swiss, Canadian, and Chinese cases.
