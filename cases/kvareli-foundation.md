## Narrative
Jemal Inaishvili — a Georgian businessman and politician (Deputy Chairman of the Georgian Parliament; former CEO of Poti Sea Port; past president of the Georgian Chamber of Commerce and Industry) described in press coverage as the country's largest collector of contemporary art — has built a purpose-built foundation in Kvareli, his native town in the Kakheti wine region of eastern Georgia, devoted to the preservation, exhibition, and scholarly study of Georgian contemporary art [secondary: [Artforum](https://www.artforum.com/news/kvareli-foundation-for-contemporary-art-opens-in-georgia-1234757728/); [Artdaily](https://artdaily.com/news/199355/Kvareli-Foundation-for-Contemporary-Art-to-open-in-eastern-Georgia)]. The building, designed by the Tbilisi firm Midamo (architects Giorgi Chkonia and Dato Danelia) with the surrounding Caucasus foothills in mind, includes exhibition galleries and spaces for cultural programming; some coverage also describes an attached rooftop and hospitality component, unconfirmed in detail this run. It opens to the public October 31, 2026, with an inaugural exhibition titled "Unveiling Kvareli Collection," running through January 2027.

The case arrives as part of a wider, genuinely new-region pattern this dataset has now tracked across several runs: private, purpose-built contemporary-art institutions opening in countries with no prior museum of the kind, following Almaty's ALMA (Kazakhstan), Tselinny Center (Kazakhstan), Khao Yai Art Forest (Thailand), and Muzej Lah (Slovenia). Unlike most of those, Kvareli's founder holds a public political office concurrently with his role as founder-patron, a governance texture ­— a sitting national legislator as sole controller of a private cultural institution — this dataset has not coded before and cannot yet assess (no conflict, controversy, or public-disclosure requirement specific to that overlap was located this run).
