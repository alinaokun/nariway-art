## Narrative
Kenneth C. Griffin, the hedge fund billionaire and a longtime ARTnews Top 200 collector, quietly placed *Madonna of the Yarnwinder* — a painting attributed on the Met's own wall label to "Leonardo da Vinci and studio" (dated circa 1501–1505, one of fewer than 20 surviving Leonardo-attributed paintings) — on long-term loan to the Metropolitan Museum of Art. The work simply appeared in Gallery 609 of the Met's European Paintings department in spring 2026, with no press release, no wall-text acknowledgment beyond a standard loan credit, and no public confirmation of purchase price, insurance value, or loan term from either the museum or Griffin himself [secondary: [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/met-museum-leonardo-da-vinci-ken-griffin-1234794238/); [Cultured](https://www.culturedmag.com/article/2026/08/04/art-leonardo-da-vinci-met-museum-kenneth-griffin/); [Puck](https://puck.news/newsletter_content/ken-griffins-stealth-leonardo-the-mets-orientalism-show/)]. Trade press, not the museum's own communications, first noticed and reported the loan.

**Relevance to the evidence base.** This is a direct, domestic data point for this run's H6/H8 disconfirmation hunt into whether sole private control can deliver real public access without any institutional surrender (see [[what-we-now-believe]]'s Niarchos/Nahmad entries). At the smallest possible scale — one painting rather than an entire collection — Griffin's loan shows the identical mechanism: full private ownership retained, the work fully recallable at will, public view delivered anyway, and (unlike Wildenstein) no scandal or regulatory shadow located. It also contrasts sharply with this dataset's institution-building cases: no foundation, no board, no press campaign, the lowest-drama and most reversible pathway a major collector can choose, worth holding against [[chinati]] or [[glenstone]]'s far heavier institutional commitments for the same underlying goal (public access to important art).
