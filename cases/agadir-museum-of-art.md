## Narrative (sourced)

Morocco's National Foundation of Museums (Fondation Nationale des Musées, FNM), established in 2011 to manage, preserve, and promote the country's museum heritage, operates a growing network of sites across the country [secondary: Wikipedia]. Moroccan philanthropist and collector El Khalil Belguench has emerged as a repeat donor to this network: in October 2020, on the occasion of the FNM's 10th anniversary, he donated more than 170 works — including European Orientalist artists who had visited Morocco — to constitute the collection of a new museum at Villa Harris in Tangier [secondary: Morocco World News]. In April 2023, a second Belguench gift of 150 works by Moroccan and foreign artists became the founding art collection of the Agadir Museum of Art, inaugurated April 27, 2023 by Head of Government Aziz Akhannouch, in a building previously used as a municipal heritage/ethnographic museum and renovated as part of the 2020-2024 Agadir Urban Development Program [secondary: lejardinauxetoiles.net; Wikipedia]. The museum is run as a partnership between the Agadir City Council, the Wilaya of the Souss-Massa Region, and the FNM, and continues to display existing ethnographic holdings (jewelry, carpets, pottery, ceramics) alongside the donated art collection [secondary: Wikipedia; excursion/tourism sourcing cross-checked]. No source located this run places Belguench on the FNM's own governing board or in any other formal role within it, distinguishing this case's donor relationship from the closer institutional ties this dataset has documented in comparable "readily accepted" cases elsewhere (e.g. [[mathaf-arab-museum-modern-art]], coded this same run, where the donor holds a governance seat in the receiving body). We read this as a modest, second data point — after [[rosenwald-collection-nga-loc]] — that a government cultural authority with an independent, non-ticket-revenue funding base can absorb a private donor's gift without requiring a prior institutional relationship; the pattern here is also structurally distinct from the Mellon/Rosenwald "repeat gift into one named institution" shape, since Belguench's repeat giving spans *two different sites within one national network* rather than one museum, a variant this dataset's H2 chain had not yet observed.
