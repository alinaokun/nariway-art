## Narrative
Fondation Cartier directly sharpens Pattern (per Batch 13's [[report-dataset]] note) on the **found-foundation-without-independent-capital** variant that [[fondazione-prada]] introduced to this sample. Prada was coded as a fourth funding architecture alongside personal-endowment-funded (Beyeler, Chinati, Glenstone), postal-clerk-salary-funded (Vogel), and corporate-collection-owned (the bank cases): an institutionally independent public museum with a real founder-decider, funded by an ongoing corporate sponsorship relationship rather than a fixed endowment. Fondation Cartier is now a **second, older case with the identical shape**, and it changes the read of that pattern from "one contemporary luxury-house example" to "a recurring corporate-foundation model, tested across four decades." Where Prada's Guidance Committee (2023) was coded as the first documented step away from pure founder-sole control, Cartier shows what four decades of that same funding architecture looks like when it works: continuous operation since 1984, an increasingly ambitious sequence of purpose-built venues (Jouy-en-Josas → Raspail 1994 → Place du Palais-Royal 2025, each larger and more architecturally prominent than the last), and no distress signal of any kind found this run, despite depending the entire time on Cartier's (and since the 1988 merger, Richemont's) continued willingness to fund it — precisely the dependency this sample has not yet seen tested by a downturn, the same caveat flagged for Prada.

Because Fondation Cartier predates Fondazione Prada by roughly a decade (1984 vs. 1993) and predates Prada's own public venues by even longer (Cartier's Raspail building opened 1994, seventeen years before Prada's first Venice venue in 2011), it also reframes the "corporate-foundation-to-public-museum model at scale" thesis [[candidate-universe]]'s §8 row already names: Cartier, not Prada, is this sample's true origin case for the model, and Prada's own Guidance Committee move in 2023 can now be read as a 30-years-in institutional maturation step of a kind Cartier itself may or may not have already made at a comparable age (Cartier's own board/governance detail beyond Perrin's continuous presidency is `unknown` this run, a gap worth closing before drawing that comparison further).

The 2025 relocation is also a clean, live instance of Pattern (building-vs-collection) this sample has tracked informally: does a building change the institution's mission, or just its container? Every account found this run frames the move as an *expansion* of the existing "living creation" mission Perrin set at founding in 1984 — the new building is explicitly described as enabling more configurations of the same commissioning-and-exhibition program, not a pivot to a new thesis — and the inaugural exhibition, "Exposition Générale," is explicitly framed as a 40-year retrospective of the *existing* collection and mission rather than a fresh start. This reads, provisionally, as a "building changed, mission didn't" case, the inverse of what a distressed institution's building change (e.g., di Rosa's campus sale, coded elsewhere in the sample) usually signals; worth revisiting once post-relocation attendance and programming data accumulate, since a €225–245M construction outlay for a sponsorship-funded (not endowment-funded) institution is also, by construction, a large one-time bet on Richemont's continued willingness to fund Fondation Cartier at that level going forward.

Finally, the Foundation's founding sequence — 1984, three years *before* the 1987 French law that formalized corporate-sponsorship foundations, with Perrin himself reportedly influential in that 1987 reform — is worth flagging next to the corporate self-narrated-founding-date pattern this sample already tracks from the UBS/Credit Suisse and PaineWebber cases. There, the finding was that corporate collections compress a layered, M&A-driven history into a single, cleaner founding date than the record supports. Fondation Cartier's puzzle runs the other direction: the *legal category* that would define this type of institution did not yet exist when the institution itself was founded, meaning the 1984 founding date is likely accurate but the institution's exact legal form at founding, versus its form after the 1987/1990 statutory reforms, is itself an open question this run could not resolve.
