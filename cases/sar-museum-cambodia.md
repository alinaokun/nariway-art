## Narrative

SAR – Museum of Cambodian Modern Art opened in April 2025 in downtown Siem Reap, near the Old Market and the river, as Cambodia's first museum dedicated exclusively to the country's modern art. It was founded by Hor Chakriya, a Cambodian art enthusiast and curator, in collaboration with Pierre-André Romano, a French art collector and long-term Cambodia resident. The two spent years researching and locating paintings from Cambodia's modern era — spanning independence through the Khmer Rouge period, when much of the country's art was lost, destroyed, or scattered abroad — and worked to bring dispersed works back to Cambodia. The museum opened with more than 100 works by over 50 Cambodian artists across roughly 700 square metres on two floors, and announced in July 2026 that it was entering a new phase of development — additional galleries, expanded research and acquisition programs — ahead of a formal grand opening.

This case fills a real, previously undocumented geographic gap in this dataset's Southeast Asia coverage and is a clean instance of H5 (small collector-founded institutions filling representational gaps large museums leave open): unlike most of this dataset's founder-museum cases, SAR was not built around one wealthy individual's pre-existing personal collection, but around a deliberate, multi-year repatriation and preservation project aimed at recovering a national art history that had nowhere else to live. It is also, like [[witness-collection-vietnam]], an instance of the field's cross-border-collector shape — one of its two founders is not Cambodian — though unlike Witness Collection, SAR is building toward a fixed public home in the country whose art it collects rather than staying a touring, homeless model.
