# Cultural Museum of African Art (The Eric Edwards Collection)

One-line: Eric Edwards, a retired AT&T executive and Brooklyn native, spent nearly 50 years assembling roughly 3,000 African art objects spanning all 54 African countries — once valued at approximately $10 million — and opened them to the public in November 2023 as a nonprofit museum in Bedford-Stuyvesant, the neighborhood where he grew up, backed partly by a $1 million New York State grant.

**Why in the sample:** a genuinely overlooked model this dataset had not yet coded — a living, non-marquee, first-generation individual collector (not a mega-donor or a corporate/institutional founder) opening a small, geographically and culturally specific museum in an underserved urban neighborhood rather than a wealthy suburb or cultural capital. Also the sample's first African-American individual-collector museum case, and a fresh H5 (geographic/representational gap) test: the museum's own framing is explicitly about telling African history "that hasn't been told in the present educational system," inside a historically Black neighborhood with no comparable prior institution.

## Narrative
Eric Edwards grew up in Brooklyn and began collecting African art in the early 1970s, acquiring objects through personal and professional travel, private sales, auctions, and relationships with artists, artisans, gallery owners, and royal families across the continent over nearly five decades. By the mid-2010s the collection — roughly 3,000 items representing all 54 African countries and spanning some 4,000 years of history — was housed in his own Clinton Hill apartment and estimated at approximately $10 million. Edwards incorporated the Cultural Museum of African Art as a nonprofit in 2014 and received IRS 501(c)(3) recognition in 2015, then spent years raising funds toward a permanent public home, including a Kickstarter campaign and, eventually, roughly $1 million in New York State funding secured with the help of Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie and Assemblymember Stefani Zinerman. A groundbreaking ceremony at Restoration Plaza in Bedford-Stuyvesant — the neighborhood Edwards grew up in — drew more than 350 attendees in October 2022, and the museum opened to the public on November 18, 2023, occupying roughly 7,000 square feet on the complex's second floor. The organization's most recent available Form 990 summary (FY2024) shows a small operating base still finding its footing: $111k in revenue against $125k in expenses, with only $78.2k in total assets against a $10.4 million collection value — the kind of scale mismatch this dataset's `primary_friction: funding-gap` field is built to flag, common among newly opened, founder-led museums before an operating endowment or steady earned-revenue base develops.
