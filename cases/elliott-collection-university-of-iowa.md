# Owen and Leone Elliott Collection (University of Iowa Museum of Art / Stanley Museum of Art)

One-line: A Cedar Rapids couple's condition-of-gift offer — build us a museum and we'll give you the collection — was accepted readily by a public university in 1962, funded by more than 2,000 outside donors, and the resulting institution has survived a catastrophic flood, a 14-year displacement, and a full rebuild without ever losing the founding collection — coded specifically as this run's Part D disconfirmation instrument, testing whether H2's "the receiving end is closing" reading holds against a second institution type (a university) beyond the central-bank case tested last run.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample:** the prior run's H2 disconfirmation entry ([[botero-donation-banco-de-la-republica]], 2026-09-06) named its own "next test" explicitly: find a second institution-type example of ready, intact acceptance — "a university, a national library, or a sovereign cultural authority" — beyond the one central-bank case found so far, to see whether that pattern generalizes past Latin America and past a donor whose fame might be doing unmeasured work. This case answers that directly: the University of Iowa, a large public research university with its own pre-existing non-art revenue base (state appropriation plus tuition, unrelated to museum ticket or membership income — the same structural feature the Banco de la República finding named as the operative variable), accepted the Elliotts' offer outright and built a museum specifically to house it, no insider tie to the university located in sourcing beyond the gift itself.

## Narrative (sourced)

In 1962, Cedar Rapids business owners Owen and Leone Elliott made the University of Iowa a definitive offer: their collection of roughly 1,200 objects — paintings and works on paper by modernists including Picasso and Gabriele Münter, plus antique silver and jade — would go to the university on one condition, that a dedicated museum be built to house it alongside the university's own existing and future art holdings [secondary: Stanley Museum of Art history page, University of Iowa Libraries]. The university accepted, and more than 2,000 individual and business donors contributed toward construction of a new building, designed by New York architect Max Abramovitz in a Brutalist style, which opened in 1969 as the University of Iowa Museum of Art [secondary]. The museum went on to acquire other major works, most famously Jackson Pollock's 1943 "Mural," which by the 2010s was insured for $140 million [secondary: Omaha World-Herald]. In June 2008, a historic Iowa River flood inundated the university's arts campus; the museum's collection, including "Mural," was evacuated ahead of the flood and relocated to the Figge Art Museum in Davenport, Iowa, beginning a 14-year displacement during which "Mural" itself underwent conservation at the Getty and then toured internationally (Peggy Guggenheim Collection Venice, Deutsche Bank KunstHalle Berlin, Museo Picasso Málaga, and others), seen by more than 2.7 million people [secondary: Iowa Now, Nelson-Atkins]. The rebuilt museum reopened as the UI Stanley Museum of Art on August 26, 2022, with "Mural" returned to permanent display [secondary: Iowa Now]. No source located this run describes any prior institutional or personal tie between the Elliotts and the University of Iowa predating the 1962 offer — their own archival finding aid describes them simply as "Cedar Rapids business people and collectors," with no alumni status or trusteeship confirmed either way.
