# Fondation Zinsou

One-line: A living French-Beninese founder built a free contemporary-art foundation and museum from scratch in Benin, explicitly to give a first museum-going experience to a local audience that had never had one — a clean H5 case (filling a real representational gap, in a country and a public with essentially no prior encounter with a contemporary-art institution) and this run's first coded case from Benin, the dataset's sample African country beyond Nigeria, South Africa, Morocco, and the UAE.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]] — the single machine source the export reads. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample.** Every prior African case in this dataset (MOWAA, Yemisi Shyllon, Barjeel, Standard Bank) sits in Nigeria, South Africa, or the Gulf. Fondation Zinsou closes a real, previously-flagged geography gap (candidate-universe.md's open-threads note, carried across the last several runs: "a second Africa case beyond Nigeria/South Africa/Morocco/UAE") with a genuinely distinct shape again: a young, still-living founder building deliberately *for* an audience with no prior museum-going habit, rather than for an existing collector class — closer in spirit to [[khao-yai-art-forest]] or [[muzej-lah]]'s "new-region" pattern than to the corporate or Gulf cases already coded on the continent.

## Narrative (sourced)

Marie-Cécile Zinsou, a French-Beninese art historian born in 1982, founded the Fondation Zinsou in Cotonou in 2005 [secondary: Wikipedia; Art Basel, "Meet the collectors: Marie-Cécile Zinsou"]. Her father, Lionel Zinsou, a French-Beninese banker and later Prime Minister of Benin (2015–2016), is publicly associated with the family's philanthropic commitment to the Foundation, though the museum and its collection are run and publicly fronted by Marie-Cécile as president [secondary]. In 2013 the Foundation opened the Musée d'Art Contemporain de Ouidah, described in its own materials as the first dedicated contemporary-art museum of its kind in Benin [secondary: fondation-zinsou.org].

The Foundation's stated mission is distinctive within this dataset: rather than building around a pre-existing private collection acquired for personal reasons and later made public (the McNay/Barnes/Glenstone pattern), Zinsou has described the project as explicitly audience-first — introducing contemporary African art, including work by artists such as Jeremy Demester, Joël Andrianomearisoa, and Ibrahim Mahama, to a Beninese public with little or no prior museum-going tradition [secondary: momaa.org; Art Basel profile]. She received the Praemium Imperiale young-artist grant in Japan in 2014 in recognition of the Foundation's work [secondary].

This case is coded at `record`-to-`expanded` depth from WebSearch synthesis only; no direct fetch of Beninese or French filings was attempted this run (WebFetch remained `EGRESS_BLOCKED`, see [[report-dataset]]).
