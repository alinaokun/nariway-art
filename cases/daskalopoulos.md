## Narrative
Dimitris Daskalopoulos, the Greek industrialist and collector, began building the D.Daskalopoulos Collection in 1994 around a single thesis — the human body as a source of creativity and a vessel of existential and social struggle. In April 2022 he announced that "over 350 works by 142 artists" would be gifted across four museums in three countries: 140 to EMST in Athens, 110 to Tate, and around 100 jointly to the Solomon R. Guggenheim in New York and the Museum of Contemporary Art Chicago. The Guggenheim/MCA grouping is the case's distinctive mechanism: a **shared-ownership** gift meant to force ongoing collaboration between two institutions in caring for and presenting the same works — a governance innovation rarely seen at this scale. The gift is accompanied by a curatorial network — a dedicated post at Tate, a shared post spanning the Guggenheim and MCA Chicago, and curatorial support at EMST — so the donation transfers not only objects but the staffing to keep them in circulation and scholarship. Like the Cisneros dispersal, the model deliberately avoids a founder museum, betting that embedding the collection inside four permanent public institutions is more durable than a single building. Because the works enter (or are co-owned within) permanent collections, their public life does not depend on any Daskalopoulos institution surviving him. Daskalopoulos remains living, so long-run durability is not yet testable, but the structure is designed to be self-sustaining.
