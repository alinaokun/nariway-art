# Cookie Factory (Denver)

One-line: real-estate developer and collector Amanda Precourt converted a condemned Denver fortune-cookie factory into a free, noncommercial contemporary art space, opened May 2025, and deliberately declined to form a nonprofit at all — no board, no fundraising, no donor competition.

**Why in the sample:** the sample's clearest current instance of "the eponymous museum is losing share to lighter models" (Pattern 3) taken to its logical extreme — not a lighter *nonprofit* model (a foundation, a loan program) but no institutional wrapper whatsoever. Precourt is not choosing this because a heavier structure is unaffordable (her family's wealth comes from oil-and-gas patriarch Jay Precourt); she is choosing it on stated philosophy ("I don't feel that fundraising against other organizations is the right way to go to create change"). A live, still-forming case for H6/H8: it stress-tests whether "governance design" as this dataset codes it even applies to an institution its founder has deliberately kept outside any governance structure at all.

## Narrative (sourced)

Amanda Precourt, a Denver-born real-estate developer (AJP Realty & Design) and the daughter of oil-and-gas entrepreneur Jay Precourt, converted a former fortune-cookie factory in Denver's Baker neighborhood — reportedly condemned before her purchase — into a free, public contemporary-art space that opened in May 2025 [secondary: [Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2025/05/23/new-art-center-debuts-in-an-old-denver-fortune-cookie-factory/); [5280](https://5280.com/inside-denvers-cookie-factory/)]. Paris-based curator Jérôme Sans serves as artistic director; Precourt's partner, exhibiting artist Andrew Jensdotter, is director of exhibitions [secondary: [Frieze](https://www.frieze.com/article/jerome-sans-denvers-new-cookie-factory-art-space)]. The roughly 5,000-5,700-square-foot gallery, plus a sculpture garden and video-screening rooms, is free and open to the public Wednesdays 4-7pm and by appointment [secondary: [Cultured](https://www.culturedmag.com/article/2026/01/16/art-amanda-precourt-cookie-factory-denver/); [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/entertainment/former-fortune-cookie-factory-cracks-future-wide-open-in-denvers-baker-neighborhood-with-new-business-venture)].

The governance choice is the case's most citable feature: multiple outlets independently report Precourt deliberately declined to form a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, eschewing a board and "donor competition" in favor of curatorial independence — the space is privately funded and not dictated by fundraising or profit, and art is not for sale through the gallery [secondary: Cultured; Colorado Sun]. Precourt's own stated rationale: "I don't feel that fundraising against other organizations is the right way to go to create change" — she would rather collaborate with other institutions on events than compete with them for donors [secondary: Cultured].

A widely circulated figure describing Precourt's personal net worth (~$5M, via a low-quality SEO aggregator site) could not be corroborated by any reputable source this run and is **not** coded as fact; her family wealth is instead evidenced qualitatively (her father's energy-sector fortune, her own real-estate development practice) [secondary, low confidence on the specific figure].
