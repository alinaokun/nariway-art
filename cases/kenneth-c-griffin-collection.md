# Kenneth C. Griffin Collection

One-line: Citadel founder Ken Griffin has built one of the most valuable private art collections in the US, from a $300M de Kooning to a Leonardo, and makes it public entirely through long-term museum loans that follow him from Chicago to Florida rather than through a museum of his own.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample:** a pure instance of the "public life through loans, no building" pathway at the very top of the market, a living-collector counterpart to [[broad]] and [[art-bridges-foundation]] but with no foundation or venue at all, only personal loans to institutions where Griffin holds a donor or trustee relationship. The 2026 Met Leonardo loan, made with no press release, signals a move from postwar and contemporary trophies into Old Master territory (Artnet framed it as "big ambitions"), and is the headline event that prompted this record.

## Notes and cautions (verification)
- **Confirmed holdings** (each price/year sourced): de Kooning *Interchange* ($300M, 2015, from the Geffen Foundation); Pollock *Number 17A* ($200M, 2015, same $500M two-work deal); Jasper Johns *False Start* ($80M, 2006); Basquiat *Boy and Dog in a Johnnypump* (>$100M, 2020); Cézanne *Curtain, Jug and Fruit Bowl* (~$60M, 1999); Richter *Abstract Picture (599)* ($46M, 2015). Rothko, Lichtenstein, Ryman, Degas, and Monet works are named in loan reporting (prices not all public).
- **Non-art cultural property** (confirmed, kept in the internal record, referenced lightly in the public origin): first-printing U.S. Constitution ($43.2M, 2021, Sotheby's; lent to the National Constitution Center); a first-edition Bill of Rights; the "Apex" Stegosaurus fossil ($44.6M, 2024).
- **Deliberately excluded as unverified** (per research flags): a Jasper Johns *Flag* (only *False Start* is confirmed); "Stan" the T. rex (a different buyer; do not attribute to Griffin); a Gutenberg Bible (no source). The Norton 2018 donation figure is reported inconsistently ($16M vs $20M) so no figure is stated. The Met Leonardo loan term ("two years") is reported but unofficial, so it is omitted from the public fields; the acquisition price and date are not public.
- Public access is via loans credited to the "Kenneth C. Griffin Collection"; the **Kenneth C. Griffin Charitable Fund** is his philanthropic vehicle, not a collection-holding entity. His MoMA ($40M, 2015) and Whitney relationships are donor/board ties, not collection loans.
