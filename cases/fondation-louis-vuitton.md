## Narrative (sourced)

Bernard Arnault, chairman and CEO of LVMH, announced plans in 2006 for a dedicated contemporary-art building in Paris and commissioned Frank Gehry, who described Arnault's own involvement as critical to every round of design and revision [secondary: ArchDaily, Gagosian Quarterly]. The building, a shell-like structure of twelve glass sails set in the Bois de Boulogne's Jardin d'Acclimatation, opened to the public October 27, 2014, as a fondation d'entreprise — a corporate foundation under French law, sponsored by LVMH and its subsidiaries, with a stated mission to encourage and promote contemporary artistic creation in France and internationally [secondary: Wikipedia]. Since opening it has mounted a series of major loan exhibitions alongside its own growing permanent collection, drawing on LVMH's scale to secure partnerships (with the Morozov family's Russian modern-art holdings, the Courtauld Institute, and others) that a smaller private foundation could not attempt. Unlike this dataset's individual-collector cases, Fondation Louis Vuitton's durability rests on the ongoing commitment of a public, shareholder-owned conglomerate rather than one person's private wealth — a distinct governance shape from Fondazione Prada (privately held by the Prada family) or Fondation Cartier, worth tracking as LVMH's own corporate fortunes or leadership eventually change.
