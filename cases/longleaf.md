# Longleaf Art Park / Berkowitz Contemporary Foundation

One-line: Hedge-fund manager Bruce Berkowitz, after abandoning a planned Miami museum in 2021, is building the 15.5-acre Longleaf Art Park (opening 2026) inside a St. Joe Company development to permanently house Richard Serra's monumental *Passage of Time*; the Berkowitz Contemporary Foundation (a 990-PF operating foundation with ~$82.3M net assets) funds it, his daughter runs it, and day-to-day operations are outsourced to the Cultural Arts Alliance of Walton County.

**Why in the sample:** a **pivot** case — a founder who tried the standalone-museum pathway, walked away, and re-routed to an art park operated by a local arts nonprofit. It tests the branched/abandoned-plan dynamic and the single-work-anchored art-park form.

## Narrative

Bruce Berkowitz, founder of Fairholme Capital Management and chairman of the publicly traded St. Joe Company, first tried the standalone-museum pathway: a 45,000-sq-ft Berkowitz museum was planned for Miami's Edgewater in 2014, cleared municipal opposition by 2015, then was abandoned in early 2021 [source: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/06/20/bruce-berkowitz-art-park-richard-serra-florida-panhandle-real-estate | secondary]. He re-routed to an art park: announced June 2024, the 15.5-acre Longleaf Art Park sits inside St. Joe's Watersound Origins development and will be anchored by Richard Serra's monumental *Passage of Time*, housed in a purpose-built pavilion by OLI Architecture designed with the artist [source: https://sowal.com/story/the-st-joe-company-announces-longleaf-art-park-at-watersound-origins | secondary]. The park is funded by the Berkowitz Contemporary Foundation, a private operating foundation (990-PF, IRS ruling 2014) that reported $82.3M in net assets and $10.4M of new contributions in FY2024 against just $0.74M of charitable disbursements — a war chest being built ahead of opening [source: https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/463390328 | primary]. Governance is family-controlled: Bruce founded the foundation and his daughter Chloe Berkowitz runs it [source: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/06/20/bruce-berkowitz-art-park-richard-serra-florida-panhandle-real-estate | secondary]. Crucially, day-to-day operations are contractually delegated to a local nonprofit, the Cultural Arts Alliance of Walton County — an outsourced-operations model rather than a founder-staffed institution [source: https://www.culturalartsalliance.com/post/the-st-joe-company-announces-the-longleaf-art-park-at-the-watersound-origins-community | secondary]. Access is planned to be free and public, situating the park as a civic amenity woven into a real-estate development [source: https://sowal.com/story/the-st-joe-company-announces-longleaf-art-park-at-watersound-origins | secondary]. With opening slated for 2026, durability is not yet testable — but the abandoned Miami museum makes this a rare documented case of a founder switching pathways.

- **The 111x net-assets/opex ratio is a false durability signal** — expenses are grantmaking, not museum operations; do not chart it against operating institutions without the pre-opening flag.

- **Operations outsourced** — the survive-the-founder question is unusual here: the founder-family funds it but a third-party nonprofit operates it, decoupling money from control.
