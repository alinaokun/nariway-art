# Hilma af Klint Foundation

One-line: The foundation established in 1972 to steward pioneering Swedish abstractionist Hilma af Klint's estate has run for over 50 years under a governance rule requiring both family chairmanship and an Anthroposophical Society board majority — a structural echo of the artist's own spiritualist intent — until a 2023-2026 lawsuit pitted the family chairman, invoking that spiritualist mandate against commercialization, against four fellow board members and the Foundation's own CEO over a proposed David Zwirner gallery partnership; a settlement reported in May 2026 ended the litigation without a confirmed public resolution of the underlying Zwirner deal.

## Narrative (sourced)

Hilma af Klint (1862-1944) is now widely credited as among the earliest abstract painters in Western art history, though her abstract work was largely unseen publicly during her lifetime and, per her own reported wishes, for roughly two decades after her 1944 death [secondary, widely reported across af Klint scholarship]. Her family established the Hilma af Klint Foundation in 1972 to hold and manage her artistic estate, under statutes that name the head of the af Klint family as the Foundation's "natural chairman" (with power to appoint a different family member instead) and require that a majority of its 3-5-member board belong to the Anthroposophical Society, the spiritualist movement that shaped af Klint's own late work [secondary: Hilma af Klint Foundation, "About the Board"]. The Foundation ran without major public conflict for roughly half a century, financed by its own revenues and private support, while af Klint's international reputation grew substantially, including a major 2018-19 Guggenheim retrospective that became one of the museum's best-attended shows. In December 2024, a proposed representation partnership between the Foundation and mega-gallery David Zwirner — which would have given Zwirner a formal role overseeing sales and exhibitions of af Klint's work — was approved by four of the Foundation's five board members but opposed by the fifth: Erik af Klint, the artist's great-grandnephew and the Foundation's chairman since 2023, who called the deal a "hostile takeover" that risked commercializing af Klint's legacy against her own spiritualist intent [secondary: Hyperallergic, Artnet News]. In 2023, Erik af Klint had filed suit accusing the Foundation's four other trustees and its CEO of pursuing improperly authorized commercial deals, including NFTs and an immersive digital experience built from af Klint's work. David Zwirner publicly rejected the characterization, describing instead "a power struggle within the board... a standoff between the four board members and one board member who is trying to sabotage them" [secondary: Artnet News]. The dispute continued into 2025, with reporting describing af Klint's legacy as being "at risk of being locked away." A settlement was reported by the Foundation itself in a page titled "The Conflict within the Hilma af Klint Foundation is over," dated around May 2026: the chairman and board member Juhani Selvani reached agreement on the Foundation's future direction, including continuing to make af Klint's works known through exhibitions, and the Board reached "a common understanding regarding the interpretation of the Foundation's statutes" [secondary, WebSearch-surfaced snippet of the Foundation's own page — WebFetch was `EGRESS_BLOCKED` against hilmaafklint.se this run, so the settlement's full text could not be directly read]. Whether the Zwirner partnership itself proceeded, was formally abandoned, or remains unresolved as part of that settlement was **not confirmed** by any source located this run.
