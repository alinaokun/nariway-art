# Museo Soumaya

One-line: A living telecom billionaire's foundation-owned, free-to-the-public museum — coded specifically as this run's Part D disconfirmation instrument, testing whether extreme personal wealth and a critically-derided lack of curatorial coherence still produce a durable, thriving public institution.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample:** closes Mexico's remaining gap alongside [[museo-jumex]] and would be this dataset's clearest test of whether H4 ("distinctiveness/coherence predicts durability better than market value") survives an extreme counter-case — a museum whose own critics have repeatedly attacked its lack of curatorial thesis and uneven quality, yet which has run continuously, free, for over three decades on a single billionaire's ongoing personal funding, with no endowment structure of the kind this dataset's other durable cases (Menil, Phillips, Beyeler) rely on.

## Narrative (sourced)

Carlos Slim Helú, the Mexican telecommunications magnate, established Fundación Carlos Slim in 1986 and opened the first Museo Soumaya building at Plaza Loreto in December 1994, naming it for his wife Soumaya Domit, who died in 1999 [secondary: Wikipedia, Fundación Carlos Slim]. The museum's landmark second building, a Fernando Romero-designed tower clad in over 16,000 hexagonal aluminum tiles at the Plaza Carso development, opened March 29, 2011 [secondary: Mexico News Daily]. Both buildings offer free admission year-round, funded entirely by the foundation with no reliance on ticket revenue or government subsidy [secondary]. The collection spans more than 30 centuries — European Old Masters, Mexican colonial and modern art, Mesoamerican objects, one of the largest Rodin sculpture holdings outside France, and Impressionist works — reported at over 66,000 objects, though this figure has not been independently corroborated against a primary catalogue [secondary]. Since opening, the museum has drawn sustained, specific criticism from independent architecture and art critics for lacking a coherent curatorial thesis: reviewers writing in Glasstire (2012), DailyArt Magazine, and the New York Review of Architecture have each separately described the collection as overwhelming, poorly contextualized, and uneven in quality despite its scale and famous-name attributions [secondary]. No funding crisis, governance dispute, or closure threat of any kind was located across 32 years of continuous operation — durability here appears wholly decoupled from the curatorial coherence question this dataset's H4 hypothesis treats as a predictor.

## Gaps / contradictions

- No founding-endowment or ongoing operating-budget figure was locatable anywhere in secondary sourcing — an unusually complete financial opacity for an institution of this scale and public profile, itself a data point about how little transparency a wholly founder-funded (rather than endowed) museum can operate under indefinitely without apparent consequence.
- The collection-size figure (66,000+) appears consistently across tourism and museum-directory sources but traces to no single verifiable original count; treat as a widely repeated, unconfirmed estimate.
- Whether the persistent curatorial criticism has had any effect on visitation, funding, or governance was not established this run — durability and reputation appear to be running on two entirely separate tracks here, worth a closer look in a future pass.
