## Narrative
Rebecca Hoffberger founded what became the American Visionary Art Museum in 1989, and opened it to the public in a converted Baltimore whiskey warehouse in 1995, built specifically around self-taught, untrained "visionary" artists the mainstream art world had largely ignored [secondary: Wikipedia, AVAM]. The museum went on to be designated by Congress as America's national museum and education center for this category of art, giving it a form of institutional legitimacy unusual among single-founder museums in this dataset — closer to the Souls Grown Deep-style "distinctiveness fills a real gap" pattern than to a personal-monument model. Hoffberger served as executive director and primary curator continuously for 33 years, stepping back from the full-time director role in 2022 [secondary: Baltimore Fishbowl].

Her chosen successor, Jenenne Whitfield — previously president and CEO of Detroit's Heidelberg Project — became only the museum's second director in its history, taking the role in September 2022. Whitfield departed "effective immediately" in September 2023, roughly a year into the job, with subsequent reporting (AFRO American Newspapers) describing the exit in terms that suggest it was not entirely voluntary [secondary: CBS Baltimore, AFRO]. AVAM operated for roughly 18 months before naming Ellen Owens, then director of the Castellani Art Museum at Niagara University, as its next director; she assumed the role in June 2025 and, per an August 2026 Baltimore Banner profile, is now working to establish the museum as more of a "regular presence in people's lives," language that itself signals a stabilization effort still underway [secondary: The Baltimore Banner, Baltimore Magazine].

For H6/H8, AVAM is a genuinely live case rather than a settled one: a founder who held sole *operating*, if not sole *legal*, control for over three decades stepped back, and the two directorships since have visibly struggled to establish continuity — one lasting barely a year. This is not yet a Terra-style collapse (the museum remains open, financially strained but operating), but it is a real-time instance of exactly the turbulence H6/H8's "founder's disease" mechanism predicts immediately after a long sole-operator tenure ends, worth a return visit in future runs to see whether Owens's tenure stabilizes the pattern or a fourth director follows.
