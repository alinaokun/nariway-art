## Narrative
Alice Walton, daughter of Walmart founder Sam Walton, built Crystal Bridges as a survey museum of American art in her hometown of Bentonville, Arkansas, opening in 2011 in a purpose-built Moshe Safdie complex set in about 120 acres of Ozark landscape with public trails, and endowed for free general admission by Walmart. She drove the collecting personally and aggressively — buying Asher Durand's *Kindred Spirits* for a reported ~$35M in 2005, acquiring a Gilbert Stuart Washington and Rockwell's *Rosie the Riveter* — and several acquisitions drew national controversy at the *selling* institutions, notably the Eakins *Gross Clinic* rescue by Philadelphia and the court-approved sale of a half-share in Fisk University's Stieglitz collection over the Tennessee attorney general's objection. The financial base is exceptional and comes in two layers that must not be conflated: the Walton Family Foundation's 2011 founding gift of $800M (split operating/acquisitions/capital), part of a ~$1.2B 2010 commitment; and the museum's own FY2024 Form 990, which reports ~$2.05B in net assets against ~$96M in expenses — a ~21.4x coverage (upper bound) that reflects accumulated gifts, the collection, buildings, and investment appreciation over thirteen years, not the endowment alone. Governance is family-controlled, and the 2021 handoff of the board chair from Alice to Olivia Walton is an early, within-family continuity signal, though the founder is living and true succession remains `not-yet-testable`. The institution is unambiguously thriving — more than five million cumulative visitors and a ~$150M Safdie expansion opening in June 2026 — with essentially no documented friction to itself, its risk concentrated (like Glenstone) in founder-dependency and eventual succession rather than money. Crystal Bridges anchors the "capital-abundant, founder-living, succession-untested" cluster: distinctiveness aside, its durability question is governance over time, and its constraints remain deliberately `open`.
