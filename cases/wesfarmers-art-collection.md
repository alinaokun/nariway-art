# Wesfarmers Collection of Australian Art

One-line: An Australian conglomerate has built and retained a 1,000-plus-work, single-country-focused corporate art collection continuously since 1977 through committee, not founder, decision-making — a genuine contrast case for how a collection can compound for nearly five decades with no individual collector, founder-death risk, or institutional-independence question at all.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]] — the single machine source the export reads. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why in the sample:** fills this dataset's Australia/Oceania corporate gap (the sample's only prior Australian case, [[mona-museum]], is a private-individual founder-museum) and adds a genuinely different governance shape to the `origin: corporate` sub-population: unlike the bank-embedded collections in this sample (UBS, Deutsche Bank, HSBC, Standard Bank, Monte dei Paschi), Wesfarmers is not a financial institution and the collection was never framed as client-facing brand marketing at founding — it began as one general manager's internal pitch to the board on cultural and investment grounds, and has been managed by a dedicated curator with no reported founder-figure ever attached to it. It tests H6's governance-durability claim at an extreme this dataset otherwise lacks: a collection with **no individual decision owner at any point** in its history, retained and grown by ordinary corporate governance for 49 years with no closure, sale, or crisis of any kind documented.

## Narrative (sourced)

Wesfarmers began as a Western Australian farmers' cooperative in 1914 and grew into one of Australia's largest listed conglomerates, spanning retail, chemicals, and industrials. In 1977, a company general manager made the internal case to the board that acquiring high-quality Australian art was both a sound investment and a statement of corporate culture; the board approved the purchase of an Elioth Gruner pastoral impressionist scene, the collection's first work [secondary: Wesfarmers company history]. Unlike this dataset's individual-collector cases, no single person is credited as the collection's founder or guiding sensibility at any point in its subsequent history — acquisitions have been managed by a dedicated curator (Helen Carroll, named in current company materials) operating within ordinary corporate approval processes. The collection has grown to more than 1,000 works by over 300 artists, deliberately restricted to Australian art across two-plus centuries, including a significant First Peoples/Indigenous Australian art component [secondary: Wesfarmers company website]. It has never been housed in a dedicated museum: works are displayed across Wesfarmers offices and other corporate locations, with a rotating selection on permanent public display at the Perth Concert Hall, and the company runs an active loan and touring program to Australian state galleries and international institutions, plus a public online catalogue and app. No sale, deaccessioning event, funding crisis, or governance dispute involving the collection was located in this run's research — a striking contrast to nearly every other corporate case in this dataset (Lehman's forced 2010 liquidation, Enron's bankruptcy-driven dispersal, Credit Suisse's collection absorbed into UBS after a 2023 forced merger), none of which shares Wesfarmers' uninterrupted, board-governed continuity through nearly five decades and multiple economic cycles.
