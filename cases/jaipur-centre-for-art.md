# Jaipur Centre for Art

*Coded to [[case-template]]. WebFetch was tested once against theartnewspaper.com and returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` (consistent with the environment note); every fact below is WebSearch-snippet synthesis of art-press and design-press coverage (The Art Newspaper, Wallpaper*, ANI, Whitewall, Harper's Bazaar India, Serenade, ASEF culture360, Robb Report India), tagged `secondary` throughout, plus general reference material on Indian nonprofit legal forms. One-line: Sawai Padmanabh Singh, the young head of the former royal house of Jaipur, and art advisor Noelle Kadar co-founded a 2,600-sq.-ft contemporary-art exhibition space inside the still-lived-in City Palace, opening 22 November 2024 with a curated show of blue-chip Indian and international artists; less than two years in, it runs a rotating exhibition program, an artist residency, and a free public film series, with no public collection, endowment, or financial disclosure of any kind located.

## Narrative (sourced)

Jaipur Centre for Art opened on 22 November 2024 as a joint project of Sawai Padmanabh Singh — informally styled "Maharaja of Jaipur," a title with no constitutional standing since India's 1971 abolition of princely recognition, but real social and economic weight as head of a family that still occupies and substantially controls the City Palace — and Noelle Kadar, a Rhode Island School of Design-trained art advisor with roughly two decades in India's contemporary-art scene, including as International and VIP Relations Director at the India Art Fair and as artistic director of Jaipur's Sculpture Park from 2018 to 2020 [secondary: [ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/hh-maharaja-sawai-padmanabh-singh-of-jaipur-amp-contemporary-arts-specialist-noelle-kadar-launch-jaipur-centre-for-art-at-city-palace-in-november2420240828180224/); [Robb Report India](https://www.robbreportindia.com/home-and-design/art/noelle-kadar-jaipur-centre-for-art-city-palace-artwork)]. The venue is small — 2,600 sq. ft — carved into a wing of the City Palace complex, which otherwise functions simultaneously as a partial family residence, a much older palace museum (the Maharaja Sawai Man Singh II Museum Trust, established 1959, an entirely separate governing body from JCA), and a heritage-hotel and event-rental operation generating the family's other revenue [secondary: [Museu.MS](https://museu.ms/museum/details/17787); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/magazines/style/luxury/article/3092273/polo-palaces-and-fashion-billionaire-indian-royal-family-who)]. Padmanabh Singh has framed the project as continuity rather than departure: "My family has long been a part of the contemporary conversation — this is a natural step" [secondary, quoted via ANI/multiple outlets].

The inaugural exhibition, "A New Way of Seeing," curated by Peter Nagy, opened with eight artists spanning Indian and international rosters — Anish Kapoor, Hiroshi Sugimoto, Sean Scully, Alicja Kwade, Dayanita Singh, L.N. Tallur, Tanya Goel, and Manjunath Kamath [secondary: [ASEF culture360](https://culture360.asef.org/news-events/india-new-space-jaipur-centre-for-art-with-a-focus-on-dialogue-in-contemporary-art/); [Serenade](https://serenademagazine.art/jaipur-centre-for-art-inaugurates-with-a-new-way-of-seeing/)]. Programming has continued on a roughly seasonal cycle since: "(IN DIA)LOGUE, Tradition & Transformation," a collaboration with Whitewall curated by Kadar, ran 3 May–3 June 2025 with Indian artists including Biraaj Dodiya, Thukral & Tagra, and Raghav Babbar; "Non-Residency," with Rajiv Menon Contemporary, ran 9 August–8 September 2025, tied to JCA's own artist-residency program (participants reported included Melissa Joseph, Suchitra Mattai, Anoushka Mirchandani, and Nibha Akireddy); and "Salt Lines," by the artist duo Hylozoic/Desires and previously shown at Mumbai's Dr. Bhau Daji Lad Museum, ran 3 July–9 August 2026 [secondary: multiple, including [Whitewall](https://whitewall.art/art/whitewall-and-jaipur-centre-for-art-unveil-in-dialogue-a-new-vision-for-indian-art/), [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/News/salt-lines-brings-indias-forgotten-colonial-salt-barrier-to-jaipur)]. From 2025, JCA has also run a free public film program, "Artists' Cinema" — its second edition (3 July–9 August 2026, curated by Dr. Shwetal A. Patel) presented more than 150 films and moving-image works in partnership with Art21, the British Council, the Goethe-Institut, the Film Heritage Foundation, and the Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art Sri Lanka [secondary: [abirpothi](https://www.abirpothi.com/film-programme-artists-cinema-jaipur/)]. Taken together, this is a consistent, internationally networked exhibition program sustained across roughly twenty months — evidence of real institutional momentum, but still short of the operating history the sample's `outcome_category` vocabulary requires to call anything beyond `emerging`.

No source located this run states JCA's legal form, funding structure, budget, or any financial figure. This is where the case earns its place in the sample as a disclosure-regime marker: India has no equivalent of the US Form 990. A registered Public Charitable Trust would file with a state Charity Commissioner; a registered Society would file with a state Registrar of Societies; a Section 8 nonprofit company would file audited accounts with the central Ministry of Corporate Affairs — three genuinely different regimes, none centrally and freely searchable the way ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer makes US 990s searchable, and **none confirmed this run to apply to JCA specifically**, because it is not established which (if any) of these forms JCA has taken, or whether it instead operates informally as a family-run program inside the existing, separately governed 1959 Museum Trust or as an unincorporated project funded directly from family wealth (the same "form-unconfirmed" pattern this sample's [[khao-yai-art-forest]] file documented for Thailand). Padmanabh Singh's and the wider Jaipur royal family's personal wealth is separately and heavily reported in lifestyle/business press (figures ranging from roughly $700M to $2.8B across sources), but that is founder-level wealth reporting, not an institutional filing, and is deliberately not coded into any of the case's financial fields.

One further piece of context, kept out of the coded header because it does not clearly concern JCA itself: the wider Jaipur royal family has been engaged in long-running, well-documented property disputes over other family holdings (reported in Indian press across the 2010s–2020s, involving Padmanabh Singh, his mother Diya Kumari — herself Rajasthan's Deputy Chief Minister — and other family branches). No source found this run ties that litigation to the City Palace or to JCA specifically, but it is a reminder that "who actually controls this family's cultural assets" is contested terrain in this family more broadly, which is relevant background for any future primary-verification pass on JCA's own governance.
