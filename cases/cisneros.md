## Narrative
Patricia Phelps de Cisneros and her late husband Gustavo Cisneros built, from the 1970s, one of the world's foremost collections of Latin American art, spanning colonial-era works, mid-century geometric abstraction, and contemporary practice. Rather than found a Cisneros museum, Patricia Phelps de Cisneros chose deliberate dispersal into existing institutions: in 2016 she gave MoMA roughly 102 works of geometric abstraction and endowed a dedicated research institute there for the study of art from Latin America. In January 2018 the Colección Patricia Phelps de Cisneros announced a further gift of "more than 200 works" by 91 artists from 22 countries to six museums across the Americas and Europe — MoMA (90 works), the Reina Sofía in Madrid, the Blanton at UT Austin, MAMBA in Buenos Aires, MALI in Lima, and the Bronx Museum — all as gifts joining permanent collections, not loans. The strategy is a wager that embedding Latin American art inside canonical Western institutions does more lasting cultural work than concentrating it in a single founder building. Because the works enter permanent collections, the model's durability does not hinge on a Cisneros institution surviving its founder — a structural contrast with the museum cases. Gustavo Cisneros died in December 2023; Patricia Phelps de Cisneros remains active, and the dispersed works are already in public hands.
