## Narrative (sourced)

Sheikh Hassan bin Mohammed bin Ali Al Thani began building a collection of modern and contemporary art from the Arab world, and its historical connections from North Africa to Asia, in the early 1990s — initially housing it across two private villas in Doha's Madinat Khalifa neighborhood while he and early advisors developed the idea of what a dedicated Arab-perspective art museum could look like [secondary: Mathaf.org.qa "About Us"]. The collection later broadened to include 20th-century artists across the wider Middle East, North Africa, and Arab diaspora, plus objects — including pre-Islamic works from ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt — that Sheikh Hassan considered formative influences on Arab modernists [secondary: Mathaf.org.qa]. In 2010, the collection opened to the public as Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art, in a former school building within Qatar Foundation's Education City, converted by French architect Jean-François Bodin — the first institution of its kind in the region [secondary: Mathaf.org.qa; Wikipedia]. The collection, now numbering more than 9,000 works, is co-owned by Qatar Museums and Qatar Foundation. Sheikh Hassan himself holds senior roles in both bodies (Vice Chairperson of Qatar Museums; Cultural Affairs Advisor at Qatar Foundation), a structural overlap this dataset's H2 "readily accepted" hunts have consistently found whenever a candidate first looks like a clean arm's-length acceptance and then does not survive closer inspection — the sixteenth, twentieth, and other prior passes each found a comparable undisclosed or disclosed insider tie. Mathaf is coded here primarily as a geography and institution-type breadth case (Qatar; a national cultural authority as co-owner) rather than as a disconfirming instrument, with the insider caveat stated plainly so a future reader does not mistake it for the still-open "genuinely cold sovereign-authority acceptance" test.
