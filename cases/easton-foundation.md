# The Easton Foundation (Louise Bourgeois)

One-line: Louise Bourgeois established The Easton Foundation in 1984, 26 years before her 2010 death, and bequeathed it her Chelsea home, an adjacent townhouse, and a substantial body of her own work; led since her death by Jerry Gorovoy, her studio assistant and closest professional collaborator of more than three decades (not a family member), the Foundation is now in its 42nd year with $168M in total assets (FY2024) and no documented governance disruption — a second, artist-originated instance (after [[chinati]]) of a founding-moment permanence doctrine, and a fresh test of `decision_owner` succeeding to a non-family professional collaborator rather than a spouse, child, or independent board recruited after death.

## Narrative (sourced)

Louise Bourgeois established The Easton Foundation in 1984, granted charitable status that April, a full 26 years before her own death at age 98 in 2010 [secondary: GuideStar]. This places the Foundation's founding-instrument lock-in earlier than all but [[fondation-beyeler]] (28 years pre-death) in the sample, and earlier than [[chinati]]'s comparable artist-driven permanence doctrine, itself established by Donald Judd during his lifetime. Unlike Beyeler, whose succession ran to a childless founder's independent, non-family board, Bourgeois's own succession plan ran to a different kind of non-family figure entirely: Jerry Gorovoy, who worked as her studio assistant and closest professional collaborator for more than three decades, became President of The Easton Foundation on her death and remains in that role fifteen years later, with no documented dispute, litigation, or governance crisis of the kind that struck [[terra]] on a comparably sudden founder-sole transition [secondary: Garage Museum, Hauser & Wirth]. On her 2010 death, Bourgeois bequeathed her Chelsea home and an adjacent townhouse directly to the Foundation, which now uses the buildings as its offices and as the home of the Louise Bourgeois Archive, a study center and residency open by appointment to curators and scholars, alongside a sculpture garden and small public exhibition space presenting original archival materials [secondary: My Art Guides, theeastonfoundation.org]. A further governance data point: Maggie Wright, on staff since 2009 with a focus on the Archive, was named Foundation Director in 2018 — a second leadership appointment under Gorovoy's presidency, suggesting an institution now capable of adding professional staff depth rather than remaining a single-successor operation. Financially, WebSearch-surfaced aggregator data (not a direct 990 pull) puts the Foundation's FY2024 total assets at $168M, against $7.89M in reported revenue and $2.86M in expenses the same year [secondary] — a net-assets-to-opex ratio near 59x that almost certainly reflects Bourgeois's own artwork held at market value on the Foundation's balance sheet, the same private-operating-foundation measurement caveat Pattern 10 already flags for [[broad]], [[glenstone]], and [[thoma]], not a genuine 59-years'-cushion of true endowment. The case's clearest contribution to the dataset is the `decision_owner` question: Gorovoy fits none of the schema's current values cleanly (not `spouse`, not `children`, not a recruited `independent-board`, not a hired `art-advisor` in the usual commercial sense) — he is closer to a chosen artistic executor, a distinct successor shape the current vocabulary undercounts alongside the civic and corporate gaps Pattern 6 already names.
