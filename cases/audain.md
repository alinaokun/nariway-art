# Audain Art Museum

One-line: Vancouver homebuilder Michael Audain and his wife Yoshiko Karasawa built a $35M CAD purpose-built museum in Whistler (opened 2016) to house their ~200-work private collection of British Columbia art, funded by the Audain Foundation, on a 199-year, $10 municipal land lease, with a separate endowment foundation still capitalizing.

**Why in the sample:** a living-founder, purpose-built, single-region museum with the durability question wide open — the endowment is being built *after* the doors opened, so it tests whether a founder can pre-fund survival before he dies.

## Narrative

Michael Audain (b. 1937), chairman of Polygon Homes and a fifth-generation British Columbian, spent decades with his wife Yoshiko Karasawa assembling a collection focused entirely on the art of British Columbia — from rare 19th-century First Nations masks through some two dozen Emily Carr paintings to contemporary work by Jeff Wall, Stan Douglas and Rodney Graham [source: https://canadianart.ca/features/whistler-wonders-michael-audain-builds-museum/ | secondary]. Rather than gift the collection to an existing institution, the couple built a standalone 56,000-sq-ft museum designed by Patkau Architects in Whistler, chosen for its natural setting, transit access and year-round 2.7M-visitor tourist base [source: https://canadianart.ca/features/whistler-wonders-michael-audain-builds-museum/ | secondary]. Construction (~CAD $35M) was funded by the Audain Foundation, and the municipality leased the land for 199 years at a symbolic $10 [source: https://canadianart.ca/features/whistler-wonders-michael-audain-builds-museum/ | secondary]. The museum opened 12 March 2016 with roughly 200 donated works, now grown to about 300 [source: https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3475154 | secondary]. Governance sits with a Board of Trustees under the Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act, but the founder and his foundation remain the driving force [source: https://audainartmuseum.com/governance/ | inferred]. A separate Audain Art Museum Foundation was created in 2015 explicitly to build the permanent endowment that will fund operations "in the years ahead" — meaning the durability capital is still being raised rather than pre-loaded [source: https://audainartmuseum.com/aam-foundation/ | secondary]. Audain has directed over CAD $100M to BC arts institutions overall, including a $100M gift to the Vancouver Art Gallery, so the museum is one node in a large living-donor program [source: https://vanmag.com/city/arts-and-culture/whistlers-new-art-gallery/ | secondary]. The survive-the-founder test cannot yet be run: the founder is living and the endowment is unquantified.
