# Wolf Kahn Foundation & Archives

One-line: painters Wolf Kahn and Emily Mason set up a joint foundation in 1998, nineteen years before Mason's 2019 death and twenty-two before Kahn's 2020 death; the foundation split by family line in 2022 and, in 2026, opened its first dedicated archive space under a non-family professional president, one year ahead of Kahn's centennial.

**Why in the sample:** the sample's cleanest **artist-origin** (not collector-origin) foundation, and a second pre-death-structure case alongside [[pearlman-foundation]] — but where Pearlman's disposition took 70 years to fully resolve, the Kahn/Mason foundation was operating grant programs, exhibitions, and archival work continuously from the year it was founded, with no dormant gap between structure and activity. Also a live test of H6: governance passed not to family members but to a career arts-administrator (Ellen McCulloch-Lovell, former Vermont Arts Council director and Clinton White House staffer), a non-family professional leadership model this dataset has not yet coded elsewhere.

## Narrative (sourced)

Wolf Kahn and Emily Mason, both painters, married in Venice in 1957 and in 1998 established a joint philanthropic foundation to formalize support for the arts during their own lifetimes — a genuinely pre-death structure, like [[pearlman-foundation]], but one that began operating immediately rather than sitting dormant for decades [secondary: [Art New England](https://artnewengland.com/the-wolf-kahn-foundation-refines-its-focus/); [Miles McEnery Gallery](https://www.milesmcenery.com/news/the-wolf-kahn-foundation-the-emily-mason-alice-trumbull-mason-foundation)]. Mason died in 2019, Kahn in 2020. In 2022 the joint foundation was formally split into two separate entities along family lines — the Wolf Kahn Foundation, and the Emily Mason | Alice Trumbull Mason Foundation (covering Mason and her own mother, painter Alice Trumbull Mason) — a division that lets each family's artistic legacy be managed and funded independently rather than as one combined program [secondary: same sources]. The Wolf Kahn side is led by Ellen McCulloch-Lovell as president, a career arts administrator with no family relationship to Kahn — she previously ran the Vermont Arts Council, served as chief of staff to Senator Patrick Leahy, and held posts in the Clinton White House on arts and humanities policy [secondary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_McCulloch-Lovell)]. In August 2026, one year ahead of Kahn's October 2027 centennial, the Foundation opened its first dedicated public-facing space at 134 West 26th Street in Manhattan — an archive and research center cataloguing and preserving letters, early studies, lecture materials, and photographs documenting Kahn's life and process [secondary: [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/wolf-kahn-foundation-opens-new-space-in-nyc/); [Artforum](https://www.artforum.com/news/wolf-kahn-foundation-has-new-space-in-manhattan-1234757745/)]. The Foundation also runs an active grants program (one snippet cites $255K distributed to 69 grantees in 2024, though it is unclear whether this pre- or post-dates the 2022 split cleanly) [secondary: fundsforNGOs].
