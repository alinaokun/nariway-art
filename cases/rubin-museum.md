## Narrative
The Rubin Museum is the sample's cleanest test yet of a question [[deutsche-bank-collection]] raised at corporate scale: when an institution retreats from its building, is the building or the collection the thing that actually stopped working? Deutsche Bank answered as a healthy for-profit parent with no public disclosure obligations. Rubin answers as a mid-large, well-capitalized (reportedly $150M+ endowment), living-founder nonprofit museum — a much closer structural match to the report's core case type (McNay, Menil, Phillips, Glenstone) than any corporate collection is, which makes the comparison sharper. Twenty years after opening in a converted Chelsea department-store building, the museum's leadership chose to sell that building and dissolve the fixed-venue model entirely, reorganizing around traveling exhibitions, institutional partnerships, loans, scholarship, and digital access — explicitly framed as a way to reach *more* people than a single 17th Street address ever could, not as a retreat forced by inadequate funds.

This adds a needed fourth shape to the "building vs. collection" question the sample already tracks via CAM Raleigh, the Rauschenberg Foundation HQ, and Deutsche Bank: a **living-founder, non-distressed, single-collection museum choosing to give up its own building entirely**, as opposed to a distressed regional museum (CAM Raleigh, funding-strained), an artist-legacy foundation divesting excess real estate while keeping its actual program intact (Rauschenberg), or a for-profit corporate parent with no comparable public-benefit obligation (Deutsche Bank). Because Rubin is a 501(c)(3) museum with a confirmed EIN (22-6799567) and a reported nine-figure endowment, it is also, unlike Deutsche Bank, a case where the report's standard net-assets-to-opex methodology could eventually produce a real, comparable durability figure once WebFetch is unblocked — making this a high-value primary-verification target, not just a WebSearch-only curiosity.

The Rubin case also sharpens a distinction worth carrying into the 50-case pattern set: `outcome_category: pivoted` currently covers institutions as different as Neue Galerie (merging *into* a larger institution), Deutsche Bank (a corporate parent narrowing footprint), and now Rubin (a solo-standing nonprofit choosing to have no fixed venue at all while remaining fully independent). These are not the same durability story, even though all three currently share one coded value — worth flagging for a future schema refinement once more `pivoted` cases accumulate.
