# Fresno Metropolitan Museum of Art and Science

One-line: A coalition of Fresno civic leaders raised $5.5M to open a combined art-and-science museum in 1984; after a renovation-loan default during the 2008-2009 financial crisis, it closed permanently in January 2010 and its 3,218-object collection was auctioned by Sotheby's and Bonhams to pay creditors — a second, independently-sourced instance of a genuinely independent-board institution failing on pure financial exhaustion, not founder-death or governance vacuum.

## Narrative (sourced)

Beginning in 1978, a coalition of Fresno civic leaders explored creating a regional museum for the San Joaquin Valley; between 1981 and 1985 the community raised more than $5.5 million to open the museum in the historic downtown Fresno Bee Building, and it opened April 8, 1984, becoming one of the largest museums between San Francisco and Los Angeles [secondary: tfaoi.org, Wikipedia]. The museum operated under an independent nonprofit board for over two decades with no single controlling founder, family, or corporate parent — the closest governance shape in this dataset to Corcoran's own "genuine independent, self-perpetuating board" description. In August 2005 it began an extensive interior renovation, financed by loans, and reopened November 13, 2008 — directly into the 2008-2009 financial crisis. Monthly expenses outpaced revenue, the museum defaulted on its renovation loans, and the board voted to close permanently, effective January 5, 2010; board president Stewart Randall announced the closure was "regrettabl[e]" but necessary [secondary: ABC30 Fresno]. Unlike Corcoran, which negotiated a multi-institution split of its collection to two major receiving institutions (the National Gallery of Art and the Smithsonian), Fresno's closure resolved through a straightforward forced liquidation: 3,218 objects from the collection were divided and sold through Sotheby's and Bonhams, netting roughly $1.8 million toward creditor repayment, with additional objects sold directly to interested parties [secondary: Wonderful Museums, corroborated by Artforum's period coverage of the sale]. Note: **the Fresno Metropolitan Museum is a distinct institution from the still-operating Fresno Art Museum** (formerly the Fresno Arts Center) — no source located this run shows any merger or institutional relationship between the two beyond sharing a city and, confusingly, an overlapping "arts museum" identity; this distinction is stated explicitly here to avoid a conflation error a less careful research pass could make.
