# The Mark Rothko Foundation

One-line: An artist-estate foundation that, rather than building a single Rothko museum, deliberately dispersed more than 1,000 of the artist's works across museums worldwide before dissolving — a clean bucket-3 artist-estate distribution, with the National Gallery of Art as the anchor recipient and the High Museum of Art among the institutions that received works.

The Mark Rothko Foundation was reconstituted to hold and distribute the artist's estate following the widely reported litigation over its handling. Between 1984 and 1986 it distributed more than 1,000 works to institutions around the world, with the National Gallery of Art receiving 296 paintings and roughly 600 works on paper along with the foundation's archive; the foundation then concluded. A distinct alternative to the single-artist-museum model (cf. the Rothko Chapel), it is coded here as an intentional-dispersal / artist-estate distribution.
