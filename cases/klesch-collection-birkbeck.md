# The Klesch Collection — Birkbeck AI/Art-History Fellowship

One-line: London-based collectors Gary and Anita Klesch, who have spent since 2014 building a 40+-work private collection of Old Master paintings, are retaining the collection in full private ownership and instead funding a two-year postdoctoral fellowship at Birkbeck, University of London — Anita Klesch's own alma mater — to research the intersection of AI and art history, a distinct "give to the field, not to a building" legacy vehicle this sample had not yet captured.

## Why in the sample

Every `found-*` pathway in this dataset (standalone museum, house museum, art park, foundation) assumes the collector's endgame is a public-facing institution built around the *collection itself*. The Klesches do something structurally different: the **collection stays exactly where it is** — private, uncatalogued publicly, not pledged to any museum — while a separate philanthropic act funds **the field's scholarly capacity** (a named postdoctoral position, publications, public lectures) at a university the collection has no formal relationship to beyond the gift. This is closest to a **scholarship/prize model** (cf. the already-noted "Klesch Collection Scholarship for Graduate Studies in Renaissance and Baroque Painting," a separate, earlier prize this same collection has funded) scaled up into an ongoing academic post. It tests H4/H5 (does a collection's *distinctiveness* — a genuinely personal Old Master collection assembled by a Birkbeck-trained art historian — predict an unusual, non-institutional form of public contribution) more than it tests governance/survival hypotheses, since no institution is being founded and no succession event is in view.

## Narrative (sourced)

A. Gary Klesch (b. 1947, Cleveland, Ohio; Anglo-American businessman; founder and chairman of the Klesch Group, a Geneva-based industrial/distressed-investment conglomerate with reported 2023 turnover of €8.5 billion) and Dr. Anita Klesch built their Old Master collection together beginning in 2014, when they acquired a set of Giuseppe Arcimboldo's *Four Seasons* — a purchase both describe as the spark for what became "a collaborative venture" [source: thekleschcollection.com/about; confidence: secondary]. The collection has since grown to more than 40 paintings spanning Italian, Dutch, Flemish, French, German, and Spanish artists of the 15th through 17th centuries, including works by Anthony van Dyck, Sofonisba Anguissola, and Peter Paul Rubens [source: thekleschcollection.com; confidence: secondary].

Anita Klesch's own biography is unusually load-bearing for this case: she is a Birkbeck, University of London alumna, completing an MA in Computer Applications for History of Art in 1999 and a PhD in History of Art in 2012, her dissertation titled *"A Golden Age or Crisis? The History of Art in the Age of Information Technology."* [source: The Art Newspaper, 2026-08-20; confidence: secondary]. The couple's 2026 gift — a two-year postdoctoral fellowship "at the intersection of art history, AI and emerging technologies," supervised by Joel McKim and housed at Birkbeck's Vasari Research Centre for Art and Technology — reads less as generic philanthropy and more as a **direct extension of Anita Klesch's own scholarly identity back into her home institution**, distinct from the more common pattern (elsewhere in this sample) of a collector funding a museum built around their *own collection's contents*. This gift funds research infrastructure for the field generally, unconnected to the display or interpretation of the Klesches' own paintings [source: The Art Newspaper, 2026-08-20; confidence: secondary].

This is the collection's **second** identified academic-infrastructure gift: an earlier "Klesch Collection Scholarship for Graduate Studies in Renaissance and Baroque Painting" was already running as of 2025–2026 coverage (Spain Culture USA / Art History News), funding graduate study rather than postdoctoral research [confidence: secondary]. Read together, the two gifts suggest a **repeatable pattern rather than a one-off act**: this collector couple appears to treat scholarship-funding as their standing legacy vehicle, in preference to founding or expanding any public-facing institution around the paintings themselves.
