## Narrative
Eli and Edythe Broad created The Broad Art Foundation in 1984 as a "lending library" for postwar and contemporary art, an explicit alternative to letting works sit in private storage or a single building. The Foundation states it has "loaned artworks to museums more than 9,000 times" to "over 600 museums and galleries worldwide" since its founding, with borrowing institutions bearing packing, transport, handling, and insurance costs. In 2015 the Broads branched the model, opening The Broad museum in downtown Los Angeles (designed by Diller Scofidio + Renfro) as a free public museum; the combined Broad collection is described as "over 2,000 works" by "nearly 200 artists." The Foundation is a private operating foundation (990-PF, EIN 95-4664939) reporting $785.9M in net assets against $7.2M of expenses in FY2024 — a very high coverage ratio, but net assets embed the collection's carried value and cannot be read as a spendable endowment (an UPPER BOUND under [[claims-register]] C4). Eli Broad died in April 2021; Edythe Broad remains active in the Broad Foundations as of 2026, and both the lending library and the museum continued operating past his death, so the model has survived one founder. The case is the report's clearest example of a collector choosing dispersed public access through loans rather than concentration in a single founder monument — while still ultimately building one alongside it.

## Gaps / contradictions
- The "over 2,000 works / nearly 200 artists" figure is the **combined** Broad collection; the Foundation's lending pool alone is not separately published (`unknown`).
- Loan counts are round self-reported figures ("more than 9,000") — precise, dated totals unpublished; treat as primary-from-source but not audited.
- `net_assets_to_opex_ratio` is an UPPER BOUND; no spendable-endowment figure is isolable from a 990-PF for an operating art foundation.
- Two distinct Broad vehicles exist (The Broad Art Foundation, EIN 95-4664939, art/lending; and The Eli and Edythe Broad Foundation, education/science) — figures above are the art foundation only; do not fuse.
