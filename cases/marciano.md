# Marciano Art Foundation — a labor-relations crisis, not a money crisis

*Coded to [[case-template]]. Report-grade, sourced. Included because it is this report's cleanest example of a disposition failing for reasons unrelated to endowment size or founder death: both founders are alive and the foundation is not thinly funded, yet the public-facing institution went dark almost overnight after a governance/labor-relations rupture, and public access has only partially, quietly resumed five years later. ## Narrative

Maurice and Paul Marciano, co-founders of Guess Jeans, opened the Marciano Art Foundation on May 25, 2017, in a converted 1961 Scottish Rite Masonic Temple on Wilshire Boulevard, vacant since 1994 and redesigned by architect Kulapat Yantrasast of wHY [source: search synthesis of https://hyperallergic.com/marciano-arts-foundation/ and https://www.archpaper.com/2017/08/why-masonic-temple-marciano-art-foundation/; confidence: secondary]. The foundation offered free, reservation-based public access to a roughly 1,500-work contemporary-art collection the brothers had assembled [source: search synthesis of https://laist.com/news/entertainment/marciano; confidence: secondary]. Unlike most cases in this report, the founders were — and remain — alive, and the foundation was not obviously capital-starved: its 990-PF total assets have been reported in the ~$50M range in the years around the crisis and after, an order of magnitude above what a small operating foundation would need to survive on paper. That combination — living founders, apparently adequate assets, sudden institutional collapse — is what makes this case diagnostically useful: the failure mode here is neither succession nor money, but governance and labor relations.

In late October 2019, visitor-services associates at the foundation — reported as making a minimum wage of roughly $14.25/hour, without benefits, and complaining of irregular, unpredictably cut schedules — announced a campaign to unionize with AFSCME/District Council 36 [source: search synthesis of https://news.artnet.com/art-world/marciano-foundation-settles-laid-off-workers-1896750 and https://lamag.com/business/marciano-art-foundation-closed/; confidence: secondary]. Within days, on November 6, 2019, the foundation announced it was ending its current exhibitions (Yayoi Kusama and Nicolas Party) early and laying off its visitor-services staff, citing low attendance over the preceding weeks [source: search synthesis of https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/11/06/los-angeless-marciano-art-foundation-lays-off-staff-trying-to-unionise and https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/marciano-art-foundation-fires-employees-union-13520/; confidence: secondary]. Reports of the headcount affected are inconsistent across outlets — "at least 60" in some contemporaneous coverage, 70 in the class-action settlement that followed — and this case does not resolve that discrepancy; both figures are coded as reported, not reconciled. On December 6, 2019, the Marciano brothers stated the foundation "will remain closed permanently" [source: search synthesis of https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/marciano-art-foundation-hanneke-skerath-director-1234711333/; confidence: secondary].

**On causation, the record is genuinely contested and this case treats it that way rather than picking a side.** The foundation's stated reason was declining attendance — notable given that admission was free, which critics used to question the claim, and given reports that the foundation had lobby signage soliciting children's-exhibition submissions for a February opening, cited by union organizers as evidence the institution had been planning to keep operating [source: search synthesis of https://lamag.com/business/marciano-art-foundation-closed/; confidence: secondary]. Laid-off workers and their union called the timing — days after the unionization announcement — evidence of retaliation, and filed an unfair-labor-practice charge with the NLRB, which opened an investigation [source: search synthesis of https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lifestyle/lifestyle-news/national-labor-relations-board-investigating-marciano-art-foundation-staff-layoffs-1252943/; confidence: secondary]. A separate suit alleged the layoffs violated the 60-day advance-notice requirement of mass-layoff (WARN-type) law. The foundation ultimately settled the class action, paying a reported $205,000 to workers plus $70,000 in legal fees, in exchange for the union dropping its unfair-labor-practice complaint [source: search synthesis of https://hyperallergic.com/marciano-art-foundation-settles-lawsuit-over-visitor-services-layoffs/; confidence: secondary]. A settlement of this kind resolves litigation risk; it is not a legal finding on the underlying retaliation question, and this file does not treat it as one. Accordingly `primary_friction` is coded `staffing` — a labor dispute and the layoff itself are the least-contested facts on record — while flagging `governance-conflict` (the brothers' unilateral, board-thin decision-making, exercised without a documented independent-board check) as the closely adjacent, better-fitting frame that the report's broader governance argument may want to foreground; the strict "unionization caused the closure" causal claim is reporting and union allegation, not an adjudicated or foundation-admitted fact.

The building was not idle throughout the closure: between roughly 2021 and 2023 the Marciano brothers' longtime dealer Gagosian mounted three solo shows in the space, and nonprofit arts organizations LAXART and LAND separately staged programs there [source: search synthesis of https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/marciano-art-foundation-hanneke-skerath-director-1234711333/; confidence: secondary]. In February 2024, with little announcement, the foundation mounted its first in-house collection exhibition since 2019 ("Transmissions: Selections from the Marciano Collection"), and in July 2024 it named independent curator Hanneke Skerath as its first director, a newly created position [source: search synthesis of same; confidence: secondary]. The foundation described a "new, scaled-down model" combining free by-appointment collection access with lending the building to partner nonprofit organizations for their own programs [source: search synthesis of same; confidence: secondary]. By 2025–2026 the foundation's own site listed regular Tuesday–Saturday public hours with timed-entry reservations and a current exhibition ("John Giorno: No Nostalgia," Oct. 2025–July 2026, with a announced closure for reinstallation July 21–Sept. 18, 2026) [source: search synthesis of https://marcianoartfoundation.org/visit/; confidence: secondary — this session could not directly fetch the foundation's own site to confirm]. Governance has not visibly changed: the 990-PF director list found in this pass shows Paul Marciano as Chair and President, his niece Olivia Marciano as Secretary, and William Payne as Treasurer — a three-person, majority-family board with no evidence of an independent-board expansion after the crisis.
