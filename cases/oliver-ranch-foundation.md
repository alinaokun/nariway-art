# Oliver Ranch Foundation

One-line: Steve and Nancy Oliver turned a 100-acre Sonoma County sheep pasture into a living commission program — 18 site-specific works by artists including Richard Serra, Bruce Nauman, and Ann Hamilton, each conceived only for its exact spot on the land — then locked in its future in 2009 by partnering with a community foundation rather than building a solo private vehicle.

## Narrative (sourced)

Steve and Nancy Oliver bought 100 acres in Sonoma County in 1981 for what Steve Oliver has described as "my daughter's 4-H project gone bad" — extra grazing land, not an art plan [secondary: Sculpture Magazine, Independent Collectors]. In 1985 they commissioned their first work, Judith Shea's *Shepherd's Muse*, and began inviting artists to conceive pieces explicitly for specific sites on the land, a practice that has since produced 18 large-scale, permanent installations by artists including Richard Serra, Bruce Nauman, Ann Hamilton, Miroslaw Balka, Fred Sandback, and Bill Fontana [secondary]. Unlike acquisition-driven sculpture parks, every work here originates as a site-specific commission — "relationship to land" is described as the one constraint imposed on each artist. In 2009 the Olivers formalized the program by partnering with Community Foundation Sonoma County to create the Oliver Ranch Foundation, a structural choice distinct from either building an independent 501(c)(3) from scratch or giving the collection to an existing museum — a hybrid, community-anchored governance shape this dataset had not yet coded [secondary: independent-collectors.com]. Financially the foundation entity itself reports a small operating budget ($89K FY2024 expenses against $12.6M net assets), which reads as an extreme upper-bound ratio but almost certainly reflects that meaningful maintenance and commissioning costs sit outside this specific filing, not evidence of unusual financial strength — flagged rather than taken at face value.
