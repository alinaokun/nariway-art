# WestLB / Portigon Art Collection → Kunst im Landesbesitz (NRW)

One-line: A failed German state bank's 297-work corporate collection (Picasso, Macke, Beuys, Richter, Uecker) was slated for a bailout-funding sale in 2015, then rescued intact by the state of North Rhine-Westphalia in 2016 for €30M and distributed to public museums via a dedicated foundation — the corporate-collection sub-sample's first case of a *government* stepping in to convert a forced private sale into public ownership.

*(Public projection fields are in the frontmatter above, per [[case-template]]. The prose below is the internal research record.)*

**Why coded this run:** fills two gaps at once — the sample's first German bank-collection case (candidate-universe's own "verify-first" list flagged the European bank-collection dispersals as "a rich, underexamined vein of M&A-and-restructuring transitions") and a genuinely distinct corporate outcome shape. Every prior corporate-failure case in this dataset ([[lehman-brothers-collection]], [[enron-art-collection]]) ends in private dispersal at auction. WestLB/Portigon starts down that same road, an insolvent state-linked institution planning a forced sale, and is diverted at the last step by direct government intervention, ending instead in public-museum custody. A fresh, sourced data point for "public ownership is not public access" ([[what-we-now-believe]]): distribution across "the state's museums and institutions" resembles Vogel's blanket-placement model (broad, not curated to individual museums' stated interest) more than Souls Grown Deep's, worth flagging for a future test of that belief's mechanism claim, though this run could not confirm current (2023–2026) exhibition/loan activity for any specific work.

## Narrative (sourced)

WestLB, one of Germany's largest state-owned ("Landesbank") banks, built a substantial corporate art collection beginning in 1969, eventually including works by Picasso, August Macke, Joseph Beuys, Günther Uecker, Paul Signac, Gerhard Richter, and Thomas Schütte, alongside a reported rare Stradivari violin [secondary: Artnet News]. WestLB collapsed during the 2008–2012 financial crisis and was wound down; its residual entity, Portigon AG, inherited the art collection along with the bank's obligation to help repay the public bailout that had propped it up [secondary: Artnet News, Wikipedia]. In 2015, Portigon drafted a confidential list of roughly 400 works for possible sale to help meet that repayment obligation — a plan NRW's own museum directors publicly condemned as "cynical," given the works' cultural significance [secondary: Artnet News]. Rather than let the sale proceed, NRW's Finance and Culture ministries negotiated a rescue: in 2016 the state purchased the 297-work core collection for €30M through a newly created foundation, "Kunst im Landesbesitz" ("Art in State Possession"), financed by a 100-year loan from the state's own NRW Bank [secondary: Handelsblatt, Artnet News]. The purchased works were then distributed across NRW's public museums and institutions rather than concentrated in a single venue. This is a distinct outcome shape from every other corporate-failure case in this dataset: Lehman Brothers and Enron both ended in straightforward post-collapse private auction; here, a government intervened specifically to keep a failing corporate collection in public hands rather than let market forces disperse it, a state-level analogue to a donor's own "keep it together, keep it public" instinct.
