# Operating Rhythm — how Nariway runs day to day (the learning engine)

*The evidence-phase operating model (Aug 2026). Nariway is not running "marketing channels." Qwoted, Artobiography, and direct conversations are three parts of ONE research and learning engine that builds knowledge, access, relationships, and eventually the field. Largely adopted from an Aug 2026 conversation, with one CFO guardrail added (below). Pairs with [[field-definition]] (the intellectual project) and [[problem-discovery]] (the evidence phase).*

## The three-part engine (not channels)
- **Qwoted = the discovery engine.** Not for finding sources for a piece already decided, but for discovering PEOPLE, PROBLEMS, and LANGUAGE. Issue open questions we do not yet know the answer to ("who is responsible for the future of a private collection?"). Cheap field research; even the PR-pitch noise surrounds a few real findings.
- **Artobiography = the intellectual magnet, and the permission to enter their world.** Keep it resolutely editorial (good enough to read even if Nariway didn't exist), but stay within the collection-futures subject so the body of work compounds into authority on the one question. Every story is a legitimate reason to contact the people connected to it, who lead to other people.
- **Direct conversations = the field laboratory.** 20 to 30 people who know things Alina doesn't. Dawn, Qwoted respondents, Artobiography contacts, the warm network. No ask, no selling; understand the phenomenon before designing the business. Every good conversation ends with "who else sees this part of the art world clearly?", which compounds 20 into 50 with no cold prospecting.

They feed each other: a question → a Qwoted query → people → interviews → new cases → an Artobiography feature or a published conversation → sent to the people it's relevant to → replies, introductions, invitations → the evidence changes the [[decision-map]] or reveals a new model → the cycle restarts. No content calendar, follower goals, SEO treadmill, or Reels strategy.

## The daily practice — Research · Talk · Write · Capture
Not "work on marketing." Four things, repeatedly:
- **Research (60-90 min).** Answer ONE real question about one collection, not "build the knowledge base." (What happened after the founder died? Who advised them? Why did this private museum close? What did it cost to operate? What alternatives were considered?)
- **Talk (30-60 min), the non-negotiable new habit.** ONE outward-facing action every working day: send a Qwoted query, review responses, send five interview questions, write to an Artobiography contact, ask Dawn for an intro, follow up, thank someone. One counts. The rule: never five straight days inside Claude Code.
- **Write (60-90 min).** Artobiography, the next real piece. Two excellent pieces a month beats 30 Notes and 12 LinkedIn posts.
- **Capture (15-30 min), the most important new practice.** After every conversation, response, or finding, record: what surprised me; what this person called the problem; what collectors apparently care about; where responsibility fragmented; what assumption of mine this contradicted; what new model I discovered; who else to talk to; what new question this raises. This is how the field emerges from evidence rather than imagination. Captures route to [[problem-discovery]] and [[what-we-now-believe]].

## Channels (decided)
- **No Instagram, podcast, YouTube, or TikTok yet.** Followers are not the scarce resource; knowledge, access, and relationships are. An hour on a carousel produces less of all three than an hour corresponding with an estate attorney who has handled five collector estates. Parked.
- **LinkedIn = professional verification and network mapping, not a publishing obligation.** Someone sees a Qwoted request → searches Alina → finds Nariway + Artobiography → sees she's serious. Keep the profile current (done); use it to connect and map mutual contacts. No posting treadmill.
- **Written interviews are a formal Artobiography format.** Features answer "what happened?"; conversations answer "what are people seeing now?" Over ~50 interviews with people who have unusual visibility into the life of collections, that qualitative dataset becomes a proprietary Nariway asset. The published interview is the artifact; the accumulated knowledge is the asset. Credibility test still applies ([[conversations-series]]).

## The weekly scorecard (learning, not followers)
| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Meaningful external conversations / written interviews | 2 |
| New high-quality relationships | 2-5 |
| New collection cases discovered | 3+ |
| Important assumptions challenged | 1+ |
| Artobiography progress | substantial |
| New ideas worth investigating | unlimited |
| **Willingness-to-pay signal (CFO guardrail)** | **note any** |

Monthly: **what do we understand now that we didn't 30 days ago?** If substantial, Nariway is moving.

**The guardrail Alina keeps (the CFO's standing job):** the model rightly says "understand the phenomenon before designing the business", but a beautifully-run learning engine that never converts is a think tank, not a company. So keep the willingness-to-pay signal on the scorecard and the H7B clock live: the evidence phase has to reach "a real situation where someone would hire Nariway," not just "we understand it." Learning is the method; a first paid engagement is still the goal.

## What Toi asks (replacing task-tracking)
HOME and the check-in shift from projects and tasks toward four questions: **What did we learn? Who did we hear from? What are we writing? What should we test next?** And once a week, the important one: **did anything change what we think?** That is where [[what-we-now-believe]] evolves from the accumulating evidence.
