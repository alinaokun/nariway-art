# Decision — Sotheby's Institute "Art, Finance, and Investment" (5-day)

*[[advisory-panel|Advisory Panel]] convened 2026-08-12. Status: **awaiting Alina.***

**Question:** enroll in Sotheby's Institute's **Art, Finance, and Investment** five-day NY intensive, **~$2,500** (Fall Oct 26–30, 2026 or Spring Jan 25–29, 2027), taught by **Andrea Danese** (who also taught the Alternative Investments course Alina took in 2024 and rated among her best). → [[courses-register]]

## The panel (in brief)
- **CFO (economic):** the cost is not just $2,500, it is $2,500 plus **five days of founder attention**, the scarcest capital. Return is unclear, because the content likely **overlaps the course she already took** with the same instructor, and it deepens the **art-as-asset-class** frame she deliberately chose not to monetize. Both the dollars and the days have higher-return uses right now. Lean: **not now.**
- **Research (intellectual):** the material is adjacent, not core. Art-as-investment and tokenization sit outside Nariway's legacy-and-stewardship thesis. The genuinely useful slice, art valuation and art-secured lending as they bear on estates, she can get faster and cheaper from the reports and the [[market-intelligence]] she is already reading. A five-day course is a heavy way to fill a narrow gap. Lean: **targeted reading instead.**
- **CMO (relationships):** the value here would be the cohort, not the content. But a finance-course room is a mix of advisors, career-changers, and students, not concentrated with the UHNW collectors or estate attorneys Nariway needs. Better-targeted connection spends exist (see recommendation). Lean: **better rooms elsewhere.**
- **Founder-Life (energy/fit):** she liked Danese and genuinely enjoys learning, both real positives. But she already rejected art investing after his last course, so five more days inside that frame is enjoyable input that does not point where she is going. Enjoyment is real; it should not set direction. **Soft no.**
- **Skeptic (the uncomfortable read):** this is a **comfortable, familiar purchase** (a trusted instructor, a known-good format) at exactly the moment the company needs **external traction, not more input**. It risks being activity that feels like progress. The real test in front of her is the Dawn call and the warm-network conversations, not another classroom.

## Toi's recommendation — **defer / decline for now**
The lenses converge (with the usual caveat that agreement among same-substrate lenses is cheap). Not because it is a bad course, it is proven and she trusts the instructor, but because it likely **repeats content she has**, deepens a frame she **deliberately left**, and spends her two scarcest resources on input when the next real gains are outward.
- **If she wants a course this season,** the more on-thesis choice is the **[[courses-register|UC Berkeley Art, Law, and Finance Certificate]]** (four days, a capped, mixed cohort of collectors, advisors, museum people, and attorneys, on exactly the forces that govern a collection entering public life). Better content fit and a better room.
- **Otherwise,** put the $2,500 and the five days toward the **market conversations that actually test whether anyone pays** (Dawn, the warm network). Revisit the Sotheby's course only if a specific art-finance gap surfaces in real client work.
- **Regardless of the course,** keep **Andrea Danese** as a warm art-finance contact (former CEO, Athena Art Finance; connects to [[Rebecca L. Fine|Rebecca Fine]]'s world). The relationship is worth more than the second course.
