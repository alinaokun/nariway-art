# AI-writing tells — the forbidden-patterns checklist (living)

Every Nariway draft (Artobiography, the report, Notes, LinkedIn, emails, and Toi's own writing to Alina) is checked against this before it reaches her. **It grows**: add a line whenever a new tell is spotted in the wild. Alina hates AI writing and writes about AI for a living, so a clean fingerprint is non-negotiable. This is the mechanical checklist; the principles live in [[voice]].

## The tells, and the fix
1. **The "not X, but Y" antithesis.** "That's not compliance, that's stalling." → Say the second half only: "They're stalling." *(Same thing that made the Substack Notes read as AI, already fixed there.)*
2. **Two short bits stuck together.** "Fast. Simple." "No fluff. Just answers." → Pick one. *(This is the staccato ban already in [[voice]], never three short in a row; extend it to never two clipped fragments as a rhetorical beat.)*
3. **The two-picture metaphor with no advice.** "Less a hammer, more a scalpel." → Say what to actually do.
4. **Clapping for itself.** "And that matters." "That's the part everyone misses." "Which is exactly the point." → Delete. Nothing is lost.
5. **The X-of-Y analogy.** "It's the Excel of AI agents." → Only works if the reader knows both; usually they don't. Cut or explain plainly.
6. **Warming up before talking.** "Here's the thing." "Let me be clear." "The truth is." → Start one sentence later. *(Already on the forbidden-phrases list in [[voice]].)*
7. **Always three things.** "Faster, cheaper, smarter." → Real life gives two, or five. Never always a tidy three.
8. **The fake range.** "5 to 10 minutes." → A range means you never measured. Say one number: seven minutes.
9. **The ending that repeats the post.** "In short…" "At the end of the day…" → Just stop. End on the last real point. *(This is the "no manufactured conclusion" rule, already fixed in the Notes.)*

Plus Alina's standing fingerprint from [[voice]]: **no em dashes, no colons inside sentences, no hedging, no cheap intensifiers** (transformed, revolutionized), no telling the reader how to feel.

## On the STE / "Use ASD-STE100" advice (be discerning)
The Note also recommends Simplified Technical English (aircraft-manual writing, controlled vocabulary) via a `/ste` skill. **Adopt the tell-hunting above universally, but do NOT put Alina's literary voice through STE.** STE flattens prose to plane-manual plainness, which is right for utilitarian copy (steps, methodology notes, some emails) and wrong for the curatorial, recognition-layer voice that is the whole point of Artobiography and the report's narrative. Hassid says it himself: turn it off for anything with a heart, and Alina's writing is the heart kind. Her voice guide is already her tuned standard, better for her purpose than a generic aviation one. **Zinsser's four** (Simplicity, Brevity, Clarity, Humanity) do align, especially Humanity, which is her recognition layer.

## How this grows
Spot a new tell, in a draft or out in the world, add it here with its fix. Toi checks every draft against the current list, and reports repeat offenders so the list keeps sharpening.
