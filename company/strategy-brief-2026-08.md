## Core proposition
Nariway works on **the long-term future of private art collections.** A collection often sits outside the professional infrastructure that manages the rest of a family's wealth, until a trigger (transfer, death, sale, insurance event, tax issue, institutional decision) forces the family to deal with it. Nariway exists at that moment: it helps owners, families, family offices, and their advisers understand the choices, compare precedent, organize the decision, and coordinate the specialists who execute it. Narrower than "art advisory," broader than any one specialty. Owner-side research, strategy, decision support, and coordination — not appraisal, not legal/tax advice, not dealing, not museum development.

## The strategic filter (apply before adding anything — §25)
Before adding research, a page, a data field, a publication section, or a feature, ask:
1. Does this help explain, research, compare, or manage the long-term future of a private art collection?
2. Does it improve the evidence base, professional usefulness, distribution, credibility, or ability to deliver the eventual advisory engagement?
3. Is it public knowledge to share, or collection-specific application that belongs in paid work?
4. Does it reinforce the distinction between Nariway and traditional art advisory / appraisal / legal / tax / museum consulting?
5. Is it supported by enough evidence to publish, or are we building a container because the architecture says it should exist?
If the answer is no, defer it.

## The business architecture — one research engine, four products
1. **Open research** — the Collection Index and Knowledge Base. Free, rigorous, sourced; demonstrates authority and makes Nariway discoverable.
2. **Current intelligence** — Artobiography, the weekly Substack from [[nariway-signals|Signals]] ([[weekly-edition]]). 3–5 private-collection developments with concise why-it-matters analysis.
3. **Professional publishing — Nariway Briefing (NEW).** A periodic, highly designed research briefing for advisers and family-office professionals. An object they keep, not a lead-gen PDF. Digital open; print selective and intentionally distributed. See "New workstreams" below.
4. **Advisory** — high-value owner-side work applying the research to one collection and one family's decisions.

The flywheel: a development enters Signals → may become an Artobiography item → creates/updates a Collection Index record → feeds the Knowledge Base → may become a Briefing → improves the precedent base used in advisory work.

## Advisory offer (hypothesis to test, not a finished product)
- **Collection Futures Orientation** — a bounded, lower-risk front door (previously ~$5–10K, ~2–3 weeks; **pricing is a test variable**). Clarifies the situation, identifies the decisions and specialist questions, and determines whether a full study is warranted.
- **Collection Futures Study** — the deeper engagement (~$25–50K, ~8–12 weeks): compare precedent and pathways, organize decision factors, map tax/legal/appraisal/institutional dependencies, deliver a written study and a working session.
- **No commission tied to the route chosen.** Independence is part of the value. Details in [[collection-futures-study]].

## Knowledge Base direction (§9) — confirms the plan
Depth-gated: a subject earns space only to the depth it changes what can happen to the collection. The strong launch subjects named in the brief are **exactly** the [[knowledge-base-v1-content-plan|Wave 1/2 set]]: the Great Wealth Transfer, the cost of keeping, valuing (different purposes, different correct values), estate planning, partnering with an institution, governance and durability, ownership is not access. Do not pad weak territories to complete the architecture; fill gaps deliberately with subagents when a subject is strategically important.

## New workstreams the brief introduces (go-to-market, not build)
- **Nariway Briefing No. 01 — "What Happens to the Collection?"** ~24 exceptional print pages (heavy paper, primary-source citations, contrasting Collection Index cases), digital open. First run ~250 copies, placed deliberately with estate-planning and family-office leaders, art/estate attorneys, appraisers, museum people, selected journalists, and connectors. Five-part premise in §11 of the source doc.
- **Distribution by embedding in professional communities (§12)** — NAEPC and local Estate Planning Councils first (multidisciplinary, local, team-based). Pilot markets each testing one thing: Greater NJ (lowest-friction first test), NYC (credibility with sophisticated advisers), Westchester (research distribution + modest sponsorship), Palm Beach (publishing as market entry), Miami (cross-disciplinary density). Behave as a contributor, not a seller — learn distribution, knowledge gaps, and what would make a professional comfortable referring a collection situation.
- **Credential strategy (§14)** — the CPA gives professional standing; build **domain** standing via NAEPC participation + selective estate/gift/trust/charitable coursework. AEP and STEP later (grow into, not prerequisites). **Do not** pursue an appraisal credential or generic art-advisory certificates.
- **Specialist network (§15)** — build the bench (estate/trust attorneys, tax/CPA, appraisers, insurers, conservators, museum specialists, philanthropic and family-office advisers), knowing *when* each matters, not internalizing every competency.
- **Elizabeth Attew (§17)** — accepted LinkedIn connection, adjacent-market signal (special projects / family offices / passion assets). No outreach now; follow, and reach out naturally once there is a strong new public object (relaunched Artobiography or Briefing No. 01). → log in CRM.
- **Sponsorship/underwriting (§18)** — councils and future underwriters (trust companies, banks, insurers, appraisal/law firms) may support research with **explicit editorial independence**; not now.

## Positioning & language rules (§23–§24)
- **"Private art collections," not "significant private collections."** Drop "significant" as a default qualifier. *(New rule — supersedes prior copy.)*
- The long-term future of private art collections; independent, owner-side; precedent from what other collections have actually done; works alongside specialists, does not replace them; no transaction commission tied to the route; public research demonstrates capability, paid work applies it.
- No em dashes. Simple, precise, restrained, sophisticated; not marketing copy, no jargon, no clever phrasing. Distinguish **sourced fact / Nariway classification / cross-case finding / professional advice** — never imply legal, tax, appraisal, investment, or museum expertise beyond what Nariway owns.

## Success over 6–12 months (§21) — evidence, not seven figures
The goal is evidence that a seven-figure business *can* exist: professionals forward the research; advisers ask to receive briefings; Nariway is invited to contribute/sponsor/participate; someone says "I have a family you should talk to"; a real collection situation arrives; the first bounded paid engagement occurs; the offer sharpens from real work. Only then optimize pricing, staffing, recurring revenue, sponsorship, scale.
