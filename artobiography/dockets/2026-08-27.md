# Artobiography docket — 2026-08-27

Twelve candidates pulled from the past week of Nariway Signals (Aug 20–27), strongest first; Alina selects 4–6 for the drafted edition.

## 1. Phillips Collection ends its 18-month "core collection" fight

**What happened:** The Phillips Collection reached an agreement with its board of members (a non-governing body including Phillips family descendants and former staff) redefining "core collection" to cover the museum's full 1985 catalogue rather than the narrower 1999 list it had used since 2000. The ten works already pressed toward Sotheby's since 2024 (O'Keeffe, Seurat, Kapoor) can now be sold; everything else in the broader catalogue is now permanently protected from future deaccessioning.
**Date:** Reported Aug 26, 2026 (dispute ran since ~early 2025; new rule takes deaccessioning votes back to Oct 31, 2025)
**Primary source:** [Phillips Collection Management Policy (official, PDF)](https://www.phillipscollection.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/phillips-collection-management-policy.pdf)
**Secondary:** [Hyperallergic, "Why Is DC's Phillips Collection Selling Off Its Masterpieces?"](https://hyperallergic.com/why-is-dc-phillips-collection-selling-off-its-masterpieces/)
**Related Nariway collection:** [[phillips-collection]]
**Landscape subject:** Governance and durability — donor intent, how a founder's "collection" gets redefined decades later
**Why it may matter:** Resolving an 18-month fight over what "core collection" means gives other collector-founded institutions a concrete template for redrawing a founder's holdings without full-scale litigation.
**Verification status:** Verified (2+ independent sources)

## 2. Christie's and Sotheby's openly fighting over the Herbert Lust estate

**What happened:** Herbert Lust, an eccentric former investment banker who died in May 2026 at 99, spent seven decades assembling over 1,000 works (deep Giacometti, plus Indiana, Bellmer, Bauermeister, Stella). His estate is now shopping roughly 300 pieces worth about $60M between the two biggest auction houses, with a major Giacometti sculpture the likely top lot; he had already donated hundreds of works to museums (Hirshhorn, Tel Aviv Museum) during his lifetime.
**Date:** Reported Aug 20, 2026
**Primary source:** [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/christies-sothebys-battle-herbert-lust-collection-1234795281/)
**Secondary:** [Robb Report](https://robbreport.com/shelter/art-collectibles/christies-sothebys-herbert-lust-art-holdings-1238570046/)
**Related Nariway collection:** [[herbert-lust-collection]]
**Landscape subject:** Estate planning / disperse — the market
**Why it may matter:** A consignment fought over openly between two houses is a visible marker of what happens when a collector's own lifetime distribution plan stops partway through.
**Verification status:** Verified (2+ independent sources)

## 3. Amanda Precourt's Cookie Factory gets a full profile as the anti-vanity-museum case

**What happened:** Denver collector and developer Amanda Precourt converted a condemned fortune-cookie factory in the Baker neighborhood into Cookie Factory, a free 5,000+ sq ft public art space (exhibition and studio rooms, a video/sound theater, a sculpture garden) with Jérôme Sans as artistic director; she lives above it with her own collection. Elisa Carollo's Aug 25 Observer profile frames the project explicitly against the founder's-monument museum model, and it was picked up in this week's Art Spell roundup.
**Date:** Profile published Aug 25, 2026 (space opened May 2025)
**Primary source:** [Art Spell roundup](https://www.artspell.media/p/august-25-2026)
**Secondary:** [The Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2025/05/23/new-art-center-debuts-in-an-old-denver-fortune-cookie-factory/)
**Related Nariway collection:** [[cookie-factory-denver]]
**Landscape subject:** Public-access models — a novel non-building form
**Why it may matter:** A living collector publicly rejecting the founder's-monument model is a useful counterweight to the mega-museum pathway this docket tracks most often.
**Verification status:** Verified (2+ independent sources)

## 4. Sandy Lerner's cat-art collection gets firm dispersal dates at Bonhams

**What happened:** Cisco co-founder Sandy Lerner's three-decade collection of cat-related decorative arts, books, and collectibles — the subject of her 2017 book *Caticons* — now has confirmed sale dates: an online leg Sept 22–Oct 1 and a live sale Sept 30 at Bonhams' new New York flagship, 66 lots including a Foujita and a Giacometti. No foundation, no museum — a plain, cheerfully specific single-owner dispersal.
**Date:** Firm dates confirmed Aug 26–27, 2026; sales Sept 22–30, 2026
**Primary source:** [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/sandy-lerner-cat-themed-art-collection-auctioned-bonhams-1234795289/)
**Secondary:** [Artsy](https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-major-collection-cat-related-art-collectibles-heads-bonhams-auction-fall)
**Related Nariway collection:** none yet — candidate to add (Sandy Lerner Cat Collection)
**Landscape subject:** Disperse / sale — the market
**Why it may matter:** A cleanly personal, non-canonical collection choosing straightforward sale over any legacy vehicle is a counterweight to this docket's museum- and foundation-heavy sample.
**Verification status:** Verified (2+ independent sources)

## 5. The Met recycles a decade-old Irving gift into its own acquisitions fund

**What happened:** Sotheby's will offer more than 300 Chinese works — jades, Buddhist sculpture, porcelain, led by a Qing-dynasty jade brushpot — across two Asia Week sales (Sept 10 and Sept 14, 2026), all originally given to the Met in 2015 by philanthropists Florence and Herbert Irving. Proceeds go into an Irving fund earmarked for future Asian-art acquisitions; a similar Irving-gift sale series has run in prior years, so this is a continuation of an established arrangement rather than a one-off.
**Date:** Reported Aug 27, 2026; sales Sept 10 & 14, 2026
**Primary source:** [Artsy](https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-metropolitan-museum-will-sell-300-works-chinese-art-sothebys)
**Secondary:** [Sotheby's official sale series](https://www.sothebys.com/en/series/chinese-art-from-the-metropolitan-museum-of-art-the-florence-and-herbert-irving-gift)
**Related Nariway collection:** none yet — candidate to add (Met — Irving Chinese Art Gift deaccession-and-recycle)
**Landscape subject:** Valuation / deaccessioning — the market
**Why it may matter:** A museum recycling a donor's gift into its own acquisitions fund, structured with the family's apparent blessing, is a different shape of deaccessioning than the funding-crisis cases this docket usually logs.
**Verification status:** Verified (2+ independent sources)

## 6. A Mondrian held by one family for 70 years surfaces at Christie's

**What happened:** Architect-collector Armand Bartos bought *Composition III* (1920) in 1950 and held it with his wife Celeste until 1973; it has never once appeared at public auction. It now leads Christie's 20th/21st Century Evening Sale during Frieze Week (London, Oct 14, 2026), estimated £20–30M ($27–41M).
**Date:** Reported Aug 26–27, 2026; sale Oct 14, 2026
**Primary source:** [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/mondrian-painting-auction-christies-frieze-week-1234795778/)
**Secondary:** [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/piet-mondrian-auction-christies-london-2802907)
**Related Nariway collection:** none yet — candidate to add (Bartos Collection)
**Landscape subject:** The market / disperse
**Why it may matter:** A defining work released by the family that held it for seven decades, rather than by an estate after a death, illustrates a slower and more deliberate route to market.
**Verification status:** Verified (2+ independent sources)

A smaller "Grand Central Galleries" media-lab prelude, not the museum itself, opens spring 2026 in the meantime.
**Date:** Reported Aug 22, 2026
**Primary source:** [Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/future-las-vegas-art-museum-unveils-plans-for-media-lab-3392100/)
**Secondary:** none found matching this specific date update

**Landscape subject:** Estate planning — succession and durability after a founder's death
**Why it may matter:** A second slip in the opening date since its founder's death is worth tracking as a case of what a founder-driven museum's timeline does once the founder is no longer there to drive it.

## 8. Klesch Collection endows an art-history-and-AI fellowship instead of expanding a building

**What happened:** Anita and A. Gary Klesch, whose Klesch Collection holds 15th- through 17th-century European art (Arcimboldo, van Dyck, Anguissola, Rubens), are funding a two-year postdoctoral fellowship at Birkbeck, University of London, on the intersection of art history, AI, and digital heritage. Anita Klesch holds both a master's and a PhD from Birkbeck.
**Date:** Reported Aug 20, 2026
**Primary source:** [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/08/20/klesch-collection-endow-postdoctoral-fellowship-focusing-art-history-ai-birkbeck-london)
**Secondary:** none found
**Related Nariway collection:** [[klesch-collection-birkbeck]]
**Landscape subject:** Partner — scholarly infrastructure as a legacy vehicle
**Why it may matter:** Funding scholarly infrastructure rather than a building or a loan program is a legacy vehicle this docket sees less often, worth watching for whether other collectors follow.
**Verification status:** Single-source

## 9. Art Omi's shared-campus pavilions flagged as a taxonomy problem

**What happened:** Art Omi's 190-acre Chatham, NY site — where 12 to 18 collectors and artists (Susan and Michael Hort among the first) each design and control their own legacy pavilion in collaboration with an architect of their choosing — was reassessed by Nariway Signals this week as not fitting any of the six standing pathway families (build / partner / give / disperse / sell / keep-family). A proposal for a new "shared-campus" family is now pending Alina's decision. Phase One opens June 2027.
**Date:** Flagged in Signals Aug 23, 2026 (project first reported 2024)
**Primary source:** [Art Omi Pavilions](https://artomipavilions.org/)
**Secondary:** [The Architect's Newspaper](https://www.archpaper.com/2024/07/art-omi-debuts-new-architect-and-artist-designed-pavilions-in-chatham-new-york/)
**Related Nariway collection:** none yet — candidate to add (case file deferred pending [[pathway-family-proposal-shared-campus]])
**Landscape subject:** Governance and durability — a genuinely novel non-building form
**Why it may matter:** A shared-campus model where each participant controls one pavilion does not fit the usual pathway categories, which may be reason enough to treat it as its own case type.
**Verification status:** Verified (2+ independent sources)

## 11. Ken Griffin's Leonardo hangs quietly at the Met, on loan

**What happened:** *Madonna of the Yarnwinder* (studio of Leonardo, c. 1505), a work owned by hedge-fund billionaire Ken Griffin, has hung at the Met since spring 2026 with no announcement — only a new wall label and trade-press notice. It is one of very few Leonardo-attributed works on view in North America.

## 12. Yusaku Maezawa's superyacht carries a two-story private gallery

**What happened:** Japanese billionaire Yusaku Maezawa took delivery of the *Nausicaä*, a $350M, 375-foot superyacht with a two-story revolving gallery holding works by Warhol, Koons, Prince, and Wool. Unlike most items in this docket, the space is entirely private, with no public access.
**Date:** Reported Aug 19–20, 2026
**Primary source:** [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/art-collector-yusaku-maezawa-superyacht-2798941)
**Secondary:** [ARTnews, "Industry Moves"](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/bruce-museum-chief-curator-yusaku-maezawa-yacht-industry-moves-1234795285/)
