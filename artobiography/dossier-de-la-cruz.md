# Reporting Dossier — The De la Cruz Collection

*This is a reporter's pre-reporting file, not a draft. Every factual claim is tagged and sourced. It exists to be argued with.*

## 0. Note on sourcing quality and the biggest evidence gaps

The public record on the **arc** of this story is unusually good and consistent: the timeline (Rosa's death Feb 2024 → closure → Christie's single-owner sale May 2024 → ICA Miami building purchase Oct 2024) is corroborated across WLRN, ARTnews, Artnet, The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic, Wikipedia, and ICA Miami's own announcement. Christie's auction results are firmly documented. Carlos de la Cruz gave at least one on-record interview (to the *Miami Herald*, republished/summarized via WLRN and Yahoo) in which he explained his decision in his own words — the single most valuable primary-voice source we have, and the spine of the "was selling the plan?" question.

The record is **thin or absent** on exactly the things the governing question turns on: (1) any **estate/probate document** — no will, trust, or gift instrument has been located, so Carlos's stated reasoning is his own paraphrase, not a verified plan; (2) whether the Collection was ever a **501(c)(3)** or always a privately owned exhibition space (I found no filing; the Moore Space, by contrast, is described as a nonprofit); (3) the **final total** and disposition of the full ~200-work consignment — only the 26-lot single-owner Evening Sale is cleanly reported, with the remaining works dispersed into Christie's day/other evening sales and no consolidated total published; (4) the **destinations of most major works** (only the top González-Torres is traced, to the Pola Museum in Japan); (5) the internal life of the **education programs** — real, repeatedly cited, but never documented in budget or headcount terms. Two factual conflicts recur and are flagged in §6: Rosa's **birth year** (1942 vs 1944) and the day-of-week/exact framing of her death. Biggest single caveat: **almost nothing here is from a primary document.** It is press reporting, much of it tracing back to the same *Miami Herald* interview and the same Christie's press materials. A serious piece needs the probate file and a direct Carlos interview to move from "well-reported" to "known."

Tags used below: **[CONFIRMED]** = multiple independent reputable sources; **[SINGLE-SOURCE]** = one reputable source, uncorroborated; **[INFERENCE]** = reasoned from facts, not stated; **[UNKNOWN]** = not found / would require primary docs.

## 2. Rosa's and Carlos's own words — what THEY said they wanted

**Carlos de la Cruz, to the *Miami Herald* (via WLRN, April 2024)** — the core primary-voice statements:

- On the decision and whose project it was: *"The collection was her baby. She did a wonderful job of presenting it to the community. I'm very proud of what she did. Very, very proud of what she did, and I'd be happy to do it all over again. But it was hers. And it's different now."* [CONFIRMED quote] (WLRN)
- On the ending: *"The Herald had published a headline when Queen Elizabeth was buried that said 'End of an Era.' For me, it was an end of an era, too."* [CONFIRMED quote] (WLRN)
- On the works' future once sold: *"Hopefully, people that buy it will show it."* [CONFIRMED quote] (WLRN) — note this is a *hope*, not a *condition*; he attached no resale/display covenant.
- On Rosa's character: *"She speaks her mind. She doesn't mince words. She's like a force in the river, a current."* [CONFIRMED quote] (WLRN)
- The reason given for not passing it on: taxes and maintenance costs for ~1,000 works, and an unwillingness to burden the next generation — **five children, 17 grandchildren, six great-grandchildren**. Carlos said he **had not planned to sell** and had not expected Rosa's death; he simply never reopened. [CONFIRMED as reported] (WLRN; Artnet)

- On the ICA sale (Oct 2024): *"I am really happy to have helped the ICA to grow. This combined space now gives Miami a museum about the same size as the Whitney Museum in New York City."* [CONFIRMED quote] (WLRN ICA piece)

**The "three destinies" philosophy** (central to the governing question, HANDLE WITH CARE): the vault records Carlos paraphrasing Rosa's view that art *"will either go to an institution, which might not be able to show them; to your kids, who might not want them; or to an auction house,"* and choosing the third. This is a **[SINGLE-SOURCE], reported paraphrase** (tracing to the *Miami Herald* interview) — I did **not** re-confirm it verbatim this session and it should be treated as Carlos's after-the-fact framing, not a documented instrument. **If genuine, it is the strongest evidence that selling was a considered plan, not a failure.** Flag its provenance in any use.

**Rosa's own recorded statements** about the collection's purpose/mission: **[UNKNOWN / thin]** — I did not locate a first-person Rosa quote laying out the mission this session. The collection's mission language (free public access; education) is attested by the programs themselves and by others' testimony, not by a sourced Rosa quotation. **Primary source needed:** the collection's own website "About/Mission" text (delacruzcollection.org, cited in the vault but not re-fetched here) and any catalog forewords or interviews in Rosa's voice.

**What they said they wanted, in sum:** free public access to serious contemporary art in Miami; direct support of living artists; and an education/travel pipeline for local students. What Carlos said about the *future*: not continuation — an unsentimental, self-described "end of an era," with a hope (not a requirement) that buyers would keep showing the work.

## 4. What was sold, and what happened to the building

**The art**
- Lifetime collection: **~1,000 works** (contemporary, strong in Ana Mendieta, Félix González-Torres, Christopher Wool, Mark Bradford, Peter Doig, Isa Genzken, Sterling Ruby, Gabriel Orozco, Jim Hodges, Dan Colen, Nate Lowman, Glenn Ligon, Mark Grotjahn). [CONFIRMED breadth] (multiple)
- Consigned to Christie's: **"over 200 works"** across multiple sales; pre-sale whole-consignment estimate **~$30M**. [CONFIRMED as reported] (WLRN; Artnet)
- **Single-owner "The Rosa de la Cruz Collection" Evening Sale, May 14, 2024, NY:** **26 lots offered, 25 sold** (one withdrawal); **$28.1M hammer / $34.4M with fees**; estimate **$25.8M–$37.9M** (hammer). Sell-through ~90%+ by volume. **Auction records set for Ana Mendieta and Félix González-Torres.** [CONFIRMED] (The Art Newspaper; Artnet; Collector Daily)
- **Top lot:** **Félix González-Torres, "Untitled" (America #3), 1992** (light-string work) — **$11.5M hammer / $13.6M with fees**; **acquired by the Pola Museum of Art, Hakone, Japan** (the one traced major destination). [CONFIRMED] (The Art Newspaper)
- **Remaining ~170+ works** dispersed into Christie's 21st-Century Evening, 20th-Century Evening, and Post-War & Contemporary Day sales (May 14/16/17). **A consolidated de-la-Cruz total across all tranches was NOT published** in what I found. [UNKNOWN — the single most concrete number still missing]
- **Structure:** single-owner marquee sale + dispersal — i.e., the 26 best as a named collection, the balance folded into general sales. Christie's used a **house guarantee** plus **17 third-party guarantees**. [CONFIRMED] (The Art Newspaper)
- Note a **valuation contradiction (§6):** The Art Newspaper reports the collection was "once valued at approximately $100 million," against a ~$30M consignment estimate and $34.4M realized for the marquee 26. These are not reconciled in the reporting.

**The building**
- **23 NE 41st Street**, 30,000 sq ft, Design District, architect John Marquette. **Sold to ICA Miami for $25M**, announced **Oct 15, 2024**, funded **entirely by private philanthropy** (Craig Robins among named donors). ICA sits next door; the purchase roughly **doubles** its space. Planned uses: permanent-collection galleries (ICA said ~99.5% of its collection had been in storage), education programming, special exhibitions, a Knight Foundation new-media gallery. Reopening targeted before end of 2025. [CONFIRMED] (ARTnews; WLRN; The Art Newspaper)
- Sold by **Carlos / the de la Cruz side** (WLRN frames it as Carlos "helping ICA grow"; ARTnews frames the sale on behalf of the collection following Rosa's death). Whether title ran through an estate/trust vs. Carlos personally: **[UNKNOWN — deed/probate would confirm].**
- **The art proceeds ($34.4M+) and the building proceeds ($25M) are separate transactions and must not be summed.**

## 6. Contradictions and unresolved points in the existing reporting

1. **Rosa's birth year: 1942 vs 1944.** The vault's earlier synthesis cited 1942 (from an Artnet snippet); WLRN and ARTnews obituaries give **1944**. Age-at-death (81, Feb 2024) is consistent with **1942** *or* an early-1943 birthday, and slightly *inconsistent* with 1944 (she'd have been 79–80). **Unresolved — a birth record/obituary would settle it.**
2. **Day/date and residence of death.** Death date **Feb 25, 2024** is well-corroborated and **Feb 25, 2024 was a Sunday** (fits the "died on the museum's closed day" framing). One search snippet rendered it as "Sunday, February 27" — **that is internally inconsistent** (Feb 27 was a Tuesday) and appears to be an error. Residence is given as **Key Biscayne** in some accounts and **Miami/her home** in others; likely the same Key Biscayne home. Minor, but note.
3. **Closure timing/framing.** Accounts range from "closed immediately / never reopened after Feb 25" to The Art Newspaper's "closed in March 2024." Not contradictory in substance (never reopened → formally closed weeks later), but the *formal* closure date is soft.
4. **"$30M estimate" vs "$34.4M realized (26 lots)" vs "once valued ~$100M."** The pre-sale ~$30M figure was for the **whole multi-sale consignment**; the $34.4M (with fees) was the **26-lot marquee sale alone**; the ~$100M is a loose historical valuation of the **full ~1,000-work collection**. The reporting never reconciles these, and **no consolidated all-tranches total is published.** Do not silently merge them.
5. **Number of works sold.** "26 lots" (marquee sale) vs "over 200 works" (total consignment) vs "~1,000" (lifetime). All three are correct at different scopes but are easily conflated in secondary coverage.
6. **Was selling "always the plan"?** Carlos says he **had not planned to sell** and never expected Rosa's death — yet also invokes Rosa's "three destinies" philosophy that seems to pre-rationalize a sale. These sit in tension: unplanned in timing, but arguably pre-considered in principle. **This is the story's central unresolved tension, not a factual error — preserve it.**
7. **501(c)(3) status.** Widely described as a "museum," but no nonprofit filing was located; the governance shape (private exhibition space vs. incorporated nonprofit) is **unconfirmed**.

## 8. Questions the existing record cannot answer (interviews / primary docs only)

- **What did Carlos actually intend — and when?** Was a sale ever discussed with Rosa? Is the "three destinies" philosophy a real shared conviction or an after-the-fact frame? (Only Carlos, the children, or Yanez can speak to this; the probate file may hint at intent.)
- **Was selling the plan, or the absence of one?** The governing hypothesis. Carlos says "unplanned," yet chose fast and decisively. Did the couple ever consider — and reject — a foundation, a gift to PAMM/ICA, or a family trust? (Estate docs; family.)
- **Did any binding instrument exist?** Will, trust, gift agreement, or nothing? (Probate.) If nothing, the "sole discretion → sell by default" reading is confirmed; if something, the story changes.
- **What did the education mission mean to them, concretely?** How many students, which schools, how funded, does it continue? Was it Rosa's central purpose or one program among many? (Yanez; DASH/NWSA; family.)
- **Where did the major works go?** Only the top González-Torres (Pola Museum, Japan) is traced. Where are the Mendietas, the Wools, the Bradfords now — private hands, museums, back to Miami? (Christie's; buyers where public.)
- **Was the Collection ever a nonprofit** — and if so, what happened to any charitable assets? (Sunbiz/IRS.)
- **Why ICA, and on what terms?** Was the building sale negotiated (below-market, given ICA's mission-fit and the Braman/Robins network), or arm's-length at $25M? Any use restrictions honoring the de la Cruz legacy? (Deed; Gartenfeld; Robins.)
- **What did the Miami art world lose, and was that loss inevitable?** Not "should it have survived" — but what a free, artist-supporting, education-driven private museum provided that its dispersal removed, and whether Carlos sees the ICA transfer as continuation of that or a clean end.
