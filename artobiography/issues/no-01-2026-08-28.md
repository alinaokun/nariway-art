**01**

## The Lucas Museum finally has an opening date

September 22 is now the date. After years in which George Lucas and Mellody Hobson's museum was described only as opening sometime in 2026, the Lucas Museum of Narrative Art has set a firm opening in Los Angeles, a month away. The couple have spent more than a decade and roughly a billion dollars on the 300,000-square-foot building, designed by the architect Ma Yansong and under construction in Exposition Park since 2018, to house a collection of more than 40,000 works spanning comics, illustration, film stills, and fine art. The founders have long argued that visual storytelling is a people's art, undervalued by a fine-art world that treats narrative and illustration as lesser forms.

The scale is unusual even for a founder's museum. Most collectors who build institutions do so around a defined body of work, a period, a medium, a region. The Lucas collection is held together instead by an argument about what deserves to be taken seriously, which gives the museum a broader and less predictable remit than most. A month from now there will be a building full of art to measure the argument against.

### THIS WEEK

**02**

### A museum designed around time

Canyon opens this fall on Manhattan's Lower East Side, a space the collector Robert Rosenkranz has built for video, sound, and performance, the kind of work that asks for more time than a painting does. Joseph Thompson, the founding director of MASS MoCA, will run it as something between a museum, a performance venue, and a place to spend an evening, with late hours and real hospitality. Rosenkranz already shows work of this kind inside his own home, and Canyon extends that into a public building shaped around how the art wants to be seen rather than fitted to a conventional gallery.

**03**

### Art Omi is testing a shared-campus model

Art Omi, on a 190-acre site in Chatham, New York, is building a campus where twelve to eighteen collectors and artists will each commission their own pavilion, working with an architect of their choosing, to hold part of their collection. Susan and Michael Hort are among the first participants, and Phase One opens in June 2027. It is neither one founder's museum nor a dispersal, but shared land and infrastructure with each participant keeping control of their own building and legacy, a middle path for a collector who wants a lasting public home without the cost and risk of going it alone.

**04**

### Las Vegas pushes its museum opening to 2029

The Las Vegas Museum of Art, long championed by the collector and philanthropist Elaine Wynn, has moved its opening to the fall of 2029. A smaller prelude, a media lab called Grand Central Galleries, opens first, in the spring of 2026. It is the first substantial timeline news since Wynn, who drove the project, died in April 2025. The museum has been planned as the city's first major art institution, and the delay tracks the gap her death left at the center of it.

### IN BRIEF

**Wolf Kahn Foundation opens its archive**
A permanent archive and scholarship center for the painter has opened at 134 West 26th Street in Manhattan, by appointment, holding his studies, letters, lectures, and photographs. It arrives a year ahead of his centennial, run by his longtime friend Ellen McCulloch-Lovell.

**Klesch collection funds an AI fellowship**
Anita and A. Gary Klesch are endowing a two-year fellowship in art history and artificial intelligence at Birkbeck, University of London, putting a collection's resources into the field rather than a museum of their own.

**Museums experiment with shared ownership**
The Met and Cleveland have split a group of eighteenth-century Chinese prints, and the Frans Hals Museum and the Mauritshuis have jointly acquired two Frans Hals paintings from a private collection, part of a wider turn toward shared ownership of work no single museum wants to chase alone.
