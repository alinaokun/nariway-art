### Lucas finally has a date

The Lucas Museum of Narrative Art will open in Los Angeles on September 22, ending months in which the project was described only as opening sometime in 2026. George Lucas and Mellody Hobson have spent years and roughly a billion dollars on the 300,000-square-foot building, which will house a collection of more than 40,000 works spanning comics, illustration, film stills, and fine art. The founders have long argued that visual storytelling is a people's art, undervalued by a fine-art world that treats narrative and illustration as lesser forms.

The scale is unusual even for a founder's museum. Most collectors who build institutions do so around a defined body of work, a period, a medium, a region. The Lucas collection is held together instead by an argument about what deserves to be taken seriously, which gives the museum a broader and less predictable remit. A month from now there will be a building full of art to measure that argument against.

### Elsewhere

**A museum designed for slow art**
Canyon opens this fall on Manhattan's Lower East Side, a space the collector Robert Rosenkranz has built for video, sound, and performance, work that asks for more time than a painting does. Joseph Thompson, the founding director of MASS MoCA, will run it as something between a museum, a performance venue, and a place to spend an evening, with late hours and real hospitality. Rosenkranz already shows work of this kind inside his own home, and Canyon extends that into a public building shaped around how the art wants to be seen.

**A different model for the collector's museum**
Art Omi, on a 190-acre site in Chatham, New York, is building a campus where twelve to eighteen collectors and artists will each commission their own pavilion, working with an architect of their choosing, to hold part of their collection. Susan and Michael Hort are among the first participants, and Phase One opens in June 2027. It is neither one founder's museum nor a dispersal, but shared land and infrastructure with each participant keeping control of their own building and legacy.

**Las Vegas moves to 2029**
The Las Vegas Museum of Art, long championed by the collector Elaine Wynn, has pushed its opening to the fall of 2029, with a smaller media-lab prelude called Grand Central Galleries arriving first in the spring of 2026. It is the first substantial timeline news since Wynn died in April 2025.

### In brief

**Wolf Kahn Foundation opens an archive.** A permanent archive and scholarship center for the painter has opened at 134 West 26th Street in Manhattan, by appointment, holding his studies, letters, lectures, and photographs. It arrives a year ahead of his centennial, run by his longtime friend Ellen McCulloch-Lovell.

**A collection funds scholarship instead of a building.** Anita and A. Gary Klesch are endowing a two-year fellowship in art history and artificial intelligence at Birkbeck, University of London, directing a collection's resources toward the field rather than a museum of their own.

**Museums are buying together.** The Met and Cleveland have split a group of eighteenth-century Chinese prints, and the Frans Hals Museum and the Mauritshuis have jointly acquired two Frans Hals paintings out of a private collection, part of a wider turn toward shared ownership of work no single museum wants to chase alone.
