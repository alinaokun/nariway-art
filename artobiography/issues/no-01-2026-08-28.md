## Artobiography
*No. 01 · August 28, 2026*

### This week in private collections

**The Lucas Museum of Narrative Art has an opening date**

George Lucas and Mellody Hobson's Museum of Narrative Art will open in Los Angeles on September 22. The 300,000-square-foot building holds more than 40,000 works spanning comics, illustration, film stills, and fine art, gathered under the idea that visual storytelling belongs to everyone. The museum has been described as opening sometime in 2026 for months, and now has a fixed date, a month away.

It is the largest current test of whether a collection built around a single idea can become an institution at civic scale without losing the specificity that made it a collection in the first place. What the founders actually gathered, and how the building chooses to present it, is the thing to watch.

**Robert Rosenkranz is opening a museum designed around time-based art**

Canyon, scheduled to open this fall on Manhattan's Lower East Side, is being developed by the collector Robert Rosenkranz for video, sound, performance, and other time-based work. Joseph Thompson, the founding director of MASS MoCA, will lead it, and the space is conceived as part museum, part performance venue, and part gathering place, with evening hours and genuine hospitality.

The institution is being shaped around the way the collection needs to be experienced, rather than the reverse. Most private collections that go public adopt a conventional museum model and fit the art to it. Canyon is another example of the character of a collection deciding the form of the institution built around it.

**Art Omi is building a campus where each collector designs their own pavilion**

Art Omi, on a 190-acre site in Chatham, New York, is developing a campus where twelve to eighteen collectors and artists will each commission a building to hold part of their collection, working with an architect of their choosing. Susan and Michael Hort are among the first participants. Phase One opens in June 2027.

This is neither a single founder's museum nor a dispersal. It is shared land and shared infrastructure, with each participant keeping control of their own structure and their own legacy. For a collector who wants a lasting public home without the cost and risk of a standalone museum, it is a new option worth watching for whether it repeats.

**The Las Vegas Museum of Art has pushed its opening to 2029**

The Las Vegas Museum of Art, long championed by the collector and philanthropist Elaine Wynn, has moved its opening to the fall of 2029. A smaller prelude, a media lab called Grand Central Galleries, opens first, in the spring of 2026. This is the first substantial timeline news since Wynn died in April 2025.

Set beside the Lucas Museum's firm date, it shows the same variable from the other side. A collector-driven museum tends to run on the energy of the person driving it, and a collection's public future often depends on whether that person is still there to see it through.

Related collection → Las Vegas Museum of Art

**The Wolf Kahn Foundation has opened a public archive**

The Wolf Kahn Foundation has opened a permanent archive and scholarship center in Manhattan, at 134 West 26th Street, available by appointment. It holds early studies, letters, lectures, and photographs, and is run by the painter's longtime friend Ellen McCulloch-Lovell. The opening comes a year ahead of Kahn's centennial.

Public access to a collection does not always mean a museum. An archive turns a private body of material into standing infrastructure for scholarship, at a fraction of a museum's cost and obligation. It is one of the quieter answers to what a collection can become, and one of the more durable.

### Also worth noting

Anita and A. Gary Klesch are endowing a two-year fellowship in art history and artificial intelligence at Birkbeck, University of London, directing a collection's resources toward scholarship rather than a building.

Museums are increasingly buying major works together rather than competing for them, with the Met and Cleveland splitting a group of Chinese prints and the Frans Hals Museum and the Mauritshuis sharing two Frans Hals paintings out of a private collection, a sign that shared ownership is becoming a common answer for where a dispersing collection's best pieces land.

Canada's federal Indigenous Art Centre still cannot account for 111 works two years after an audit first flagged them, a reminder that a collection with no single accountable steward can quietly lose track of itself.
