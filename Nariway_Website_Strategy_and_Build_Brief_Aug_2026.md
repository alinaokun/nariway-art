# Nariway Website Strategy and Build Brief

**Version:** August 27, 2026  
**Status:** Current source of truth for the Nariway website-design Claude Code project  
**Purpose:** Rebuild nariway.com around the current professional-services business while preserving the strongest elements of the existing visual identity.

---

## How to use this document

This is the authoritative website strategy.

The site must no longer present Nariway primarily as a research initiative, general collection-future advisory, publication, or cultural project.

The current business positioning is:

> **Nariway is a specialist service used by estate attorneys and fiduciaries to control and document the resolution of significant art collections.**

The website has one primary job:

> **When Alina meets an estate attorney, trustee, trust officer, or other professional at an Estate Planning Council event, that person should be able to visit nariway.com and understand within seconds what Nariway does, why it is different, what the work product looks like, and when they would call.**

The site is not being designed to generate mass online leads.

It is a credibility and explanation tool for a high-trust referral business.

# 1. What exists now

The current Nariway homepage has a strong, restrained visual language that should not be discarded casually.

Current homepage characteristics:

- large architectural hero image with pale stone/cream arches and empty framed recesses;
- black serif hero headline;
- strong white space;
- small, restrained navigation;
- simple black-and-white typography;
- thin horizontal rules;
- editorial layout;
- a "Conversations" section with interview cards;
- positioning around "The future of private art collections";
- supporting copy around collections remaining in families, entering museums/universities, becoming foundations, traveling, being divided or sold;
- a line that "Nariway studies what happens next";
- lower-page language describing Nariway as "Research and advisory for private art collections";
- a call to collectors and families considering the future of a collection;
- footer links to Collections, About, Contact, and Artobiography.

The current About page also has valuable visual qualities:

- white background;
- restrained serif/sans hierarchy;
- generous margins;
- founder portrait;
- direct biography;
- no overdesigned luxury effects.

Current About positioning says:

- "Every collection has a future";
- Nariway studies what happens to private collections;
- research informs advisory work with collectors and families;
- the founder biography emphasizes finance/strategy experience as well as being an art collector, researcher, and writer.

The new site should **preserve the visual restraint** while replacing the strategic message.

# 2. What should be kept

## Keep the quiet visual identity

Do not turn Nariway into:

- a blue corporate law-firm site;
- a black-and-gold luxury art-advisory site;
- a dark family-office template;
- a startup SaaS site;
- a trendy AI company;
- an editorial magazine;
- a museum clone.

The current design already communicates:

- discretion;
- taste;
- calm;
- seriousness;
- art adjacency;
- restraint.

Those are useful.

## Keep the architectural hero language

The current cream architectural image can remain as part of the Nariway visual identity if it works responsively with the new copy.

The empty frames and arches can be interpreted as transition, absence, and the future of objects without literally showing a painting.

However:

- do not flood the rest of the site with beige;
- keep white as the dominant page background;
- use the cream/stone visual as an accent or hero moment;
- increase visual precision lower on the page through structured tables, report excerpts, diagrams, and document-like components.

The new site should feel like:

> **museum catalogue restraint + private-bank clarity + a beautifully designed professional working paper**

not like a generic luxury site.

## Keep the serif/sans contrast

The current serif headline typography is a strength.

Keep the overall typographic logic:

- serif for major statements;
- clean sans for navigation, labels, body support, metadata, process details, and report UI;
- moderate line length;
- generous leading;
- no excessive all-caps;
- no heavy display type.

Do not change fonts simply to make the redesign feel new unless there is a clear functional problem.

## Keep Collections

Collections remains a main navigation item.

But its role changes materially. See the Collections section below.

## Keep the founder photograph

The current About page portrait is professional and works within the restrained layout.

It can remain unless a new photo is intentionally commissioned later.

# 3. What must change

## The target audience changes

Current site target:

- collectors;
- families;
- general readers interested in private collections.

New primary target:

1. trusts-and-estates attorneys;
2. professional fiduciaries;
3. trustees and trust officers;
4. private trust companies;
5. family offices;
6. other estate and art specialists who can refer matters.

Collectors and family members are secondary.

The site should speak the language of professional responsibility, not collector aspiration.

## The core action changes

Current CTA:

> considering the future of a collection?

New CTA:

> discussing a matter.

Preferred CTA language:

- **Discuss a matter**
- **Contact Nariway**
- **See how Nariway works**
- **View the deliverables**

Avoid:

- Book a demo
- Get started
- Unlock
- Transform
- Journey
- Discovery call
- Learn more if a more specific CTA is available

## Research is no longer the business

Do not lead with:

- research institute;
- Nariway studies;
- research and advisory;
- publishing;
- conversations;
- thought leadership.

Research can support:

- Collections;
- occasional professional resources;
- service credibility.

But the homepage must unmistakably say:

> **professional service first**

## Remove Artobiography from the main Nariway experience

Artobiography may remain a separate property elsewhere, but it should not occupy a footer column on the Nariway professional-services site.

It creates category confusion.

Do not delete external work without instruction, but remove it from the primary Nariway website hierarchy.

## Conversations should no longer dominate the homepage

The existing interview archive can remain online if there are useful URLs, backlinks, or content worth preserving.

But:

- remove the large Conversations module from the homepage;
- do not make Conversations a primary nav item;
- do not make visitors interpret Nariway as an editorial platform.

Possible treatment:

- preserve existing conversation pages;
- keep them accessible through search or a low-priority Research index later;
- selectively link to relevant interviews from Collections or About if useful.

> **Nariway is paid only by the estate or fiduciary for its professional services. We do not receive auction commissions, dealer commissions, referral fees, or a percentage of sale proceeds.**

# 6. Voice and copy rules

## Tone

Use plain, confident language.

Avoid inflated vocabulary.

Avoid marketing clichés.

Avoid abstract claims that cannot be demonstrated.

Avoid long philosophical openings.

Avoid sounding like an art historian.

Avoid sounding like a law firm.

Avoid sounding like a software company.

## Preferred sentence style

Short to medium-length sentences.

Specific nouns.

Concrete verbs.

Direct explanations.

Professional but not stiff.

## Avoid these words and tendencies

Do not use:

- unlock;
- transform;
- journey;
- visionary;
- elite;
- thrive;
- illuminate;
- ignite;
- delve;
- unravel;
- tapestry;
- beckons;
- blueprint;
- ecosystem when a simpler word works;
- seamless unless literally describing an integration;
- bespoke everywhere;
- white-glove everywhere;
- cutting-edge;
- revolutionary;
- AI-powered as a headline claim.

No emojis.

No hashtags.

Avoid titles with colons.

## Titles should be simple

Good:

- When a private collection becomes an estate matter
- From appraisal to resolution
- What Nariway controls
- The record behind the decision
- How Nariway works
- What the estate receives
- Independent by design
- How private collections change hands

Avoid:

- Nariway: The Future of Fiduciary Art Management
- The Process: From Appraisal to Resolution

# 8. Homepage strategy

The homepage should answer seven questions in order:

1. What is Nariway?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. How does it work?
5. What do I actually receive?
6. What does it cost / what kinds of engagements exist?
7. Why should I trust Nariway?

Do not begin with a long macro market story.

The $992 billion transfer is useful supporting context, but the homepage should lead with the service.

A simplified table or diagram:

|---|---|
| Estate attorney | Legal advice |
| Fiduciary | Decisions and approvals |

## Section 5 — Process

### Eyebrow

HOW IT WORKS

### Heading

# From appraisal to resolution

Show seven steps.

1. **Establish the control record**  
   Organize the in-scope works, existing documents, values, experts, beneficiaries, and active disposition paths.

2. **Structure the expert inputs**  
   Record who supplied each value or opinion, for what purpose, and from which source.

3. **Compare the alternatives**  
   Normalize auction proposals, private offers, and other disposition paths so their terms can be understood on the same basis.

4. **Document the decision**  
   Capture the information considered and the fiduciary's selected action.

5. **Track execution**  
   Follow contracts, shipments, sales, donations, distributions, and outstanding items.

6. **Reconcile the result**  
   Tie settlement statements, expenses, transfers, and receipts back to the estate record.

7. **Close the art workstream**  
   Produce the final Collection Resolution Report and unresolved-exception schedule.

**Complete**  
Bring the relevant collection, value, expert, beneficiary, and disposition information into one controlled matter record.

Track source evidence, approvals, custody, deadlines, and unresolved exceptions.
