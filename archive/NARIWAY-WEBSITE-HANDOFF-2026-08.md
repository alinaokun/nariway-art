# NARIWAY WEBSITE HANDOFF — August 2026

**Purpose.** A dated snapshot to brief a separate website-design agent on the *current* Nariway, without copying the internal vault. This is a briefing, **not a new canonical source**. The strategy files inside the Nariway working project remain canonical (see the Source Map at the end). Where those files genuinely disagree, this document flags it rather than picking a winner.

**How to use it.** Understand the company from this; do not treat any line here as final website copy. Live public copy is maintained canonically elsewhere (see Source Map, positioning). When in doubt, under-claim.

## 1. What Nariway is now

**Current definition.** Nariway is an independent strategic advisory concerned with the future of significant private art collections, what becomes of them, and the different ways they move into public life. Art is the *domain*. The professional *function* is strategic decision-making about the long-term fate of a collection.

**Problem territory.** A collector can spend decades building something remarkable. Eventually a different set of decisions begins: keep it in the family, donate it, sell it, lend it, divide it, or use it to create something new (a museum, a foundation, a university collection, a traveling program, or a model without a name yet). Nariway focuses on that decision, which is usually made badly, by default, or too late, and which no single existing professional clearly owns.

**Current positioning (the animating why above the what).**
- Vision (the why): *more of the world's great private art, in public view.* So much extraordinary art sits behind closed doors; more people could experience it. This is the purpose, not the product.
- Positioning (the what): Nariway focuses on the future of significant private collections and how they enter public life.
- The public language sits deliberately between "I study" (undersells, sounds like a researcher) and "I advise" (overclaims, the advisory service is not yet validated). Name the *territory and ambition*, not a priced, ready-to-buy service.

**What Nariway is NOT** (guard the copy hard here):
- Not an art advisor or dealer, not an appraiser, not a broker. It does not buy or sell art.
- Not an art writer, art historian, or curator (Artobiography is editorial, see §5, but it does not define the company).
- Not a museum-consulting or feasibility firm competing with Lord or AEA.
- Not an arts nonprofit or a foundation.
- Not a media company or a research institute.
- Not a mature consulting firm with a fixed service menu (see §3 and §9).

**How this differs from the old Nariway.** The prior Nariway (still live on the current sites) presented as an independent research institution about the *origins and future of modern working life* (the "history of work" identity, associated with the book *The Brief Experiment*). That identity is retired as the company's focus. The art-collections focus fully replaces it. The old work is legitimate history, not the current identity, and the site must not lead with it.

## 2. What we know vs. what is still hypothesis

Treat everything in this section as *research in progress*. The website must not present any finding as settled.

**Supported / well-evidenced (directional, externally sourced):**
- The wealth-transfer moment is real and large. A Deloitte model puts roughly $992B of art and collectibles changing ownership globally over about a decade, mostly by inheritance, **not** art coming to market. (Model, not a count. Never say "$992B of art will be sold.")
- Retention is the norm. Most inherited art is kept, not dispersed. So the core problem is an *unmanaged, unadvised transition*, not a fire sale. ("Retained" and "unmanaged" are two separate findings; do not fuse them.)
- The first post-inheritance decision-maker is often a surviving spouse, most often a woman (US spousal transfer, Cerulli).

**Challenged / corrected assumptions:**
- The opportunity is **not** primarily "help collectors build new museums." Founding a standalone institution is a small, fragile tail, not the mainstream outcome. Do not build the site around museum-creation.
- "Only ~6% donate to museums" is a misleading near-term figure; donation *intentions* are actually widespread but soft and long-horizon. What is genuinely rare is founding a new institution.

**Provisional internal finding (do NOT publish as fact):** across the coded case set, governance design at founding looks like it may predict survival better than endowment size. This is a hypothesis under test, not a claim to put on the website.

**H7A vs H7B (the crux, and why the site must be humble):**
- **H7A (market gap):** collectors may lack a recognized *independent* advisor who owns the full range of long-term pathway decisions before a disposition is locked. Plausible, not proven.
- **H7B (willingness to pay):** whether collectors or their families will pay meaningful fees for that independent role. **Untested.** You can be right about H7A and still have no business. Only a real buyer responding to a real offer proves H7B, and that has not happened yet.

**The website must not overstate:** any settled finding or statistic as Nariway's own proven claim; that Nariway creates museums; that the advisory is a validated, priced service; that there is a team or firm behind it; or the transfer numbers beyond their careful framing.

## 3. Business model status

**Current monetization hypotheses (three active, all low confidence, zero revenue to date):**
1. High-value collection / public-legacy advisory (bespoke engagements, retainers).
2. A bounded, priced feasibility or legacy study (the cleanest thing to eventually price-test; external comparables exist).
3. Research / media as a long-term secondary (Artobiography, and a future data or benchmarking product off the case database).

**Most promising:** the bounded feasibility/legacy study (#2) is the most defensible thing to *eventually* price-test, because comparable services have documented price anchors. The scalable ambition is a productized research product or data subscription off the proprietary case database, that is the leg that could take a solo practice toward its revenue goal.

**Not commercially validated:** all of it. Nariway has earned nothing. No offer has been priced to a real prospect. Willingness to pay is entirely untested.

**Website implication:** the site must **not** look like a mature consulting firm's service site. No pricing, no packaged offerings, no "our services" grid, no case-study testimonials, no "engage us" funnel implying a proven practice. Doing so would overclaim a business that is still in discovery and would misrepresent where things stand.

## 4. The research program

**Master question.** What happens to significant private art collections over time, what makes some of them suitable for public life, and what new institutional models can bring more privately held art into meaningful public view?

**Four inquiry areas (framed for understanding, not all public):**
- **Transfer** — what significant art is approaching a change in ownership (the wealth-transfer analysis; the timely spine).
- **Models** — how privately held art is already being brought into public life (small foundations, exhibition spaces, art parks, university partnerships, loan programs, seasonal venues, and abandoned or changed museum plans).
- **People / Collection Intelligence** — which collections may face a public-life decision, mapped from public sources only. **Internal. Not for the website.**
- **Potential** — what kinds of collections create compelling, sustainable public experiences, scored on two axes: institutional viability (can it support an institution) and cultural opportunity (should it become public, what gap does it fill).

**Collection pathways (public-appropriate as an educational frame):** standalone museum, foundation, house museum, long-term loan, gift to an existing institution, merger into an institution, intentional dispersal, traveling collection, art park, and models without a name yet. The point the site *can* make: a standalone museum is only one of many outcomes, and often not the right one.

**Role of case research.** Nariway is building a structured, sourced database of how significant collections entered public life and how they fared. It is the evidence base and the eventual moat. **The dataset itself is proprietary.** Its existence and its themes can signal credibility publicly; the coded data, sources, and per-case detail stay internal.

**Institution Building and the Decision Map (proprietary method, not published in detail).** Nariway's distinctive role is helping a collector *determine which public-life model makes sense before committing to one*, rather than executing a museum they have already decided on. Internally this is a decision lifecycle (suitability, then comparing forms while testing viability in a loop, then structure, build, operate, sustain) plus a bench of independent specialists. This is the advisory's intellectual property and its future method. The website may gesture at *how Nariway thinks* (independent, sequence-aware, sees the whole decision, not just one specialist's slice) but should not publish the lifecycle, the dependency rules, or the specialist bench.

**Public vs proprietary, summarized:**
- Public-appropriate: the master question as an inquiry, the pathways taxonomy as education, the forthcoming flagship report as a credibility signal, the general themes (alternative models are rising; a standalone museum is rarely the answer).
- Proprietary/internal: the coded case database and sources, Collection Intelligence, the Decision Map detail, the specialist bench, the business model, the hypotheses' internal naming.

**A forthcoming flagship report** (working title *What Becomes of Great Art Collections*) is planned as a free, data-dense annual, the top-of-funnel credibility asset. It is **not yet published.** The site may say something is coming; it must not imply it already exists or cite findings from it.

## 5. Artobiography

**Current editorial description (canonical live line):** *The stories behind remarkable art collections, and what becomes of them.*

**Role within Nariway.** Artobiography is the editorial front door and credibility engine, an independent publication (on Substack) published *by* Nariway. It is how Alina builds authority, relationships, and reach in the domain.

**How it differs from Nariway.** Nariway is the advisory and research company (the *what becomes of collections* decision). Artobiography is the publication that tells the stories. Artobiography is editorial; it is **not** the definition of the company and is not assumed to be the primary revenue model.

**Current content model, two forms:**
- Deeply researched collection features (the lives of remarkable collections and the people who built them, and what happened next).
- Conversations: shorter written interviews with the people who shape what happens to collections (collectors, founders, museum leaders, curators, advisors, conservators, attorneys, specialists).

**What the site should imply:** that Nariway has a serious editorial voice and a growing body of work; route interested readers to Artobiography. **What it should not imply:** that Nariway *is* a media outlet or art-writing shop, or that Artobiography is the business. Keep the advisory identity primary and the publication clearly secondary/supporting.

## 7. Primary audiences to design for

**Primary (speak to these first):** significant private collectors and their families who are, or will be, facing the question of what becomes of the collection. This is the intended buyer (a best-fit hypothesis, see §8 and §11).

**Referral / channel audiences (design to be *credible to*, but they are NOT the direct customer):** private-client and trusts-and-estates attorneys, family offices, wealth advisors, fine-art insurers, private banks. These are the professionals who sit near the decision and could refer it. The site should reassure them that Nariway is independent, serious, and does not compete with them.

**Secondary audiences:** museum and foundation leaders; specialists (advisors, appraisers, conservators, planners); journalists and researchers.

**A distinct, broader audience:** Artobiography readers. The site should give them a clear path to the publication without letting the editorial audience blur the advisory identity.

*Note from recent evidence:* the family-office world, though a natural channel, is a genuinely tough, transactional crowd even for full-time specialists. Lead the site's warmth and credibility with the art side (where Alina has native standing), and let the professional channels be reassured rather than courted aggressively.

## 9. Website implications already established

**What the site must accomplish now:**
- Establish the *current* Nariway clearly (art-collections focus), and retire the old history-of-work identity.
- Describe the territory (the future of significant private collections and their move into public life) with authority and restraint.
- Establish founder credibility (the finance-meets-art story).
- Route readers to Artobiography.
- Read as serious, independent, and trustworthy to both collectors and the professional channels.

**What should be deferred:**
- A full advisory "services" build, pricing, packaged offerings, and any funnel implying a proven practice.
- Publishing the flagship report or its findings (not yet public).
- Any productized data product.

**What must remain flexible:** who exactly pays (B2C vs B2B), and what the advisory ultimately promises. Do not hard-code a service definition the discovery phase has not settled.

**Safe claims:** Nariway focuses on the future of significant private art collections and how they move into public life; it is independent; it does not buy or sell; it brings a finance-and-strategy lens to a field that rarely has one; it publishes Artobiography; research is underway.

**Premature claims:** any priced or named service; that Nariway builds museums; that any finding is settled; that there is a firm/team; specific statistics presented as Nariway's proven claims.

**Advisory visibility, the decision:** **emerging / discreet, not explicit-as-a-mature-service and not entirely absent.** Present Nariway as focused on this decision and open to the right conversations, so a referrer understands what it does, without a sales pitch or a service menu. The interim guidance is that the site should describe what Nariway is *doing now* (researching this question and working with a small number of situations as they arise), framed as emerging, not established.

## 10. Visual / brand constraints

Only decisions actually made are listed. **Do not invent a visual identity beyond these.**
- Tone: composed, authoritative, curatorial, warm without being sentimental, plain and confident. It should sound like the person who wrote the essays, speaking plainly.
- Restraint over flash. No corporate-consulting cliché, no hard-sell.
- Prose fingerprint (applies to any copy): no em dashes, no colons inside sentences, no "And"/"But" sentence starts, no hedging, no cheap intensifiers. This is a firm founder standard; honor it in all site text.
- No committed palette, typography, or logo system has been finalized for the new identity. The visual direction is open, so the design agent has room, but it should serve a serious, museum-adjacent, credible register rather than a startup or arts-blog look.
- (An internal strategy deck used a stone / oxblood / serif "museum" treatment. That is a reference for *register*, not a mandated brand system.)

## 11. Open strategic questions (genuinely unresolved, and relevant to the site)

- **Who actually pays,** the collector directly (B2C) or the family office / estate (B2B). Documents hold this open; "primarily B2C" is a working hypothesis. The site must stay flexible here.
- **What the advisory concretely offers and at what price.** Undefined by design until discovery resolves it. Do not let the site fix a service definition.
- **How visible the advisory should be** beyond "emerging/discreet" is still being felt out; err discreet.
- **Whether the founder-researcher framing or a more advisory-forward framing wins over time.** Currently researcher-leaning by choice, appropriate for the evidence phase, but it may shift. Build so the emphasis can move without a redesign.
- **Naming clarity to preserve (not a conflict, but easy to muddle):** three distinct lines exist and should not be blurred, the company tagline (*significant private art collections, and what comes next*), the Artobiography description (*the stories behind remarkable art collections, and what becomes of them*), and the forthcoming report's working title (*What Becomes of Great Art Collections*). Keep the report title off the company's masthead so it is not mistaken for the company's tagline.

*No hard contradictions were found between the current strategy files. The genuine unknowns are the buyer identity and the advisory definition, both intentionally unresolved and both handled above.*
