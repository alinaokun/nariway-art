# 1. What Nariway is now

Nariway is a **founder-led art estate consulting practice**.

Alina's professional title is:

**Alina Okun, CPA**  
**Founder and Art Estate Consultant**

Do not use CGMA.

Nariway works with estate attorneys, fiduciaries, trustees, families, and other professional advisors when a significant art collection becomes part of an estate.

The key strategic change is that Nariway is **not launching with a rigid service menu or a fully predetermined delivery model**.

The business should remain broad enough to respond to the needs of each matter.

A useful external description is:

> **Nariway works with estate attorneys, fiduciaries and families when a significant art collection becomes part of an estate.**

A more detailed explanation:

> **A significant collection can involve many moving parts and many professionals. An art estate consultant works across those moving parts, helping the client keep the collection, the decisions and the people around it connected.**

Possible areas of involvement may include:

- collection information and documentation
- appraisals and outside specialists
- disposition and donation options
- beneficiary and family interests
- logistics, storage and movement
- decisions, approvals and follow-through
- other art-estate needs that emerge in a client matter

These are examples, not a fixed scope.

**Every matter is different.**

# 2. Strategic posture

Nariway is still in a market-learning and practice-building stage.

Do not behave as though the business model is fully proven.

The goal now is to:

1. establish the category **Art Estate Consultant**
2. meet estate-planning and fiduciary professionals
3. learn what problems they actually want help solving
4. accept suitable engagements
5. build the needed service around real client needs
6. observe which work repeats
7. standardize only after repetition proves what should become a repeatable service
8. automate only after the underlying work has been proven manually

This is intentionally different from the prior plan that tried to define detailed services, pricing tiers, deliverables, and operating workflows before client work existed.

The new governing principle is:

> **The practice should be shaped by real matters, not by hypothetical process design.**

And for automation:

> **Automation follows proven work. It does not precede it.**

# 5. What Nariway is not

Nariway is not being positioned as:

- a traditional art advisor
- an appraiser
- an auction house
- a dealer
- a law firm
- a tax advisory firm
- an estate administrator
- a fiduciary accountant
- a collection-management company
- a software platform
- an art-history consultancy
- a curator or connoisseurship practice

Nariway may work alongside all of these professionals where appropriate.

Useful role distinction:

### Art Appraiser
Determines value for tax, insurance, charitable, or other appraisal purposes

### Art Advisor
Provides art-market and collection advice

### Auction House / Dealer
Markets, buys, or sells art

### Estate Attorney
Provides legal advice and handles estate matters

### Fiduciary
Makes or carries out decisions within the fiduciary role

### Nariway
Works across the art-estate matter to help keep the collection-related work, decisions, specialists, and information connected

Do not over-defend boundaries in client-facing materials. Internally, preserve them carefully.

# 6. Independence and compensation

Nariway should maintain a clean fee structure.

Preferred language:

> **Nariway is paid only by the estate or fiduciary. We do not receive auction commissions, dealer commissions, referral fees, or a percentage of sale proceeds.**

This is a structural feature of the business.

Do not accept compensation that creates an economic incentive toward a specific auction house, dealer, buyer, appraiser, or disposition path without a deliberate future strategy change.

Potential supporting language:

> **Professional fees may be paid from the estate where appropriate, subject to counsel's guidance and the circumstances of the matter.**

Do not claim that Nariway fees are always deductible, always chargeable to the estate, or always treated as an estate-administration expense.

Do not use the phrase "conflict-free."

# 10. Go-to-market

The near-term GTM model is relationship-driven.

Primary channels:

- Estate Planning Councils
- NAEPC ecosystem
- trusts-and-estates attorneys
- trust officers
- fiduciaries
- private trust companies
- family-office professionals
- relevant art professionals and referral partners

The website is primarily a credibility and explanation tool, not a mass-lead engine.

The most important conversations are live professional conversations.

A useful networking opening:

> **I'm an art estate consultant. I work with estate attorneys and fiduciaries when an estate includes a significant art collection.**

If asked what that means:

> **The art can become a complicated part of the estate. There may be different valuations, several specialists, beneficiary interests, sale or donation options, and a lot of documentation moving at once. I help keep that side of the matter connected and moving forward.**

If asked what Nariway actually does:

> **It depends on the matter. I might help organize the collection information, work with appraisers and art professionals, compare different options, track open questions and decisions, coordinate logistics, or follow the collection through whatever ultimately happens to it.**

If asked why this is needed:

> **There often isn't one person looking across the whole art side of the estate. Each professional handles their piece, but someone still has to keep the moving parts connected.**

The goal of networking is not to explain every service.

The goal is to get the other professional to connect Nariway to a real matter they have seen.

# 11. Client-discovery principle

Early client work should be treated partly as service discovery.

When a prospective client asks:

> "Can you help with X?"

the first question is not:

> "Is X already in our service menu?"

The questions are:

- Is this within the broad art-estate consulting domain?
- Is it lawful and appropriate for Nariway to do?
- Does it require a licensed or specialized professional instead?
- Can Alina credibly learn, coordinate, or build what is needed?
- Is the client willing to engage Nariway for the work?
- Can the work be delivered to a professional standard?

If yes, Nariway can consider building the service around the matter.

This mirrors how many professional-services firms evolve: client demand reveals the service line.

Do not over-standardize before this learning occurs.

- wills or trust excerpts
- financial information
- beneficiary information
- appraisal reports
- insurance records
- collection inventories
- transaction proposals
- settlement documents
- addresses and locations
- private correspondence

Therefore:

- generic research agents should not crawl client matters
- client documents should never flow automatically into public content
- publishing routines should have no access to client matter data
- use least-privilege access
- no broad autonomous agent should have unrestricted access to all client matters
- no public export should ever include client-matter content
- secure transfer methods should be used for sensitive documents
- confidentiality and document-retention rules should be deliberately established

Design this boundary before the first substantial matter arrives.

# 17. Automation policy

Current default is **OFF**.

Do not re-create the old Nariway machine under new names.

The prior research/publishing operating model had too many autonomous routines.

The current rule:

> **No automated process should exist unless a real recurring business need justifies it.**

Before creating or reactivating an agent/routine, ask:

1. What real work triggers this?
2. How often has the work occurred manually?
3. What problem does automation solve?
4. What data does it access?
5. What can it change?
6. What external side effects exist?
7. What happens if it fails?
8. Is human review required?
9. Is the process stable enough to automate?
10. Is this generating value or simply generating more work?

Do not create a Delivery agent yet.

Do not create recurring research just because information might be useful.

Do not create recurring founder/CFO/CMO/QA/network agents by default.

Prefer:

- manual work
- event-triggered checklists
- narrowly scoped tools
- explicit human invocation

until repetition proves automation is warranted.

### After a networking event

- note language that resonated
- schedule follow-up

### When a prospect emerges
- research prospect and firm
- clarify matter
- identify professional boundaries
- scope possible engagement
- draft engagement terms

### When a matter begins
- establish matter environment
- define scope
- gather documents
- identify specialists
- define current questions
- create a working matter record

### Before sending client work
- run matter-specific quality review
- verify sources
- confirm assumptions
- confirm boundaries

- confirm agreed work is complete
- assemble final matter record
- identify reusable internal learning
- update service-development notes
- do not transfer confidential client content into public research

A lightweight founder review can be done manually weekly or as needed.

Do not automate it until there is a reason.
