# Sample matter map — how Nariway takes a collection from confusion to a plan

*Illustrative, constructed demonstration based on common patterns. **Not a real client.** Anonymized composite, no real names, figures, or works. For use in discovery conversations and referral introductions, to show an attorney or fiduciary what Nariway actually does. The structure mirrors the real matter model that will live in Nariway Intelligence; this is the sanitized marketing version. Pairs with [[offer-collection-transition-assessment]].*

## The matter at a glance
A collector dies. Nine months later the estate tax return is due, and the art is the least organized part of the estate.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Death of the collector; estate now in administration |
| **Collection** | ~180 works, modern and contemporary, spread across a residence, a second home, and commercial storage, with a few on loan |
| **Who is involved** | Executor, estate attorney, surviving spouse, two adult children, a prior appraiser, an auction house, a regional museum, an insurer, a shipper, a storage facility |
| **Nariway's role** | Independent coordinator, paid by the estate, no commissions |
| **The clock** | Estate tax return due in ~9 months; insurance and storage running monthly |

## Week 0: the confusion at intake
- **No one can say what exists.** Three partial inventories exist and none of them match; roughly a dozen works are unaccounted for on paper.
- **The values are the wrong values.** The only appraisal on file was done for insurance in 2019, not for estate tax, and it is stale.
- **The records are scattered.** Provenance, purchase records, and condition reports live in email, a filing cabinet, and the appraiser's files.
- **The museum gift is assumed, not confirmed.** The family believes a regional museum "would love" a large gift. The museum has not said what it would actually accept.
- **The family does not agree.** One heir wants to keep everything, another wants to sell, and the spouse wants certain works to stay together.
- **The attorney is coordinating art specialists** on top of the legal work, which is not their job and not their expertise.

## What the Collection Transition Assessment produced (a few weeks)
- **A consolidated inventory and information-gap map.** 180 works located and reconciled; 12 flagged as unaccounted, with a plan to resolve each.
- **A stakeholder map.** Who is involved, what each person controls, and who decides what.
- **A decision log** (excerpt below): every open question, the options, and who owns the next step.
- **A risk-and-deadline list.** The estate tax valuation deadline, two expiring loans, and an underinsured group in storage.
- **A preliminary path per group of works:** retain, sell, donate, store, move, or appraise further.
- **A recommended specialist team, with no commissions taken:** a qualified estate-tax appraiser, a conservator for two damaged works, the shipper, and the museum contact.

## The decision log (excerpt)
| Question | Options | Owner of next step | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Which works get a qualified estate-tax appraisal? | The ~40 works above the threshold | Nariway scopes; appraiser executes | Path set |
| Is the museum gift real? | Confirm what the museum will actually accept | Nariway opens the conversation | In progress |
| What does the family keep? | Options analysis for the spouse and two heirs | Family decides; Nariway frames the choices | Framed, pending decision |
| The 12 unaccounted works | Locate, confirm loans, or write off | Nariway runs each down | Open |

## The outcome
- The **museum gift was scoped to the 22 works the institution confirmed it would accept**, not the 60 the family assumed, before anyone spent money preparing a gift that would have been declined.
- A **qualified estate-tax appraisal was commissioned for the right works**, in time for the return.
- The **heirs aligned** around a clear options analysis instead of a standoff.
- The **attorney stopped coordinating art specialists** and got back a documented, defensible record of what exists, what is decided, and what is still open.
- The matter **moved**, on the estate's deadlines rather than despite them.

## What this shows
Reduced confusion, fewer expensive mistakes (the declined gift that never happened), faster decisions, hours of attorney and fiduciary time returned, and a defensible process, at exactly the moment money, identity, and family are most entangled. Nariway did not sell the art, take a commission, or give legal or tax advice. It kept the system connected and helped the people involved decide.
