# Flagship Report — the annual "State of" for art and legacy (initiative)

The model is Nathan Benaich's **[State of AI Report](https://www.stateof.ai/)**: a free, data-dense, authoritative annual that made Air Street Capital the reference name in its field and drove inbound deal flow. Alina wants the equivalent at the intersection of **art and finance**, a consistent, meaningful flagship that creates a unique niche for Nariway and positions it as *the* source of this kind of data. This is the productized, scalable leg the CFO keeps asking for, the distinctive asset the CMO wants, and the purpose that finally gives the case database a point. Owned jointly by CMO (positioning and distribution), Research (rigor), and Alina (voice and judgment).

## The niche it owns (why it is defensible)
The art market is already covered, Art Basel and UBS, Deloitte, TEFAF report on sales and prices. **Nariway's report is about what happens to significant collections *beyond* the market**: the fate of great private collections, the pathways into public life (museum, foundation, university, loan, dispersal, failure), and what makes them last or fall apart. No one owns that lane. The timely spine is the **Great Art Transfer** (the ~$1T wave, see [[market-intelligence]] #1), which puts "what becomes of the collection" at the center of a story everyone is suddenly telling. Alina's finance eye supplies the analytical rigor the art world rarely brings to it.

## The proprietary asset (this is the moat)
The report's edge is data no one else has: **Nariway's structured database of how significant collections entered public life and how they fared** ([[candidate-universe]], ~70+ cases coded by pathway, founder status, geography, thesis, outcome, scale, durability). Aggregated, it answers questions the field only tells anecdotally: how often collector-founded museums survive their founder, what predicts durability, how fast the alternative models (dispersal, long-term loan, foundation) are rising, where the failures cluster. **The report is the public surface of that database.** That is why completing and verifying the case studies matters now, it is not open-ended research, it is building the dataset behind the report.

## What is in it
- The transfer and the money, sourced (the ~$1T, market size, art finance, the spousal-and-women shift), all from the [[claims-register]].
- The database, visualized: pathway distribution, survival vs failure, founder-living vs deceased, geography, scale, timelines, durability drivers (the H8 question).
- The models, explained through cases, the museum, the foundation, the university gift, the loan library, the intentional dispersal, the failure.
- A view, and predictions, scored the following year (the State of AI move that builds credibility over time).
- Charts and design throughout. This is a designed asset, not a memo.

## How it uses everything already built
Case database = the evidence. Claims-register and market-intelligence = the data. Positioning = the niche. Artobiography = the narrative voice. Her finance background = the analysis. It is the output of the whole flywheel, which is why it is coherent rather than a bolt-on.

## Why it serves the goal, and the honest risk
Like State of AI feeding Air Street, a free authoritative report is a top-of-funnel engine: attention, credibility, speaking, inbound advisory, and eventually a payable data or benchmarking product off the same database. It is how a solo advisory becomes findable and gets to a million.
**The risk the CFO and Skeptic raise:** a giant annual report is the ultimate enrichment black hole. Benaich's first report was lean and his reputation compounded over years. Building a 200-slide flagship for an untested audience, while no client conversation has happened, would be exactly the "activity that feels like progress" trap. So it is phased, and it runs *alongside* the market conversations (Dawn and the warm network), never instead of them.
