> **This file is the PLAN and STANDARDS. The actual evolving report content lives in → [[what-becomes-of-great-art-collections]] (the living manuscript, WEF-style layout).** Open that one to read the report as it grows.

The model is Nathan Benaich's **[State of AI Report](https://www.stateof.ai/)**: a free, data-dense, authoritative annual that made Air Street Capital the reference name in its field and drove inbound deal flow. Alina wants the equivalent at the intersection of **art and finance**, a consistent, meaningful flagship that creates a unique niche for Nariway and positions it as *the* source of this kind of data. Owned jointly by CMO (positioning and distribution), Research (rigor), and Alina (voice and judgment).

## The niche it owns (why it is defensible)
The art market is already covered, Art Basel and UBS, Deloitte, TEFAF report on sales and prices. **Nariway's report is about what happens to significant collections *beyond* the market**: the fate of great private collections, the pathways into public life (museum, foundation, university, loan, dispersal, failure), and what makes them last or fall apart. No one owns that lane. The timely spine is the **Great Art Transfer** (see [[claims-register]] C1, ~$992B, and [[market-intelligence]] #1), which puts "what becomes of the collection" at the center of a story everyone is suddenly telling. Alina's finance eye supplies the analytical rigor the art world rarely brings to it.

## The proprietary asset (this is the moat)
The report's edge is data no one else has: **Nariway's structured database of how significant collections entered public life and how they fared** ([[candidate-universe]], ~70+ cases coded by pathway, founder status, geography, thesis, outcome, scale, durability). Aggregated, it answers questions the field only tells anecdotally: how often collector-founded museums survive their founder, what predicts durability, how fast the alternative models (dispersal, long-term loan, foundation) are rising, where the failures cluster. **The report is the public surface of that database.** That is why completing and verifying the case studies matters now, it is not open-ended research, it is building the dataset behind the report.

**North star (Alina, 2026-08-14): research nobody else has, at a McKinsey / BCG / WEF standard.** The moat is not row count, it is (1) **primary sources the field is too lazy to pull** (true endowment from IRS 990 Schedule D via the 990 PDF/XML, FTE, deeds, wills, probate) and (2) **original cross-case analysis** (governance-beats-endowment, the failure modes, the data-availability finding). The dataset-research agent now runs **twice daily** (5am and 5pm ET), with the second pass weighted to that proprietary depth once breadth nears 50. Quality is the only limit, never fabricate; verify or mark unknown.

## The strategy has evolved — the research IS the product (2026-08-20, external review + Alina)
The manuscript has crossed a line. With the coded cases, the [[claims-register]], primary-source pulls, the deliberate disconfirmation passes, primary interviews, and the qualitative-vs-statistical discipline, this is no longer a content-marketing report you finish, promote, and move on from. **It is becoming Nariway's core intellectual asset.** So flip the model:

- **Old:** research → report → PR → attention → next report.
- **New:** continuous research → proprietary knowledge base ([[what-we-now-believe|Evidence Base]] + [[report-dataset]]) → an annual **State of the Private Collection** → [[conversations-series|Conversations]] as field research → media → advisory relationships → more primary knowledge → next edition. **The report is the visible surface of a much larger thing underneath.** The category to own: *the organization that systematically studies what happens to private collections over time.* The art market is well documented; what happens to a collection after it leaves its owner is not, and that unowned territory is more defensible than "thought leadership."

**Four operating changes that follow (all endorsed, with guardrails):**

1. **Name the frameworks Nariway is discovering — but only after they survive a break-attempt.** Candidates already in the work: *governance collapse vs money collapse* (narrowed by [[corcoran-gallery]], see the caution below), *public ownership vs public access* (the multidimensional public-life model, [[field-definition]]), *the five ways corporate collections disperse or endure*, *founder constraints matter by scope, not existence*, and *a pathway that avoids one hard decision often just puts it on a clock* (lending, fractional gifts). Three or four, earned, not eleven, and **not gimmicky branding.** **Discipline: a concept earns a name only after a deliberate attempt to disprove it fails** — exactly what the Corcoran pass did to the governance claim (126 years of genuine independent governance, still died of money, so the broad "governance ≈ survival" version would have been a mis-named framework). Names ride on survived disconfirmation, per [[what-we-now-believe]].
2. **Move from explaining the world to explaining the DECISION.** The pathways chapter currently lists what a collection can become. The far more valuable (and advisory-adjacent) destination is *under what conditions does each pathway make sense* — the decision architecture. This is the [[what-we-now-believe|variables framework]] already flagged "discover-don't-invent"; the report's eventual headline is not "here are eleven things collections become" but "here is what N collection histories suggest about which structures work under which conditions." Do not publish a formula; demonstrate that Nariway understands the decision architecture better than someone meeting it for the first time.
3. **Build an escalating public conversation that CULMINATES in the report** (not finish → publish → PR). Individual findings become stories *before* launch, so that by publication day the right journalists, attorneys, advisors, and collectors already know *Nariway is studying this*. Candidate angles: the $992B transfer is a retention story, not a sale story; why museums say no; the likeliest heir to America's great collections is a woman; borrowing against art doesn't solve succession; why money isn't enough to keep a private museum alive. **Guardrail: pre-release ONLY findings that already clear the report's citation bar** (sourced, confidence-tagged). The museum-rejection number is NOT yet launch-ready (H2 medium, decline rate unknown, Leslie unanswered); a soft number sent to a journalist burns the credibility that is the whole asset.
4. **Redefine what media success means, and gate the whole strategy on it.** Not article count. The report exists to create: **authority** (Nariway = this problem), **access** (people who won't take the call today start to), **primary information** (collectors and professionals bring cases and observations), **referral relationships** (attorneys/family offices/advisors/museum people know when Nariway is relevant), and **clients** (someone recognizes their own situation). **The fifth is the gate.** The report makes the business case almost without trying — it finds many professionals around the collection but no one paid to independently own the whole keep/sell/donate decision before a crisis (H7A). That is Nariway's opening, and it is far more persuasive discovered through the cases than asserted on a website.

**The honest risk (says it plainly, because the plan and the Evidence Base both already flag it).** "The research is the product" is intellectually thrilling, which is exactly why it is dangerous. The [[what-we-now-believe|Evidence Base]] already names the deepest risk — *it has become more satisfying to build Nariway than to operate it* — and "let the findings stabilize before publishing" is correct research discipline **and** a license to keep building instead of selling. So this reframe is only valid if it **converts**: the escalating public conversation must be pointed at generating inbound situations and referral relationships, not citations, and the report-strategy scoreboard is the [[problem-discovery]] signal ladder ending in *someone asks Nariway to help with a real situation* — never "how many outlets covered it." Run this **alongside** the market conversations, never instead of them.

**The second workstream this opens — "Nariway Report Strategy" (planning, not writing).** Distinct from authoring the manuscript. Its four questions: (1) what can Nariway legitimately claim to know that nobody else has systematically studied? (2) which 3–5 original frameworks should come out of this research? (3) which findings go public before the report, and which are held for launch? (4) how does the report bridge from "fascinating research" to "Nariway is who I'd call if my family faced this decision"? Do NOT interrupt the research engine or rush the report; the jump from 50→65 cases already refined several findings, so let the majors stabilize. Start this planning layer in parallel.

## Standards (the credibility bar, non-negotiable)
The report is held to the caliber of McKinsey, BCG, and WEF research. Credibility is the whole asset, without it there is nothing.
- **Every number and claim is sourced**, with a citation and a confidence tag. Nothing uncited enters a chart or the prose. No made-up numbers, no unsubstantiated assumptions.
- **Source hierarchy, authoritative first:** (1) primary and official, IRS Form 990s (via ProPublica/IRS), museum annual reports and audited financials, official institution sites, legal and probate records, government data (IMLS); (2) reputable institutional research, Deloitte and ArtTactic, Art Basel and UBS, TEFAF, Larry's List, Cerulli, the museum associations (AAM, AAMD); (3) reputable press, The Art Newspaper, NYT, Artnet, Bloomberg; (4) scholarly work. Museum self-history and blogs are color only, never a cited fact.
- **Modeled figures are always labeled as models** with their source (e.g. the $992B is a Deloitte model, not a count), per the [[claims-register]].
- **"Unknown" is a counted value.** Every chart caption states n and how many cases were unknown for that field. Honesty about gaps is a feature.
- **A citation audit before publish:** every claim traceable to its source, no exceptions.
