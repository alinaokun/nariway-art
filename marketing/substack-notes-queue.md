**1. The collection that gave itself away**
> William Arnett spent almost thirty years building the largest collection of Black vernacular art in the American South. For a century, the art world had refused to take this work seriously.
>
> He could have put all of it in one museum with his name on the door. Instead he gave it away, one piece at a time, to more than twenty museums.
>
> He wanted these artists in the rooms that had shut them out. Now they are there.

---

**2. The art America cannot see**
> There is a large collection of American art that most Americans will never see. It hangs inside United States embassies, behind guarded doors, chosen to represent the country abroad.
>
> A small foundation in Connecticut has started letting the public in.
>
> The hard part was never owning the art. It was opening the door.

---

**3. She planned a museum she would never see**
> Marion Koogler McNay designed her San Antonio house down to the smallest detail, and painted some of the wall stencils by hand. In her will, she asked that most of it be turned into a museum.
>
> It opened in 1954 with a Picasso show she did not live to see.
>
> She had built a future that did not include her. That is what it takes for a collection to outlive the person who made it.

---

**4. Then what**
> Most collectors assume that a life of collecting ends with a grateful museum taking everything.
>
> More and more, the museum says no. It may not have the space, the budget to care for the work, or a real reason the collection fits.
>
> So a question that waited for decades arrives all at once, and no one is standing next to the collector to help answer it.

---

**5. A postal clerk and a librarian**
> Herbert and Dorothy Vogel built one of the great collections of minimalist and conceptual art on a postal clerk's salary and a librarian's. They bought only what they could carry home on the subway.
>
> When it was time to let the collection go, they gave fifty works to a museum in each of the fifty states.
>
> It was never about money. It was about how closely two people were willing to look.

---

**6. The question the art world keeps not asking**
> The art world talks constantly about what a collection is worth. It rarely asks what becomes of it.
>
> A great collection can go to a museum, a foundation, or a university. It can travel as an exhibition, sell at auction, or simply come apart.
>
> Every serious collection reaches this fork, and almost no one is standing there when it does.

---

**7. Founding a museum is the easy part**
> Founding a museum is easy to celebrate. Keeping one alive for fifty years is not.
>
> One collection I have been studying became a sculpture park on two hundred acres in the Napa Valley. To stay open, it now sells its own art and rents the grounds for events.
>
> The building was never the achievement. It was the start of the problem.

---
