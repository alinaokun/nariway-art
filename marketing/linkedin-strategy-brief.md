# Nariway LinkedIn Strategy Brief

**Version:** August 30, 2026  
**Purpose:** Define Nariway's LinkedIn content strategy in a way that supports business development, protects the brand voice, and creates a practical path toward a future weekly content agent.

---

# 1. Why LinkedIn matters

LinkedIn is the priority social platform for Nariway.

This is where estate attorneys, fiduciaries, trust officers, wealth professionals, and other referral sources already spend time. It is therefore the platform most likely to support the actual business.

Instagram may later become a secondary distribution channel for art-facing audiences, but LinkedIn comes first.

The goal is not to become a generic "creator."

The goal is to become known for a very specific kind of thinking:

**Nariway tells the stories of significant private collections and the moments when they change hands, change form, or enter public life.**

That public presence should make people think:

- this is interesting
- this is unusually thoughtful
- I did not know that
- this person clearly understands collections and what happens to them over time
- this is someone I might contact if an estate matter involved art

---

# 2. The editorial lane

The content lane is narrow by design.

Nariway should not become:

- a general art-history account
- a legal commentary account
- a broad art-market-news account
- a motivational founder account
- a newsletter disguised as LinkedIn
- a personal diary
- a generic "thought leadership" feed

The editorial lane is:

## The story of a collection and what became of it

This can take two forms:

### A. Evergreen collection stories
Examples:
- George Lucas and the Lucas Museum of Narrative Art
- The Fisher Collection and SFMOMA
- Souls Grown Deep
- Anderson Collection at Stanford
- Hall Art Foundation
- Peter Marino Art Foundation
- UBS Art Collection
- Yale University Art Gallery and collection origins
- other founder, family, corporate, or institution-linked collections

### B. Current transition moments
Examples:
- a recent donation
- a museum gift
- a new foundation
- a collection transfer
- a university gift
- an estate-related sale
- a founder collection entering public life
- a major change in a corporate or family collection

Both formats answer the same question:

**How was the collection built, and what happened next?**

---

# 3. Content objective

The content should do five jobs at once:

1. Show that Nariway understands the world of private collections
2. Make the audience smarter in a way that feels rewarding
3. Build recognition with attorneys and fiduciaries
4. Create a body of public work that supports credibility
5. Generate conversations, introductions, and eventually client opportunities

The content is not primarily for immediate lead generation.

It is a long-term trust-building asset.

---

# 4. Voice and writing principles

This is the most important part.

The posts must not sound like AI slop.

They must not sound like generic "content."

They must not sound like a social-media strategist trying to engineer engagement.

They should feel:

- informed
- natural
- specific
- understated
- intelligent
- readable
- genuinely interesting

## Voice rules

### Use specific facts early
Do not open with generic "most people don't know" constructions.

Lead with something concrete:
- a person
- a collection
- an institution
- a date
- a place
- a choice
- a surprising fact

### Show significance through details
Do not say:
- "This is important because..."
- "This shows us that..."
- "What makes this interesting is..."

Let the significance emerge from the story.

### One idea per paragraph
Short paragraphs. Clean movement. Easy to read.

### Use a real voice, not a "brand voice"
The writing should sound like a well-informed person sharing something worth knowing.

### Avoid social-media gimmicks
No "3 lessons" unless it truly serves the content.
No fake suspense.
No clickbait.
No motivational endings.
No performance of vulnerability.
No empty "thought leadership."

### End cleanly
A post can end on:
- the strongest fact
- a restrained takeaway
- a broader observation about collections and their afterlife
- a short signature line if desired

Do not end every post with a big brand slogan.

---

# 5. What to learn from strong LinkedIn creators

The useful lessons from creators like Ben Meer and Jade Bonacolta are structural, not stylistic imitation.

## What works

### Clear hook
The first line earns attention.

### High readability
Short paragraphs, natural spacing, easy scanning.

### Informative substance
The post teaches something real.

### Distinct point of view
The writer has a recognizable way of seeing the topic.

### Consistency
Readers know roughly what kind of value to expect.

### Light signature
Jade's style suggests a quiet, repeatable closing mark can work.

## What to avoid copying

- personal-brand formulas
- over-optimized opening lines
- broad productivity framing
- manufactured vulnerability
- generic content templates
- excessive list posts

Nariway should learn from their clarity and authenticity, not imitate their content categories.

---

# 6. Post format

A typical LinkedIn post should follow this internal structure:

## 1. Hook
One concrete and interesting opening line

## 2. Story
Who built the collection, or what happened

## 3. Development
A few specific details that reveal why the case matters

## 4. Closing
A restrained final sentence that lets the reader feel the broader significance

Not every post needs all four parts explicitly, but most should roughly follow that arc.

---

# 7. Frequency

Near term:

**Start with 1 post per week**

This is the recommended starting point.

Do not build a full publishing machine immediately.

The goal at the start is:
- prove the voice
- prove consistency
- prove that the topics are resonating
- learn what feels sustainable

Once that works, Nariway can gradually move toward:
- 2 posts per week
- then potentially 3 posts per week

Longer term, it is reasonable to build an internal system that proposes **3-4 posts per week**, but this should happen only after the manual version is working.

---

# 8. Topic selection criteria

A story is a good candidate if it has at least two of the following:

- a distinctive origin story
- a known founder, family, or institution
- a meaningful transition or gift
- a collection that entered public life
- a collection tied to a company or broader cultural story
- a collection that reveals something about private ownership and legacy
- a case that most readers are unlikely to know in detail
- enough reliable facts to tell the story well

Avoid posts that are merely:
- generic art news
- celebrity art gossip
- auction headlines without context
- content that depends on weak sourcing
- stories with no real Nariway relevance

# 9. Visual strategy

Every LinkedIn post should ideally include a custom image in the Nariway visual language.

The image should feel:

- editorial
- quiet
- elevated
- clean
- immediately legible
- consistent with nariway.com

## Visual rules

Use:
- the same color family used on nariway.com
- the same or closely matched typography used on nariway.com
- strong white or warm-ivory space
- black or near-black serif headline
- refined sans serif support text
- one recognizable face or logo when appropriate
- one clean visual idea

Avoid:
- busy layouts
- generic icons
- content-creator graphics
- fake urgency
- too much text
- arrows
- loud effects
- templates that feel like Instagram infographics

# 10. Headline style for images

The image headline should be interesting, but not forced.

The best model is closer to the conversation titles already used on nariway.com.

Good examples:
- The Filmmaker Who Built a Museum for Narrative Art
- The Collector Who Gave a University Its Defining Collection
- The Corporate Collection That Became a Cultural Legacy
- What Happened to a Private Collection After the Founder
- The Family Collection That Entered Public Life

Avoid:
- shock headlines
- vague curiosity gaps
- "You won't believe"
- questions that sound like clickbait
- headlines that overpromise

The headline should feel like the title of a short essay.

---

# 11. Caption standards

A LinkedIn caption should usually be 3 to 7 short paragraphs.

It should:
- begin with a fact or story
- move with a natural rhythm
- include specifics
- avoid padding
- avoid generic conclusions
- sound written, not generated

A light signature line is acceptable if it remains subtle.

Example possibilities:
- Alina
- Alina Okun
- Nariway
- A quiet recurring line such as "Nariway"

Use sparingly. Do not let the signature become gimmicky.

---

# 12. Source discipline

Because the audience includes attorneys and other detail-oriented professionals, factual accuracy matters.

Before a post is finalized:
- verify the core facts
- identify primary or high-quality sources
- confirm dates, names, institutions, and major claims
- avoid stating speculative facts as certain

Accuracy matters more than speed.

A weaker but accurate post is better than a stronger but sloppy one.

---

# 13. Approval workflow

The final workflow should preserve human judgment.

## Target future workflow

Once the voice and visual system are stable, create an internal Nariway content agent that does the following once per week:

1. Reviews the Collections library and current Signals
2. Proposes 3-4 candidate LinkedIn posts
3. Drafts captions in the approved Nariway voice
4. Creates matching images in the approved Nariway visual system
5. Provides source notes for factual verification
6. Sends the package to Alina for approval
7. Makes no public post automatically

This is important:

**The agent prepares. Alina approves.**

No autonomous publishing.

---

# 14. Agent design principles

When the future content agent is built, it should follow these rules:

- LinkedIn first
- one editorial spine only
- no broad content-pillar sprawl
- no generic AI hooks
- no over-automated content calendar behavior
- every post must trace back to a specific collection or transition
- factual claims must be checkable
- visuals must use the Nariway design system
- outputs should be bundled weekly for review
- human approval required before any post is published

The system should generate better drafts faster. It should not replace judgment.

---

# 16. What success looks like

In the first stage, success is not follower growth.

Success is:
- the posts sound like Alina
- the visuals feel recognizably Nariway
- the stories are genuinely interesting
- attorneys and art people engage thoughtfully
- people begin to associate Nariway with serious knowledge of collections and their next chapters
- the content feels sustainable

Only after that should the system become more formal.

---

# 17. Final principle

The LinkedIn strategy should feel like a natural extension of Nariway's larger identity.

It should not become a separate content business.

The purpose is simple:

**Tell stories of significant private collections in a way that is specific, intelligent, visually refined, and worth reading.**
