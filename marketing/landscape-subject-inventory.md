## THE FIVE OUTPUTS

### 1. Complete mapped inventory
The tables above (macro + 5 territories + 2 cross-cutting dimensions). Decisions live inside The Future; Tax and Cross-border are dimensions surfaced within subjects, with the canonical-home fixes from the stress-test applied (structures→Succession, governance/durability→Future, rights→Ownership).

### 2. Publication-ready now (~12, all Strong + verified enough)
1. Great Wealth Transfer & art · 2. Collector demographics · 3. The cost of keeping art · 4. Valuation & appraisal · 5. Art finance & lending · 6. Estate planning · 7. Governance & institutional durability · 8. Ownership vs access (the frame) · 9. Build an institution · 10. Partner with an institution · 11. Give to an institution · 12. Disperse deliberately. (Insurance is a near-miss — ready as a sub of cost-of-keeping.)

### 3. Promising but need more research (Moderate)
Private-museum landscape & economics · market/liquidity & sale mechanics · heirs & family · keeping-together-vs-dividing · donor intent & cy pres · ownership structures & entities · insurance (as its own subject) · storage/logistics beyond cost · collection management (practice) · sell · public-access models · how-museums-work · significance & distinctiveness · art-as-asset. Charitable-deduction and capital-gains tax facets sit here too.

### 4. Major holes (None/Thin, positioning-critical)
- **The entire Collection front-end:** provenance (N), authenticity & attribution (T), condition (N), cataloguing (N), documentation (T). This is where a collector *starts* and Nariway is near-empty.
- **Rights** (copyright/resale/moral) — N.
- **Cross-border / jurisdiction** depth — T (only case-level); **cultural-property / AML / CITES** — N.
- **Security & loss** — N.
- **Retention / keep-in-family** — T, n=0 cases, despite being the *most common outcome*.
- **Restitution / cultural-policy environment** — T.

### 5. Where the architecture becomes awkward against the actual material
- **The Collection territory launches almost empty.** The architecture (correctly) leads with the reader's starting point, but Nariway has near-zero verified material there — so the front door is the emptiest room. Honest, but it must show as "in development," not be padded.
- **The Future territory overflows.** Most cases and most Strong subjects live there (4 of the ~12 publish-ready). Tested against the material, the architecture *still reveals the searchlight*: Nariway is strong at the *end* of a collection's life and empty at the *front*. Not an architecture failure — the architecture is doing its job by making the imbalance visible.
- **Tax as a "cross-cutting dimension" is real but lopsided** — the verified tax material clusters in estate/gift and charitable (Succession + Give), so in practice Tax leans heavily into those two, with capital-gains/international thin. The dimension is correct; the evidence behind it is uneven.
- **Cross-border is a dimension with no dedicated evidence** — it's entirely case-level (the internationals) with no researched treatment; it will read as a promise, not a resource, until researched.
- **The Value/Future seam on Sale** is genuine: sale *mechanics* (Value) and the *sell decision* (Future) split the sale story; the cases sit in Future, the market data in Value. Requires deliberate cross-linking or a reader researching "selling" lands half-informed.
- **Governance/durability** is canonically in The Future but half its evidence is succession-framed (Erskine, family), so it will be a heavily cross-linked hinge — workable, but not a clean single-territory subject.
- **The macro "Landscape"** holds several Strong, page-worthy subjects (wealth transfer, demographics, private-museum landscape) — confirming the stress-test finding that it behaves like a light territory, not pure ambient context.
