## The situation (what an attorney recognizes)
When a significant art collection becomes part of an estate, it is often the least organized part of the matter, even when it is among the most valuable and meaningful. Suddenly people need answers: what exactly exists and where, which values apply and for what purpose, what should be kept, sold, donated, lent, stored, moved, or divided among beneficiaries, who among the specialists is doing what, what the family actually wants, and which decisions are still unresolved.

## What I do
When a significant art collection becomes part of an estate, I help you and your client organize the collection-related decisions, coordinate the right specialists, and keep the matter moving. I do not sell the art, take auction commissions, or compete with your legal role. The independence is the point. I am paid only by the estate or fiduciary, so my only interest is a clear, defensible outcome.

## The entry engagement: a Collection Transition Assessment
A fixed-fee, time-boxed first engagement for a family, fiduciary, or attorney. In a few weeks it produces:

- A high-level collection inventory and an information-gap map (what we have, what is missing).
- A stakeholder map (who is involved, who decides what).
- A decision log (what is open, and who owns each next step).
- A short list of urgent risks and deadlines.
- A preliminary path for each part of the collection: retain, sell, donate, store, move, or appraise further.
- A recommended specialist team (appraiser, conservator, shipper, insurer, auction house, museum contact), with no commissions taken.

It makes the first step small and defined. No one has to commit to an open-ended relationship to get clarity.

## What it is not
Not selling the art. Not taking commissions or a percentage of proceeds. Not legal, tax, appraisal, authentication, or investment advice, all of which stay with the qualified professionals. Not a replacement for the attorney, the appraiser, or the art advisor. I keep the collection-related system connected and help the people involved make and execute decisions.

## Who it is for, and who pays
The client is ultimately the family, but the engagement is usually opened by the estate attorney or fiduciary who sees the trigger event first. The estate or fiduciary pays the fee.

## The fee
A fixed fee, scoped to the collection's size and complexity, currently starting around $5,000. Fixed rather than hourly, so the cost is known up front.

## Why it is worth it
The value is reduced confusion, fewer expensive mistakes, faster decisions, and better stewardship of assets that can be impossible to replace, at exactly the moment when money, identity, and family relationships are most entangled.

- **In a discovery conversation:** lead with "the situation" and "what I do," then listen. The [[market-learning]] discovery questions do the work; this offer is what you can point to when someone asks "so what would you actually do?"

- **Pricing is a hypothesis, not a fact.** Test the ~$5,000 anchor privately with real buyers before printing it anywhere public. Do not underprice because the work feels meaningful.
- Keep it neutral and human. The title to use in plain language is **art estate consultant**, not "advisor" (which implies buying and selling).
