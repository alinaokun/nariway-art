# Board Opportunities — a researched target list

*On-thesis nonprofit boards worth Alina's name, judged against the criteria in [[2026-08-14-board-membership]]. Built by agent research across the whole field (public sources), NOT limited to the BoardAssist database. Maintained ongoing by [[network-research]]. Board seats are usually filled through relationships, so each entry names the likely **approach path**, not an application link.*

## The bar (from the decision)
1. Arts / culture, ideally touching private collections entering public life.
2. A board thick with collectors, museum/foundation leaders, and the advisor ecosystem.
3. Where Alina's finance + strategy + governance is genuinely needed and valued.
4. **Low or no give-or-get** given the $0-revenue picture (skills as the contribution). Big-give boards deferred until there is revenue.
5. Mission-aligned (public access to art).
6. A research dividend, an institution grappling with sustainability or succession.
*Avoid: off-thesis civic/social boards; large financial minimums for now. NY-metro (drivable from Montvale) preferred; strong virtual/national fits welcome.*

## Candidates
*(Populated by the research pass, 2026-08-14. Each: institution, why it fits, give/get if public, and the approach path. Ranked strongest-fit first. Give/get is marked "not public" wherever no dollar figure is documented — none was guessed.)*

### 1. Katonah Museum of Art (Katonah, NY)
[katonahmuseum.org](https://www.katonahmuseum.org/about_us/board.php) — non-collecting kunsthalle ~40 min from Montvale.
- **Why it fits:** A non-collecting exhibition museum that borrows heavily from private collections, set in collector-thick northern Westchester/Bedford. Board and staff turn over actively (new trustees and a fresh staff slate announced 2023–24), so a finance/governance trustee is a live need, not a courtesy seat.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Best lower-commitment first rung on the list — the former **Board of Overseers is now the "KMA Advisory Board,"** an upper-tier membership that lends "skills and support" and gets direct access to the Executive Director. Enter there, then convert to trustee. Warm intro through Bedford/Katonah collector or advisor circles.

### 2. Neuberger Museum of Art (Purchase, NY)
[neuberger.org](https://neuberger.org/) / [purchase.edu/neuberger-museum-of-art](https://www.purchase.edu/neuberger-museum-of-art/) — ~40 min from Montvale.
- **Why it fits:** The purest expression of Nariway's thesis on the list: founded in 1969 on Roy R. Neuberger's promised gift, its 900+ core objects are "one of the finest personal art collections in a public institution in the country." An academic museum inside SUNY perpetually balancing state support against private funding — exactly the sustainability question the flagship report studies.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Governance runs through the **Friends of the Neuberger Museum of Art and its Board of Directors** (plus the Purchase College Foundation) — the Friends board is the natural entry rung. Warm intro via Westchester collectors or the college's advancement office.

### 3. The Aldrich Contemporary Art Museum (Ridgefield, CT)
[thealdrich.org](https://thealdrich.org/about) — ~1 hr from Montvale.
- **Why it fits:** A non-collecting contemporary kunsthalle, so it lives on relationships with living artists and private lenders. Board already carries serious collectors (e.g., Eric Diefenbach, chair emeritus), and it publishes an annual report with a financial overview — a governance-literate culture that would value a former-CFO trustee.
- **Give/get:** Not public (financials in the [2024 annual report](https://thealdrich.org/client-uploads/files/Annual-Report-2024_final-7.pdf); board and officer list disclosed there and on the 990).
- **Approach path:** Recruits through the Fairfield/Westchester collector network; warm intro to Chair Diana Bowes or Treasurer Linda M. Dugan. Committee (finance/audit) service is the sensible entry.

### 4. LongHouse Reserve (East Hampton, NY)
[longhouse.org](https://longhouse.org/pages/who-we-are) — Long Island (~2.5–3 hr; board tolerates distance/virtual).
- **Why it fits:** A textbook "founder's private vision into public life" case — Jack Lenor Larsen's 16-acre house, collection, and garden — that has publicly worked through post-founder governance strain (press covered "a LongHouse divided" after Larsen's 2020 death; new board president and new director since). That succession-and-sustainability arc is precisely Nariway's research subject, learned from the inside.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Small design/collector-world board that recruits by invitation and refreshes regularly (four trustees added in one 2020 tranche). Warm intro through the design/craft-collecting or Hamptons art circles; a new director is a natural moment to add finance capacity.

### 5. Grounds For Sculpture (Hamilton, NJ)
[groundsforsculpture.org](https://www.groundsforsculpture.org/about/) — central NJ (~1 hr 15).
- **Why it fits:** Seward Johnson's private sculpture vision turned into a public park, arboretum, and museum. Real institutional scale to steward — roughly a $9M budget and a ~$37M endowment on a ~16-person board — so finance/strategy expertise is materially useful during a growth phase.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Board recruits trustees directly (e.g., Nancy Kieling's appointment was announced through the American Public Gardens Association). Warm intro via the NJ philanthropic/corporate network around president Eric Ryan; finance committee is the entry.

### 6. The Hyde Collection (Glens Falls, NY)
[hydecollection.org](https://www.hydecollection.org/about/) — upstate (~3.5 hr; the one "national/virtual" reach here).
- **Why it fits:** A house museum built on Charlotte Pruyn Hyde's private collection, and the strongest *research dividend* on the list: public record of a real financial-stabilization turnaround plus a live leadership succession (new CEO John Lefner in 2023, new board chair Mark Behan in 2025). An institution that has lived the endowment-and-succession problem from the inside.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Board recruits regionally and announces new officers/trustees publicly; distance makes this a committee/virtual-leaning fit. Warm intro through the Capital Region/Saratoga arts-and-professional network.

### 7. Visual Arts Center of New Jersey (Summit, NJ)
[artcenternj.org](https://www.artcenternj.org/) — closest option (~30 min).
- **Why it fits:** Non-collecting arts-education center that is visibly *building* its board (multiple new trustees elected in nearly every recent cycle), which signals open capacity and appetite for skills. Least on-thesis for private collections, but the best low-commitment, high-proximity first board where a former CFO adds obvious value.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Actively elects new trustees several times a year — the most straightforward direct-recruitment path on the list. Warm intro via the Summit/Union County professional network.

### 8. Nassau County Museum of Art (Roslyn Harbor, NY)
[nassaumuseum.org](https://nassaumuseum.org/support/) — Long Island (~1 hr 15).
- **Why it fits:** The highest *research dividend* and the highest risk: an abrupt 2023 director ouster and a contested 2024 sale of eight paintings drew public criticism over transparency and deaccession, and leadership has said event revenue is essential to stability. A live governance/finance repair situation — instructive to study, but enter with eyes open.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Board is repopulating and governance-stressed; approach via the Long Island collector/advisor network or a warm intro to current leadership. Treat a committee seat as due diligence before full trusteeship.

### 9. Montclair Art Museum (Montclair, NJ)
[montclairartmuseum.org](https://www.montclairartmuseum.org/board-trustees) — NJ (~40 min).
- **Why it fits:** A collecting museum with a demonstrably finance-valuing culture — its current director, Ira Wagner, rose from trustee and *Treasurer* before leading the institution, and the board runs a standing Finance committee. More established than others here, but well-matched to a governance-minded CFO and very close to home.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Trustee recruitment through the Montclair/Essex County professional-collector network; finance-committee service is the credible on-ramp.

### 10. Center for Art Law (Brooklyn, NY)
[itsartlaw.org/boards](https://itsartlaw.org/boards/) — NYC / virtual-friendly.
- **Why it fits:** Not a museum, but the connective tissue of the exact advisor ecosystem Nariway wants inside its network — T&E and art-law attorneys, appraisers, foundation counsel — a small, growing nonprofit at the art/law/collections nexus where finance and governance capacity is genuinely scarce.
- **Give/get:** Not public.
- **Approach path:** Small board recruited by the founder/managing director (Irina Tarsis) and existing directors; virtual-leaning, low-dollar, high-network. Warm intro through the art-law/advisory community.

## Also considered / not a fit (for now)
- **ArtTable** ([arttable.org](https://www.arttable.org/about/)) — a superb *network* node (1,200+ women and nonbinary visual-arts leaders; board includes advisor-ecosystem figures) and worth joining for reach, but it is a membership organization advancing professional leadership, not a governance-hungry board where a CFO's skills are the scarce input. Pursue as a network, not a board target.
- **The Glass House** ([theglasshouse.org](https://theglasshouse.org/about/)) — on-thesis modern house-and-collection site, but it is owned and operated by the National Trust; its "board" is an **Advisory Council** (chaired by Paul Goldberger), so it offers a low-commitment advisory rung rather than a fiduciary seat with real governance leverage.
- **Pollock-Krasner House & Study Center** ([stonybrook.edu/pkhouse](https://www.stonybrook.edu/commcms/pkhouse/index.php)) — perfectly on-thesis (artist's estate into public study center), but it is governed through the Stony Brook Foundation, so there is no readily available independent board seat.
- **Heckscher Museum of Art** (Huntington, NY) and **Hunterdon Art Museum** (Clinton, NJ) — solid small regional museums actively adding trustees; kept as secondary options, thinner on the serious-collector/advisor density that criteria 2 asks for.
