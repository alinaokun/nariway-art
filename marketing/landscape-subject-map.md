> The grounded inventory ChatGPT's exercise called for: every subject Nariway **already has evidence for or knows it must cover** — no hypothetical filler. For each: the question it answers · the evidence type(s) and where they live · what we have vs what's missing · whether it deserves a public page now. This tells us (a) whether the four subject families hold, (b) the first 8–12 Landscape entries, (c) the section name. Built from [[market-intelligence]] (11 ecosystem domains), [[claims-register]] (C1–C36+), the 76-collection corpus, the [[conversations-series|Conversations]], and the [[what-we-now-believe|Evidence Base]].

**Evidence key:** M = macro data · L = law/regulation · P = professional practice · C = collection cases · V = Conversations (voices) · F = original Nariway finding.
**Page-now:** ✓ (rich evidence, build in v1) · ~ (soon; good evidence) · … (later; thin or gap).

## Architecture this confirms
- **Nav = Collections · Landscape · Conversations.** The middle is one body of knowledge, not five products. Section title: **The Private Collection Landscape** — *a reference to the economics, ownership, stewardship, succession, and public life of significant art collections.* ("Landscape" leading the naming, per ChatGPT; it lets the section be the whole environment, not just "the market.")
- **Three levels on the page:** (1) the big picture (a few major stats + Nariway findings), (2) the subjects (the reference body below), (3) the decisions in practice (pathways/cases surfaced contextually — NOT a separate taxonomy). This reconciles Flagship + Market Research + Topics + Decisions + Findings as *five forms of evidence in one body*: the website is the report.
- **Subjects are the organizing principle, not publishing formats** (the Wex lesson). A subject's page looks like whatever its evidence warrants — a comparison table (Partner), sourced figures + roles (Estate planning), charts (Wealth transfer) — the same philosophy as Chinati vs Corcoran inside one Collection grammar.
