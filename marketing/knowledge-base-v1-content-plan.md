## The reframe (what this knowledge base is, and is not)
Nariway's territory is **the long-term stewardship, transition, and future of significant private art collections** — not collecting, not connoisseurship, not the market. This is the "back half of a collection's life" that the audit and inventory kept surfacing as Nariway's real, evidence-backed authority.

**The depth-gate (the rule that keeps it finite).** A subject is included only to the depth that it materially affects **long-term ownership, stewardship, succession, transfer, public access, or disposition.** Provenance, condition, authenticity, insurance, and tax all matter — but only insofar as they change what can happen to the collection. Nariway does not become a general reference on how to authenticate a painting or choose storage. It covers those subjects *through the lens of the collection's future*, and no further. (This is the research-question change ChatGPT named: not "what's missing from a comprehensive reference on private collections," but "what does Nariway need to know to help an owner evaluate the long-term future of a significant collection.")

## Part 1 — The inventory, organized around three reader questions
Provisional structure. Macro market/economic evidence is treated as a **cross-cutting empirical layer**, not forced into one group.

**Cross-cutting empirical layer — the evidence spine ("why now").** Great Wealth Transfer & art (**S**) · collector demographics & the generational/gender shift (**S**) · private-museum landscape & economics (**M**) · the estate rush / market motion (**M**) · art-secured lending market size (**S**) · the receiving end closing (H2). Surfaces inside entries and as a standing "state of the field" backdrop. Tax and Jurisdiction/Cross-border remain cross-cutting **dimensions** surfaced within entries, not their own entries.

**Q1 — What does it take to hold a significant collection?** (the ongoing reality of ownership)
- Strong: the cost of keeping (**S**), valuation (**S**), art finance & lending (**S**), storage (**S**, within cost).
- Moderate: ownership structures & entities (**M**), collection management/conservation practice (**M** cost / **T** practice), handling & logistics (**M**), insurance as its own subject (**M–S**).
- Holes (front-end, near-empty): provenance (**N**), authenticity & attribution (**T**), condition (**N**), cataloguing & documentation (**N/T**), rights (**N**), security & loss (**N**). Included only at the depth they affect transfer/value/acceptance — currently unbuilt.

**Q2 — What happens when ownership changes?** (transition and succession)
- Strong: estate planning for art (**S**), estate & gift tax (**S**, within estate planning).
- Moderate: heirs & family dynamics (**M**), keeping together vs dividing (**M**), donor intent & cy pres (**M**), ownership structures (**M**, canonical here).
- Hole: retention / keep-in-the-family (**T**, n=0 cases) — the *most common actual outcome*, least documented. "Sell wins by default" is a finding that lives across Q2/Q3.

**Q3 — What can a collection become?** (public life and disposition)
- Strong: ownership vs access, the frame (**S**), build an institution (**S**), partner with an institution (**S**), give to an institution (**S**), disperse deliberately (**S**), governance & institutional durability (**S**, canonical here, hinged to Q2).
- Moderate: sell (**M**), public-access models (**T–M**), how museums work (**M**).
- Hole: novel & shared models (**T**).

**Honest imbalance (kept visible).** Q3 is rich, Q1 is strong only on the money/holding-cost side and empty at the object front-end, and Q2 rests almost entirely on estate planning at Strong depth. This is the searchlight, not an oversight: Nariway is strongest at the end of a collection's life. v1 should lead from that strength and show the empty rooms as "in development," not pad them.

- **Reader question:** Why does what-happens-next to collections matter now?
- **Why Nariway:** the "why now" that makes advisers and collectors care; establishes Nariway handles contested macro data responsibly.
- **Research form demonstrated:** responsible macro-data synthesis + a data-integrity finding.
- **Existing evidence:** C1 ($992B art-and-collectibles rotation, framed as inheritance not sale), C8 (spousal transfer, >95% to women), C9 (UHNW holdings), C34 (UHNW population/geography), C35 (generational/gender shift), **C36 (multiple non-comparable totals — the guardrail finding)**.
- **Collection cases:** none directly (macro).
- **Conversations:** Erskine (context).
- **Missing research:** confirm current report vintages; nothing blocking.
- **Governing sources for verification:** Deloitte/ArtTactic Art & Finance Report; Cerulli Associates; UBS Global Wealth / Next Generation Report; Knight Frank Wealth Report; Art Basel/UBS Survey of Global Collecting.
- **Recommended components:** a few headline data points; a short "why the number is not one number" box (C36); a chart of where the transfer lands (US concentration); no case list.
- **Depth:** S. **Research to publication grade:** LOW (verification/refresh only).

### B. The Cost of Keeping a Collection  *(Q1)*
- **Reader question:** What does holding a significant collection actually cost each year?
- **Why Nariway:** distinctive and unowned — no one assembles the full annual carrying cost; it is the quiet pressure behind most transition decisions.
- **Research form demonstrated:** an assembled cost model (scattered benchmarks synthesized into one annual figure).
- **Existing evidence:** C13 (insurance ~1–2%/yr at home), C14 (storage rates), C15 (freeport scale), C16 ("emotional yield" ~2.6%/yr — a return drag, not cash), C18 (SFMOMA 95.2% in storage, ~$4.1M/yr), C29 (collection-manager salaries), C26 (transit is the main insured loss).
- **Collection cases:** SFMOMA (C18); freeport paradox (C15).
- **Conversations:** none direct (candidate future topic for an appraiser/insurer).
- **Missing research:** upgrade C16 to primary (read the FAJ paper); refresh storage/insurance benchmarks; one non-US data point.
- **Governing sources:** Deloitte Art & Finance Report; specialist storage rate cards (Arrow, UOVO/Crozier facility scale); insurer/broker guides (ranges only — note C27, insurance market-size figures are unreliable); Dimson/Pukthuanthong/Vorsatz, *Financial Analysts Journal* (2026); ARCS for staffing.
- **Recommended components:** an itemized annual-cost table (storage · insurance · conservation · management · handling) with sourced ranges; a home-vs-vault swing box; the "emotional yield" caveat stated correctly.
- **Depth:** S. **Research to publication grade:** LOW–MEDIUM.

### C. Valuing an Art Collection  *(Q1)*
- **Reader question:** What is a collection worth, for which purpose, and who decides?
- **Why Nariway:** the counterintuitive, high-utility truth that there is no single value; a fee-event trigger and a common adviser confusion.
- **Research form demonstrated:** a definitional finding + authoritative-source governance (USPAP/IRS).
- **Existing evidence:** C30 (USPAP percentage-fee prohibition), C31 (IRS Art Advisory Panel: 63% of items adjusted, FY2023), **C32 (three distinct correct values — the finding)**, C33 (appraisal market-size unreliable), C52 (blockage discount, ~25–37%+, litigated not fixed).
- **Collection cases:** single-artist concentrations for blockage (Dalí, Chinati; artist foundations generally).
- **Conversations:** Kelly Cahn (art advisor, Sept 10); Mark Winter valuation intel (unpublished).
- **Missing research:** pull the latest IRS Art Advisory Panel Federal Register report directly; verify USPAP language; decide blockage-discount depth.
- **Governing sources:** IRS Publication 561; Form 8283 instructions; USPAP (The Appraisal Foundation / ASB); Appraisers Association of America; the IRS Art Advisory Panel annual Federal Register report; Tax Court / Surrogate's Court opinions (blockage, C52); Sotheby's estimate-methodology page.
- **Recommended components:** the three-values comparison (FMV vs insurance replacement vs auction estimate) as a table; a "who governs each number" column; a short blockage-discount explainer for single-artist holdings.
- **Depth:** S. **Research to publication grade:** MEDIUM.

### D. Estate Planning for Art Collections  *(Q2 anchor)*
- **Reader question:** How does art move through an estate, and what changed in 2026?
- **Why Nariway:** the adviser's home turf and the referral channel; the 2026 provisions are fresh and sharp.
- **Research form demonstrated:** legal/tax framework + practitioner voice + living-law change-tracking.
- **Existing evidence:** C23 (OBBBA $15M/$30M exemption), C24 (0.5% AGI charitable floor), C25 (fractional-gift 10-year clawback), C44 (foundation excise-tax stack §4941/4942), C45 (UK Acceptance in Lieu — cross-border comparator), C46 (art-funded CRT basis limit / Flip-CRUT).
- **Collection cases:** foundation/POF cases (Glenstone, Chinati) for structure; Hilma af Klint (C47) for board-resolved constraint.
- **Conversations:** **Erskine** (POF, structures, "common goal," the alter-ego coordinator); Asher (parked).
- **Missing research:** verify the 2026 provisions (C23/C24/C25) against the actual statute/IRS text, not advisory-firm summaries; decide cross-border depth (AIL vs a genuine international-inheritance treatment — currently a hole).
- **Governing sources:** the OBBBA statute and IRS guidance; IRC §§4941/4942; ACTEC materials; Arts Council England AIL report. Practitioners (Erskine) are the *voice*, not the authority.
- **Recommended components:** the estate/gift framework; a "what changed in 2026" box (C23/C24); the professional-roles block; a "flexible pathway, hidden clawback" set (loan margin call, fractional-gift clawback, CRT basis limit); one cross-border comparator (UK AIL).
- **Depth:** S (a draft exists). **Research to publication grade:** MEDIUM.

### F. Governance and Why Collections Outlast (or Outlive) Their Founders  *(Q3, hinged to Q2)*
- **Reader question:** What makes an institution built from a collection survive its founder?
- **Why Nariway:** Nariway's single strongest *original* finding — the intellectual asset itself, defensible and evidence-tested against disconfirmation.
- **Research form demonstrated:** proprietary cross-case analysis of the coded corpus (a form nothing else in the set uses).
- **Existing evidence:** Pattern 14 (13/13 non-founder-sole cases survived their founder); H6/H8; the disconfirmation passes (Corcoran — good governance, still died on money; Dia — founder-sole crisis survived by forced handoff; Gardner — board-governed by design); C4/C37 (durability ratios, endowment norms); C19/C39 (sector finance).
- **Collection cases:** Terra, Corcoran, Dia, Barnes, McNay, Poldi Pezzoli, Norton Simon, Gardner.
- **Conversations:** **Erskine** ("founder's disease").
- **Missing research:** none blocking the *finding*; verify each marquee case's independent-board status and net-assets-to-opex against Form 990s and governance docs; state the survivorship-bias caveat honestly. The real gate is an **editorial decision** on how much of the proprietary corpus to publish, not a research gap.
- **Governing sources:** Nariway's own coded corpus as primary evidence, verified against IRS Form 990s, each institution's governance documents/site, NBER museum financial history (C37), AAM snapshots (C19/C39).
- **Recommended components:** the governance-vs-endowment finding; a small governance-at-founding table (independent board / locked succession / outcome); the honest scope caveat (prevents vacuum-collapse, not death-by-money; survivorship bias).
- **Depth:** S. **Research to publication grade:** LOW (analysis done; the decision is how much IP to expose).

### G. Ownership Is Not Access  *(Q3 — the framing anchor)*
- **Reader question:** How many ways can privately owned art actually reach the public, and when does ownership deliver access at all?
- **Why Nariway:** the conceptual spine and the animating vision ("more private art in public view"); reframes the whole field away from the museum-or-nothing binary.
- **Research form demonstrated:** a framework + a disconfirmation-tested mechanism.
- **Existing evidence:** [[field-definition]]; the "ownership ≠ access" finding; the mechanism (Vogel blanket distribution → custody without demonstrable sustained view; Souls Grown Deep curated placement → sustained exhibition life); the ~20–30% on-view figure.
- **Collection cases:** Vogel (afterlife), Fisher (loan → high view without ownership), Souls Grown Deep, Broad, Kinsey.
- **Conversations:** none yet directly on this axis (Alexis, Sage, Kate Bryan flagged for later).
- **Missing research:** verify Souls Grown Deep's exhibition record; state Vogel as an *open question* per the language discipline ("no 15-year follow-up audit identified in our research," never "nobody checked").
- **Governing sources:** Nariway's own field-definition + the Vogel afterlife investigation and Souls Grown Deep placement record, verified against museum exhibition histories; AAM-derived "% on view" figures.
- **Recommended components:** the spectrum-of-sharing framework (own↔access); the curated-vs-blanket mechanism box; 3–4 illustrative cases; the honest Vogel open-question.
- **Depth:** S (frame) with one open empirical question. **Research to publication grade:** LOW–MEDIUM.
