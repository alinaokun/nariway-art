# The Private Collection Landscape — domain-completeness audit (outside-in, 2026-08-23)

> Built **outside-in**: starting from everything a person or family responsible for a significant private art collection could reasonably need to understand across the full life of that collection — deliberately NOT starting from Nariway's master subject map, its pathway taxonomy, or what it happens to have researched. Only after the domain is mapped is it reconciled against the vault. This is a domain-completeness exercise, not a website taxonomy or a page list. No pages designed, no v1 chosen.

## Method + the four-way distinction
The instruction's distinction, applied throughout:
- **[Domain]** — a whole area a responsible owner must *understand*.
- **[Subject]** — a knowledge topic within a domain.
- **[Decision]** — an option/choice the collector can make (NOT a knowledge domain; Nariway's Build/Partner/Give/Disperse/Sell/Keep pathways are decisions, not subjects).
- **[Service]** — a professional service that *executes* something (appraisal, conservation, legal drafting) — distinct from the knowledge subject about it.

**Nariway evidence:** Rich / Partial / Thin / None. **Types:** M macro data · L law/regulation · P professional practice · C collection cases · V Conversations · A original Nariway analysis. **Pos?** = does the gap matter to Nariway's positioning. **Home** = own public treatment (own) vs dimension/subsection of another (sub).

## RECONCILIATION — the five required findings

### 1. Important subjects MISSING from the current master map
The current map is strong from valuation forward. It is **missing most of the front and physical middle of a collection's life:**
- **The entire "Understanding what you have" domain (Domain 1)** — provenance (None), authenticity & attribution (Thin), condition (None), cataloguing & inventory (None), documentation/archive (Thin), significance-assessment as a subject (Partial). This is the single biggest hole, and it is where a responsible owner *begins*.
- **Rights in the work** — copyright/reproduction, resale right (droit de suite), moral rights (None).
- **Cross-border / jurisdiction & international tax and inheritance regimes** (Partial, no dedicated treatment) — critical for significant collectors, who are frequently multi-jurisdictional.
- **Cultural-property / export controls / AML / CITES compliance** (None).
- **Security & loss** (theft, Art Loss Register) (None).
- **The market & liquidity mechanics** — how art actually sells, fees, price discovery (Partial; only the estate-rush macro angle, not the how-it-works).
- **Public-access mechanics & standards** (Thin) — the current map tracks *deciding* to go public far better than *how public life actually works*.

### 2. Current "subjects" that are really DECISIONS, SERVICES, or SUBTOPICS
- **Build / Partner / Give / Disperse / Sell / Keep are DECISIONS**, not knowledge subjects. They belong inside Domain 7 (The futures) as options, framed by the ownership-≠-access spectrum — never as the domain itself. (The user's caution, confirmed.)
- **"The professional ecosystem / who does what"** mixes a Subject (how to assemble & coordinate a team; the H7A gap = original analysis) with a list of **Services** (appraiser, conservator, attorney…). Keep the *knowledge* subject; the services are executional.
- **Appraisal** is a Service; the Subject is *valuation standards & purposes*.
- **Estate & gift tax** and **Charitable-giving mechanics** are Subtopics of Estate planning / Tax, not standalone domains.
- **Collection management, logistics/handling** straddle Subject and Service.

### 3. Subjects currently in the WRONG family
- **"Economics of private museums"** sits in the current map's *Wealth & economics*; it is really **Institutional (Domain 8) / a Build-decision dimension**.
- **"Governance & surviving the founder"** is filed under Succession; it genuinely spans **Succession *and* Institutions/Build** (institutional durability). It is a cross-cutting subject, not a Succession-only one.
- **"Art finance & lending"** is under *Ownership & care*; it is really **Money (Domain 4)** — a liquidity/market subject.
- The current four families (Wealth / Ownership / Succession / Public life) are **defensible but partly evidence-shaped** — see finding 4.

### 4. Where the taxonomy is DISTORTED by what Nariway happens to have researched
This is the most important structural finding. The current map mirrors **Nariway's searchlight, not the territory:**
- **Overweighted:** the subjects the WebSearch-based ecosystem agent could find (macro data, cost-of-keeping, tax, finance, valuation, institutional economics) and the subjects the *case corpus* covers (the public-life pathways). These are Rich because they were *researchable that way*.
- **Underweighted / invisible:** the **connoisseurship & object front-end** (provenance, authenticity, attribution, condition, cataloguing) — hard to surface via WebSearch, absent from the disposition-focused case corpus, so it reads as "not part of the field" when it is in fact where the field *starts*.
- **The pathways are overweighted as if they were the domain** — because Nariway's proprietary asset is the case corpus, the destinations loom larger than the lifecycle. Outside-in, they are one domain of nine.
- **"Keep it in the family" is underweighted (Thin, n=0)** despite being the *most common real outcome* — pure evidence bias (the corpus selects for public, documented collections).
- Net: Nariway currently looks authoritative on **the end of a collection's life** (succession, disposition, economics) and thin on **the beginning and physical middle** (what you own, how you care for it). A responsible collector needs the beginning first.

### 5. Major research gaps that would block a credible authority claim on "private art collections"
To own the whole domain (not just "what becomes of collections"), the priority gaps, roughly in order:
1. **Provenance** — history, gaps, restitution/Nazi-era, antiquities/patrimony, due-diligence practice. (None today; table-stakes for credibility.)
2. **Authenticity & attribution** — authentication mechanisms, the collapse of artist authentication boards, forgery, scientific analysis. (Thin.)
3. **Cataloguing, documentation & condition** — the records and care-state layer. (None/Thin.)
4. **Rights** — copyright/reproduction, resale right, moral rights. (None.)
5. **Cross-border / international law, tax & inheritance regimes** — for multi-jurisdictional collectors. (Partial.)
6. **The market & liquidity mechanics** — how art actually sells and prices. (Partial.)
7. **Public-access standards & mechanics** — how public life actually functions, beyond the decision to enter it. (Thin.)
8. **Retention** — the most common outcome, least documented. (Thin.)

**Bottom line:** the current map is not wrong, but it is *lopsided toward the disposition end and toward what was easy to research*. The credibility of "the reference on private art collections" depends on closing the object/knowledge front-end (Domain 1) above all — and on treating the pathways as decisions within Domain 7, not as the domain.

## What this exercise does NOT do (per instruction)
No website redesign, no v1 page selection, no final public taxonomy. This is the domain map + gap analysis only. Next natural step (separate exercise): decide which of these become public treatments, which are sub-dimensions, and — importantly — set the **research agenda** to close the front-end gaps, so the eventual public architecture reflects the domain rather than the searchlight.
