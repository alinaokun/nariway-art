The Landscape

The Great Wealth Transfer and Art

Over the coming decade a large share of the world's privately held art will pass to new hands. Most of it will move by inheritance rather than sale, which is why the question of what becomes of a collection is now reaching so many families at the same time.

$992B

An estimated $992 billion in art and collectibles could change ownership over the coming decade, much of it through inheritance rather than sale.

What it measures

The value of art and collectibles expected to change ownership globally as wealth passes between generations.

What it is not

A forecast of art coming to market. Most of it stays with heirs rather than being sold.

Derived from Deloitte's model. Ultra-high-net-worth individuals hold roughly 64% of a projected $31 trillion transfer, about $19.8 trillion, of which close to 5% is art and collectibles.

The size of the transfer

There is no single wealth-transfer number

Three institutions publish very large estimates. They are all legitimate, and they measure different things.

These estimates differ in geography, population, and time horizon. They cannot be added together or treated as one figure. Nariway cites the art-specific $992 billion from Deloitte, and keeps the total-transfer numbers separate.

What is held

Art and collectibles held within UHNW wealth

The stock of art and collectibles held by ultra-high-net-worth individuals is projected to keep growing through the decade.

Where the wealth sits, and who holds it next

The picture behind the number

Private wealth is concentrated in the United States. Knight Frank counts about 713,600 individuals worth $30 million or more, roughly 35% of them in the United States. UBS, counting the broader population worth $1 million or more, puts it near 57.5 million people, with more than 40% in the United States. The two figures measure different groups, so they belong side by side rather than added together.

The next generation may collect differently. In the Art Basel and UBS survey of global collecting, about 74% of respondents were Millennials or Gen Z, and younger collectors were active well beyond fine art, from design to collectibles. Whether those same collectors will want the fine-art collections now approaching a transfer is a genuine open question.

Who inherits first

In the United States, an estimated $54 trillion is expected to pass first between spouses, and more than 95% of it to women. The first person to decide a collection's future is often a surviving wife, not a younger heir.

The decision is not whether to sell

Most inherited art is kept. About 72% of inheritors retain at least some of what they receive, and around 80% of collectors intend to pass their collections to family. The real question is what happens when control of a collection changes hands.
