## The one sentence
An independent study of what could become of a specific collection, produced **before** the owner commits to any single route, so the whole decision is legible before specialists start executing pieces of it.

## Why this engagement, and why now
The [[business-model-ledger]] carries three hypotheses; this is #2, the one the CFO calls "the cleanest thing to eventually price-test," because it is bounded, priced, and has an external comparable (museum conceptual and master-planning work sells at $30–100K+). Every other revenue idea depends on proving someone will pay Nariway for a defined outcome first. This is that outcome. The ledger's standing warning is that the knowledge base keeps absorbing all the effort while nothing is priced in front of anyone. This document is the fix. It does not require more research to exist. It requires the material Nariway already has, pointed at one collection.

## The problem it solves (the H7A thesis, stated as a reason to exist)
When a collector reaches the question of what happens to a significant collection, every professional in the room owns a route or a task. The estate attorney implements the plan. The appraiser values. The auction house sells. The museum receives. The art advisor buys and sells works. **No one owns the decision about the routes, before a route is chosen.** That pre-decision is where a collection's future is actually determined, and it is where the owner is most alone. The Collection Futures Study exists to own that moment. It makes the whole problem legible before the owner starts solving pieces of it.

## Who it is for
- A collector or family with a significant, coherent collection who has begun asking "what happens to all of this," and has not committed to a route.
- The family-office principal or trust-and-estate attorney who watches a client stall at exactly that pre-decision moment, and needs someone independent to own it before the specialists are chosen.
- The tier is the open question discovery must answer (see [[problem-discovery]] heterogeneity reframe): above the casual-owner line, at or below the line where the collection already has a dedicated curator and a settled plan. The band where the pre-decision is genuinely unowned is the market.

## Buyer-backward: who actually hires Nariway (hypotheses, to be tested)
ChatGPT's correct challenge — design the engagement from the buyer backward before deciding its shape. These answers are drawn from the case corpus, the Erskine Conversation, and [[market-intelligence]]; they are hypotheses discovery must confirm, not settled fact.

- **Who signs.** Not usually the collector at the height of collecting. Erskine's "founder's disease" is the pattern — the person who built it is the least able to confront its end. The buyer is more often **whoever inherits responsibility for the collection's future without the authority or knowledge to resolve it**: the surviving spouse, an adult heir who has become de facto custodian, or the family-office executive or attorney holding the problem. This reframes the ICP away from "the collector" toward "the responsible party under pressure."
- **What triggers the call.** Almost never a calm strategic impulse. A death, a spouse's death, a health scare, a downsizing or move, a liquidity need, an unsolicited museum inquiry, an heir declining the collection, a tax deadline, or simply aging into the question. The call is **reactive and time-pressured**, which argues against a leisurely "study" framing and for "help me get my arms around this before I make an irreversible move."
- **What they have already tried.** They asked their existing advisors, and each answered from a single lane — the attorney proposed a trust, the auction house offered to sell, the museum asked for a gift of the best works. They received route-specific answers to a route-*selection* question. They are stuck not from a lack of options but from the absence of a neutral synthesis. This is the H7A gap as the buyer actually feels it.
- **What is at stake.** Money (avoidable tax, a forced sale at bad prices), family peace (heirs in disagreement, lasting resentment), legacy (a life's work dispersed thoughtlessly), and time (decisions made under duress after a death). The emotional stake is as large as the financial one.
- **What a successful outcome looks like.** Not a particular route. Clarity and confidence that the chosen route is right, that the family is aligned, and that no catastrophe is being walked into blind. In the buyer's words, "I understand my options and I am making this decision on purpose."
- **What earns their trust.** Independence above all — the corcoran and gift-refusal cases show why an advisor with a stake is not believed, so Nariway's no-commission rule is the central trust signal. Then demonstrated command of real precedent (the Collection Index is the proof), the finance-and-strategy credential (which reads as fiduciary seriousness to this buyer), and a referral from someone they already trust. **Trust, not price, is the risk.**
- **What Nariway knows that their advisors do not.** What has actually happened to comparable collections and why some outcomes lasted while others failed (the governance and founder-durability finding), and the cross-route tradeoffs no single specialist sees. Nariway is the only party in the room who has studied the *decision itself* rather than one of its executions.
- **The deliverable worth paying for, seen from here.** For a reactive, time-pressured, trust-gated, high-stakes buyer, the valuable thing is a clear independent map of this collection's realistic futures, with the tradeoffs and a sequence to decide, credible enough to align a family and direct the specialists. It is the study — the buyer-backward view simply sharpens *why* it is worth paying for. It is the only document in the room written by someone with no stake in the answer.

## What the client provides (inputs)
- Access to the facts of the collection — what is in it, how it is held, and the state of its documentation and condition insofar as those bear on the options.
- The owner's intentions, hopes, and constraints for the collection's future.
- The family and governance picture — heirs, their interest or disinterest, existing structures.
- The existing professional advisors already in the picture.
Nariway signs a mutual NDA before any of this changes hands. Nariway does not need appraisal-grade data to begin. It needs enough to map the options honestly.

## What Nariway does (the process, roughly 8–12 weeks)
**Phase 1 — Orientation (2–3 weeks).** Understand what the owner wants the collection to accomplish, the collection's character and constraints, the family and governance situation, and who is already advising. Produce a short scoping memo. This phase is also sold on its own (see the front door below).

**Phase 2 — Landscape and precedent (2–3 weeks).** Apply the Collection Index precedents and the Landscape knowledge to *this* collection. What have comparable collections actually done, what happened to them, and what did it cost. The economics of continued ownership. The valuation reality for this material. The tax and estate frame, and where a specialist must be brought in. Institutional appetite for a gift or partnership of this kind.

**Phase 3 — Options and tradeoffs (2–3 weeks).** Lay out the realistic routes for this specific collection — retain in the family, give to an institution, partner with one, lend, found an institution, disperse deliberately, sell, or a combination — with the tradeoffs of each, the questions still unanswered, and the specialists each route would require.

**Phase 4 — The study and the working session (1–2 weeks).** Deliver the written study and walk the owner and their advisors through it in a working session, ending with a recommended sequence for reaching a decision and a clear handoff to the specialists who execute it.

## What the client receives (the deliverable)
A written **Collection Futures Study** covering:
- what the owner wants the collection to accomplish;
- the collection's characteristics and constraints;
- family, heir, and governance considerations;
- current ownership and how the collection is held;
- the economics of continued ownership;
- the tax and estate issues flagged for specialist analysis;
- relevant precedents from the Collection Index;
- the realistic options, with tradeoffs;
- the unanswered questions;
- the specialists who need to be involved, and when;
- a coordinated process for getting to a decision.
Plus the working session that walks the owner and their advisors through it.

## What it is not (the boundaries that make it trustworthy)
Nariway is not the lawyer, the appraiser, the curator, the tax advisor, or the broker, and does not execute the chosen route. It makes the whole problem legible and coordinates the path to a decision, then hands to the specialists who carry it out. **Nariway takes no commission on any sale, gift, or placement.** The fee is the only thing Nariway earns from the engagement. That independence is what lets the study be believed — Nariway is the one party at the table with no stake in which route is chosen.

## Price and entry (revised v0.2 — H7B unproven, do not quote until discovery)
The buyer-backward view resolves the tier question. For a trust-gated buyer, a cheap paid front door is a mistake — it reads junior, and it risks becoming a paid discovery meeting. The real barrier to the first buy is trust, not price, so the entry step should *build trust*, not lower the check.

- **Entry — a free scoping conversation.** Short, unpaid, and its own reward to Nariway. Its job is to demonstrate command of the problem and independence, and to establish whether a full study is warranted. This is the trust-builder, and it replaces the paid Orientation tier.
- **The engagement — a single Collection Futures Study.** The full four-phase work and working session, priced to signal seriousness. Ledger anchor: $25–50K (advisory $20–75K, bounded legacy study $20–40K; museum conceptual and master-planning comparables $30–100K+). One tier, not three.
- **Reference engagement — the first one or two only.** Offered at a reduced rate in exchange for permission to build an anonymized case, because Nariway's asset is precedent and its own first engagements become precedent worth more than the discount.

Whether a bounded lower-scope study belongs *below* the full engagement stays open, but it is answered by a real buyer's reaction, not invented in advance to de-risk a first sale.

## How a buyer reaches this (the research-led path, not cold outreach)
The acquisition model is research-led, because it fits both the market and the founder — Nariway earns the conversation with useful research rather than requesting time from strangers. The path is: **research → attention → professional intermediary → trust → introduction → collector.** The collector facing this decision is already surrounded by estate attorneys, family-office executives, wealth advisors, appraisers, insurers, museum development staff, and art advisors. Reaching 30–50 of the right ones matters more than any large general audience.

The one channel testable now, that requires no interviewing and fits the founder, is **professional distribution — give value, ask nothing**: "I thought this might be useful in your work with collectors. We documented how [N] significant collections handled succession and stewardship. Here is the research." That is unsolicited, but it offers value rather than asking for it, which is a categorically different experience from "can I pick your brain for 30 minutes." The study is what an intermediary eventually forwards or references when a client is stuck at the pre-decision.

**The discipline that keeps this honest (the ledger's standing warning).** The research-led model is correct and is also the more comfortable bet, because it looks like the building the vault already does. It counts as validation only if it produces a near-term falsifiable signal. So:
1. **Do not sell.** When the study reaches a practitioner or a prospect, the test is "does this solve a problem you have actually seen," not "will you buy it."
2. **Watch the [[problem-discovery|signal ladder]]** for the pull, "I have a family you should talk to," not "sounds good."
3. **Price only (H7B) when a real prospect reaches for a real situation**, entering through the free scoping conversation.

Premature selling contaminates the evidence. This document exists to be shown and forwarded, not published as a sales page.
