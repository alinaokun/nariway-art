# Nariway CFO — Inaugural Brief (2026-08, rev.)

*Decision-oriented. One page. From the [[nariway-cfo]] to the CEO. Rule in force: no manufactured precision — directional evidence over invented metrics.*

## What we've invested
Heavily, and well, in **research and infrastructure** — a disciplined research program, a ~55-case universe, a strong McNay packet, an Editorial Structure Study, a versioned operating layer. **The current work product appears heavily weighted toward research and systems; commercial validation has not yet occurred.** *(Directional read from the vault/git trail — where effort seems to have gone, not measured time.)*

## What it bought — real assets, not sunk cost
A proprietary method, the seed of a dataset, a proven editorial engine, an ops layer. Not wasted.

## The finding
**No commercial validation has happened, and H7B (will anyone pay) is untested for all three active models** in the [[business-model-ledger]]. Desk research *can* improve the offer, sharpen who to approach, and set credible pricing anchors — it *cannot* prove willingness to pay. That requires getting out of the building. Your founding brief said to run market conversations early; we built the research operation first — understandable, since it's the enjoyable, fully controllable part.

## The recommendation — parallel, and discovery not pitching
Not "stop everything before case #2." **Continue the research, and in parallel begin ~5 problem-discovery conversations** — estate/private-client attorneys, multi-family-office execs, fine-art insurers. **Do not pitch. Do not price-test yet.** Learn how the decision is actually handled today and where the pain is:
- When a collector starts thinking about a collection's future, who is involved first?
- What decisions are hardest? Where does the process break down?
- What outcomes do collectors actually consider?
- What's handled well today, and what isn't?
- Who has authority to hire an outside advisor — and when would one be useful?
- What do they pay for adjacent work?

These move **H7A** (is there a recognized, unowned problem) from the demand side — complementing the case-level Decision-Owner field. Only *after* a recurring problem surfaces do you test a bounded offer + price — that's **H7B**, and that's a founder-level call.

## Why this shape (dissolving a false binary)
My first instinct framed this as "compound research *vs.* buy one H7B data point." Too either/or. The real move is **keep building the research AND start listening to the market** — without turning you into a salesperson or forcing a service model before the research has earned one.

## The principle (unchanged — the reason the CFO exists)
The CFO is not here to forecast the future. **It is here to stop Nariway from confusing activity with economic progress.** The most valuable next unit of founder attention is a handful of honest conversations — run alongside, not instead of, the work you love.
