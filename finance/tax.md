# Tax — the standing tax watch (function)

A work-function (no persona) that monitors the tax dimension of everything Nariway does, flags issues early, frames the questions worth asking, and says clearly when a licensed CPA or tax advisor is needed. It works alongside the [[nariway-cfo|CFO]] and reports into the daily check-in and the [[executive-model|reaction loop]].

**The boundary (first rule, non-negotiable).** This function is **not a CPA and does not give tax advice.** It spots issues, explains why they matter, and tells Alina when to bring in a real tax professional. Nothing here substitutes for licensed tax counsel; the most valuable output is often "keep records for this, and ask your CPA about that."

## Entity context (confirmed 2026-08)
Nariway and Artobiography operate under **Pulsar Innovation LLC, a single-member New Jersey LLC (2021)**, and all Alina's ventures are expected to sit under it. A single-member LLC is by default a **disregarded entity**: business income and expenses flow to her personal return (Schedule C) and net profit is subject to **self-employment tax**. She has a **CPA who handles tax filing but is not involved in the business**, so business-side tax planning is currently unowned.

## Standing watch areas
- **Structure and elections.** As advisory revenue grows, whether an S-corp election or another structure reduces self-employment tax is a real question for a CPA, not to decide here, but to flag when revenue arrives.
- **Deductibility and records (relevant now).** The professional-development spending we have been weighing, courses, subscriptions, museum and fair travel, the vault tools, is likely deductible as ordinary and necessary business expense, **but only with contemporaneous records.** Standing advice: keep receipts and a short business-purpose note for each. Do not overclaim; let the CPA rule.
- **Estimated taxes and SE tax.** Once paid work begins, quarterly estimates and self-employment tax apply. Flag before the first invoice.
- **State and local (NJ).** New Jersey income tax on the pass-through; whether NJ imposes sales/use tax on consulting or advisory services (most professional services are exempt, but verify) and on any productized data product later.
- **The advisory boundary, again.** Nariway does not give clients tax advice, it coordinates licensed tax and estate professionals. Keep the line clean.

## Domain tax knowledge (for the advisory and the report, distinct from Alina's own taxes)
The collector world Nariway serves is shaped by tax, charitable-deduction rules for art gifts, estate and gift tax on collections, the related-use rule, fractional gifts, private-foundation excise rules. This is **subject-matter the report and the advisory must understand** (and cite to authoritative sources), separate from Pulsar Innovation LLC's own tax. Tracked here so it is not confused with the firm's own filings.

## Live flags from these facts (all "ask the CPA", not advice)
- **Loop the CPA into the business, or add a planning-minded advisor.** The current CPA does compliance, not planning, so the deductions, structure, and estimated-tax questions below have no owner. As revenue approaches, either bring her in on the business or add a business-tax advisor.
- **Self-employment tax and the S-corp question.** As a disregarded single-member LLC, net profit carries SE tax. Once the advisory is meaningfully profitable, an **S-corp election** can reduce SE tax, a classic question for the CPA to run the numbers on. Not now, but flag it the moment real revenue arrives.
- **Estimated quarterly taxes** begin once business income starts; the CPA should set these up so there is no surprise.
- **Deductibility and records (act now).** The professional-development spending, courses, subscriptions, museum and fair travel, tools, is likely deductible, but only with **contemporaneous records**. Keep every receipt and a one-line business purpose; hand them to the CPA at tax time.
- **Everything under one LLC** means all venture income flows through the single return; keep clean per-venture books so the CPA can see what is what.
